## Introduction to Bhāratavarsha and Its Unity

Bhāratavarsha, often simply referred to as India, is an ancient land steeped in rich cultural heritage, spiritual significance, and historical continuity. The name "India" itself has evolved from the river Sindhu, later modified by Persians to Hindu and by Greeks to Indos. This vast territory stretches over 2,000 miles from North to South and more than 1,900 miles from East to West, encompassing a diverse array of natural boundaries such as seas and mountains (ref15). Despite its internal diversity—comprising different parts, tribes, and races—it has been traditionally viewed as a coherent political and geographical unity by its people. Historically, India was regarded as a unified fatherland and even as a "mother country" and "heaven on earth," owing to its rich natural resources, cultural heritage, spiritual significance, and heroic history (ref15). Pilgrimage traditions have played a key role in unifying its diverse regions spiritually and nationally.

## Bhāratavarsha: A Geographical Unity

Geographically, India is presented as a unified entity divided into nine distinct regions, providing comprehensive details about its landscape and inhabitants. This concept of geographical unity can be traced back to classical Indian texts such as the Puranas, which depict India as a single political domain governed by one overarching authority (ref15). The notion of "Ekarāt" (one-sovereign) is deeply rooted in Vedic literature and royal coronations, reflecting an ancient belief that the entire subcontinent was seen as a unified sovereign entity. This historical continuity underscores the idea of Bhāratavarsha as not only a geographical but also a political unity.

## Pilgrimage Traditions and National Unity

Pilgrimage traditions have been instrumental in fostering a sense of national unity across India’s diverse regions. Sacred and geographical elements such as seven mountains, seven principal sacred places, and numerous holy sites foster a shared identity among its people (ref15). These pilgrimage centers act as focal points for religious devotion and cultural exchange, bringing together individuals from different parts of the country to participate in common rituals and practices. The significance of these traditions can be seen in ancient texts like the Arthaśāstra and the Edicts of Asoka, which document widespread awareness of India’s territorial integrity.

## Ancient Understanding of Bhāratavarsha

Ancient Indians had a precise understanding of their geographical boundaries and political unity. This is evident from Greek accounts during Alexander's time, indicating that even foreign observers recognized India as a cohesive entity (ref15). The Puranas further elaborate on this by providing comprehensive details about the landscape and inhabitants of Bhāratavarsha, divided into nine distinct regions. This detailed understanding reflects an ancient consciousness of territorial integrity and national unity.

## Historical Reach and "Greater India"

The historical reach of Bhāratavarsha extended beyond its geographical boundaries through colonization, giving rise to the concept of “Greater India.” This broader notion encompasses regions influenced by Indian culture, religion, and political systems. The idea of Greater India highlights how the cultural and spiritual influence of Bhāratavarsha has spread far and wide, creating a shared heritage across diverse cultures (ref15). Despite these external expansions, the core unity of India remains intact, serving as a central point for global Indian diasporas.

## Nature and Nurture in Bhāratavarsha

Nature and nurture have played crucial roles in shaping the identity of Bhāratavarsha. The rich natural resources, including fertile lands, rivers, forests, and mineral wealth, have sustained its population over millennia (ref15). Additionally, the nurturing influence of cultural traditions, religious beliefs, and social practices has fostered a cohesive national identity. This combination of nature and nurture has enabled India to maintain its unique character while also promoting universal peace and calling for global unity against regression.

## Bhāratavarsha in Modern Context

In contemporary times, Bhāratavarsha continues to be recognized as a unified nation with significant historical and cultural continuity. Despite internal diversity, the shared heritage of natural resources, spiritual traditions, and political aspirations has enabled India to maintain its identity while also engaging globally (ref15). The concept of "Ekarāt" remains relevant in modern Indian governance and national consciousness, reflecting an enduring belief in the unity and sovereignty of Bhāratavarsha.

The cultural influence of Bhāratavarsha has extended beyond its geographical boundaries, contributing to global unity.
