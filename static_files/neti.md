Neti is one of the six cleansing processes known as Saṭkarmas in Haṭhayoga practices. These processes aim to purify and strengthen the body for spiritual practice (sādhanā) [ref1]. Neti specifically involves inserting a soft cotton thread through the nostrils into the mouth, facilitating the cleaning of nasal passages [ref1][ref3]. This procedure must be performed under expert supervision due to its intricate nature. The term 'Neti' also has broader implications in Indian heritage, appearing in various contexts from ancient texts to modern interpretations of cultural values. In this article, we will explore Neti's origins, methods, significance, and its place within the larger framework of Indian culture and spirituality.

## Origins and Philosophical Foundations

The practice of Neti is deeply rooted in traditional Hindu yoga texts such as the Gheranda Samhitā [ref1]. These ancient scriptures outline specific cleansing techniques designed to purify the body's internal systems. The concept behind these practices is that a clean body facilitates a clearer mind, essential for spiritual enlightenment [ref1][ref3]. Neti serves not just as a physical cleanse but also as a symbolic act of purification and discipline on one’s path towards higher consciousness.

The origins of yoga and its cleansing techniques like Neti trace back to Vedic practices where rituals were seen as pathways to divinity. The Gheranda Samhitā, in particular, provides detailed instructions on the Saṭkarmas, placing Neti third in the list [ref1]. This systematic approach highlights the importance placed on bodily purification before engaging in more intense spiritual practices.

Moreover, Neti is one of several physical disciplines aimed at preparing practitioners for deeper meditation and ultimately achieving samādhi (a state of profound absorption or unity with the divine) [ref8][ref9]. In this context, yoga’s cleansing processes are not merely exercises but transformative tools that help align the body with spiritual goals.

## Methodology and Practice

The method of performing Neti involves inserting a thin thread of soft cotton about 6 inches long into one nostril and guiding it through to emerge from the mouth [ref1][ref3]. This process requires considerable skill and caution, necessitating expert guidance. The procedure aims to clean the nasal passages thoroughly, removing impurities that could obstruct clear breathing and mental clarity.

The insertion of the thread must be done gently to avoid injury or discomfort. Practitioners are advised to ensure the cotton is soft enough not to irritate sensitive nasal tissues [ref1][ref3]. Careful preparation includes sterilizing the thread to prevent infection and ensuring it is long enough to pass through both nostrils comfortably.

Neti is typically performed daily as part of a yoga regimen, especially in practices aimed at spiritual purification. It complements other Saṭkarmas such as dhauti (stomach cleansing) and basti (colon cleansing), collectively forming a comprehensive approach to body purification [ref1][ref3]. This holistic method underscores the interconnectedness of physical health with mental and spiritual well-being.

Each school contributed to refining techniques like Neti for spiritual purposes.

In ancient texts such as the Purāņas and the Manusmriti, there are references to cleansing rituals that may have influenced or predated modern yoga practices. These texts often emphasize the importance of bodily purification in achieving higher states of consciousness [ref1][ref3]. The practice of Neti thus aligns with a long-standing tradition where physical cleanliness is equated with spiritual purity.

The significance of such practices extends beyond individual practitioners to broader cultural narratives. In Indian culture, yoga and its associated cleansing techniques are seen as pathways not just for personal enlightenment but also for maintaining societal order and harmony [ref1][ref3]. This reflects the holistic approach prevalent in traditional Indian philosophy where individual and collective well-being are interconnected.

## Philosophical Implications

The practice of Neti extends beyond physical purification to encompass broader philosophical implications within Indian thought. Yoga philosophy views the body and mind as interconnected entities where bodily cleanliness reflects mental purity [ref1][ref3]. This holistic approach is rooted in the idea that spiritual enlightenment requires a balance between physical health, mental clarity, and ethical conduct.

In this context, Neti symbolizes the removal of impurities not just from the nostrils but also metaphorically from one’s thoughts and actions. It serves as a ritual act of purification aimed at aligning the self with divine principles [ref1][ref3]. This spiritual dimension underscores how practices like Neti are seen as transformative tools for achieving higher states of consciousness.

Moreover, yoga philosophy emphasizes the importance of discipline (yama) and ethical conduct (niyama), which include practices such as Neti. These disciplines serve to prepare the practitioner for more advanced spiritual pursuits by fostering self-control and moral integrity [ref1][ref3]. The philosophical underpinnings of Neti thus reinforce its role in shaping a holistic approach to spirituality.
