## Varna

Varna is a multifaceted term within Indic knowledge traditions, encompassing various meanings and contexts.

### Social Divisions
In ancient Bhāratīya society, varṇa refers to the social divisions that have existed for thousands of years [3][4]. These divisions are based on occupation and duties and include four primary classes: Brāhmaṇas, Kṣatriyas, Vaiśyas, and Śūdras. Over time, these divisions became rigid and hereditary, leading to the creation of numerous castes and subcastes, including the Niṣāda caste [7]. Each varṇa has specific duties (svadharma) that are bound by their social standing and include specific bathing rituals such as Brāhmaṇas householders bathing twice daily [6][5].

### Religious and Philosophical Context
In Indic texts like the Bhagavata Purana, varṇa is described as a system of four social classes each with religious duties aimed at contributing to liberation from Saṃsāra [20]. The concept also extends to philosophical teachings where individuals can transcend their social divisions after realizing the Self, as detailed in texts such as the Avadhuta Upaniṣad and Devīgītā [11][12].

### Varna System and Dharmic Practices
The varṇa system includes opportunities for elevation through adherence to dharma, impacting prescribed duties and practices in conjunction with āśrama and ācāra. Additionally, the leaders of the Baṅgāla School of vaiṣṇava dharma disregarded the varṇa system in favor of cultivating primary moral and social values [10].

### Linguistic Context
Varna also refers to phonemic units or letters within Sanskrit grammar, a term used both in masculine and neuter gender as noted in ancient texts like the M.Bh. Ahnika 1 and Siva Sutras 3,4 [5][18]. In some contexts, it denotes the total number of letters in the Sanskrit alphabet (63 or 64 when considering different intonations such as udātta, anudātta, svarita) [9].

### Religious and Spiritual Figures
In various purāṇas and texts:
- Varna is mentioned as a god worshipped on the southern side of building site layouts in Chapter 247 of the Agni Purana [23].
- It is also described as the God of Death, determining souls' fates after death based on their deeds, according to Chapter 369 of the same text [24].

In the Skanda Purana, Varna plays a crucial role in societal structure and religious practices. According to Book 6 - Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Section 1 - Tīrtha-māhātmya, Chapter 218 describes that Śrāddha rituals should adhere to traditions specific to one's caste [26]. Further, in Chapter 237, Varna is personified by Viṣṇu, emphasizing his embodiment of societal order and central role in maintaining the caste-based social hierarchy [27].

### Geographical Context
There are several villages named Varna across India:
- A village in Kaprada Tehsil, Valsad District, Gujarat (pin code 396126), located approximately 43 kilometers south of Valsad and surrounded by Dadra Nagar Haveli, Pardi, Vapi, and Dharampur Talukas [28].
- Another village in Jintur Tehsil, Parbhani District, Maharashtra (pin code 431508), situated around 45 kilometers north of Parbhani and bordered by Sailu, Sengaon, Mantha, and Aundha Nagnath Talukas [28].
- Two more villages named Varna in Dholka Tehsil, Ahmedabad District, Gujarat (pin code 382265) characterized by their proximity to rivers such as Sabarmati and Dhaman Par, and offering various educational institutions including schools and colleges [28].
