## Etymology

The term **varna** is derived from Sanskrit and has multiple meanings across various Indic knowledge traditions. It can refer to color, social divisions, phonemic units (letters), and even specific deities.

## Shruti and Smriti

### Varṇa in Social Divisions

In the context of ancient Bhāratīya society, varna denotes the fourfold division into distinct social classes based on occupation and duties [1][2][3]. These divisions became rigid and hereditary over time, leading to the creation of various castes and subcastes through intermixing, including the Niṣāda caste [4]. Each varṇa has specific duties (svadharma) one is bound to perform according to their social standing. For instance, brāhmaṇa householders are expected to bathe twice daily as part of their religious obligations [5][3].

### Varna in Religious Texts

According to the Bhagavata Purana, varna refers to the four social classes in Hindu society, each with specific religious duties that contribute to the pursuit of liberation from Saṃsāra [6][7]. The concept of varṇasaṅkara denotes the mixing or intermarriage between different varnas. In some contexts, leaders of the Baṅgāla School of vaiṣṇava dharma disregarded the varna system in favor of cultivating primary moral and social values [8].

## Purana

### Agni Purana

In the **Agni Purana**, Varna has multiple roles and representations. Chapter 247 mentions it as one of the gods worshipped on the southern side of the building site layout [9]. Additionally, in Chapter 369, Varna is described as the God of Death, responsible for determining the fate of souls after death based on their deeds [10].

### Skanda Purana

In the **Skanda Purana**, varna refers to the caste system and plays a crucial role in both societal structure and religious practices. According to Book 6 - Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Section 1 - Tīrtha-māhātmya, Chapter 218, Śrāddha rituals should adhere to the traditions specific to one's caste [11]. In Chapter 237, Varṇa is personified by Viṣṇu, emphasizing his embodiment of societal order and his central role in maintaining the caste-based social hierarchy [12].

## Dharma

### Transcending Varna

Individuals within the varna system can transcend their social divisions after realizing the Self, as described in texts like the Avadhuta Upaniṣad and Devīgītā [13][14]. Opportunities for elevation within the varṇa system exist through adherence to dharma, impacting prescribed duties and practices in conjunction with āśrama and ācāra [15][16].

### Universal Dharmas

Sāmanya dharmas apply universally beyond these divisions, meaning that certain moral and social values are expected of all individuals regardless of their varna [17][18].

## Kavya

### Varṇa in Grammar

In Sanskrit grammar, varna refers to a phonemic unit or letter. The term was used both in the masculine and neuter gender in ancient times; for example, "upadiṣṭā ime varṇāḥ" from Mahābhāṣya [19].

## Location Summary

Varna is also the name of several villages across India:
- A village in Kaprada Tehsil, Valsad District, Gujarat, with a pin code of 396126.
- Another Varna exists in Jintur Tehsil, Parbhani District, Maharashtra, with a pin code of 431508.
- Two more Varanas are located in Dholka Tehsil, Ahmedabad District, Gujarat, sharing the same pin code of 382265.

These villages are characterized by their proximity to rivers such as Sabarmati and Dhaman Par, and they offer various educational institutions including schools and colleges [20].
