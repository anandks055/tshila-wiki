## Narakasura: The Demon King of Prāgjyotiṣapura

### Birth and Origin

Narakasura, also known as Bhaumasura, is said to have been born from a drop of sweat that fell onto the Earth (Bhudevī) when Varāha, an incarnation of Viṣṇu in his boar form, touched and raised the Earth. Hence, he is considered the son of the earth.[1][2] According to another account, Narakasura was born from a drop of sweat that fell from Varāha’s body onto Bhudevī.[3]

### Rise to Power

Narakasura became an invincible demon after receiving the Vaiṣṇavāstra weapon from Viṣṇu due to the prayer of Bhudevī. With this formidable weapon, Narakasura became a terror to the world and caused significant havoc by looting items from heaven, including Indra's royal umbrella and Aditi’s earrings.[1][3] He was also known for abducting thousands of women.[4]

### Defeat by Krishna

Narakasura’s reign of terror ended when he faced Śrī Kṛṣṇa. At the request of Indra, Kṛṣṇa invaded Prāgjyotiṣapura, Narakasura's capital, and defeated him along with his chief assistant Murāsura.[1] After defeating Narakasura, Kṛṣṇa restored all the valuable items that were looted to their rightful owners.[1][3]

### Legacy and Festival

The defeat of Narakasura marks the origin story of Narakacaturdaśī, which is observed on the first day of Diwali. On this day, his death is celebrated.[2] Additionally, Kṛṣṇa married the sixteen thousand women he had liberated from Narakasura’s captivity.[4]

### Post-Defeat Consequences

After Narakasura's death, his friend Dvivida caused widespread destruction in retaliation.[3] At Bhudevī’s request, Kṛṣṇa protected and crowned Bhagadatta, the son of Narakasura, as the new king.[1]

### Additional Notes

Narakasura is known to have fought with Sanaiścara during a Devāsura war and later contested Indra's position. He was eventually killed by Kṛṣṇa, who married the women from his harem after liberating them.[5] Narakasura’s kingdom was lost due to his pride of power.[5]
