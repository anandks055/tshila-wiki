## Dhritarashtra

Dhṛtarāṣṭra, meaning "One by whom the nation is supported," was a blind king of Hastināpura who played a pivotal role in the epic Mahabharata. He was born blind to Ambikā through Kŗşņa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa and was the elder brother of Pāṇḍu [1, 2]. Dhṛtarāṣṭra fathered the Kauravas, with Duryodhana being the most prominent among them. Despite his partiality towards his sons and indecisive leadership, he attempted to maintain balance between the warring factions by giving half of the kingdom to the Pāṇḍavas upon their return from Indraprastha [3]. 

However, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's decisions were heavily influenced by Śakuni’s advice, which often led to disastrous consequences. He ignored the wise counsel of his advisors like Vidura and Maitreya who advised him to make peace with the Pāṇḍavas [4, 5]. Despite this, Dhṛtarāṣṭra sought higher philosophical truths from Sanatsujāta and received war commentary from Sañjaya, who urged him not to mistreat the Pāṇḍavas [6].

In addition to his political roles, Dhṛtarāṣṭra is multifaceted. In the Garuda Purana, he is described as a divine serpent worshipped during specific lunar phases and as the eldest son of Vyasa with a hundred sons, including Duryodhana [7]. The Harivamsha Purana portrays him in various roles, such as a mighty serpent who becomes part of the Naga clan and as the father of Duryodhana leading the Kaurava army against Mathura [8].

Dhṛtarāṣṭra's character is also highlighted in Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra where he is portrayed with both positive and negative attributes. His envy towards the Pāṇḍavas ultimately led to his downfall, serving as a cautionary tale about the destructive power of jealousy [9]. After the Mahabharata war, Dhṛtarāṣṭra left the palace for the forest where he merged with the Supreme Soul.

Dhṛtarāṣṭra perished in a forest fire along with his wife Gāndhārī, daughter of King Subala. His leadership and actions significantly influenced the course of events leading to the Mahabharata war, highlighting both the strengths and weaknesses that define his complex character [10].
