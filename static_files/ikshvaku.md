## Ikshvaku

Ikshvaku is one of the ten sons (nine according to Brāhmanda Purana) of Vaivasvata Manu [1][24]. He was born from his father's sneezing and became the founder of the solar dynasty, also known as Suryavaṃśa or Ikṣvākuvamśa. This lineage is associated with significant figures such as Ambariṣa, Daśaratha, Hariścandra, Saudāsa, Vasiṣṭha, Mithi, Daśāśva, Śrī Rāma, and Triśañku [1][3].

Ikshvaku had a hundred sons and a daughter named Suvarṇā. Among his sons, Nimi started the Videha dynasty at Mithilā, which includes Janaka-Śīradhvaja, Sītā's father. Śrī Rāma belongs to this lineage [2][3].

Ikshvaku is also noted for taking his share of family property and leaving none for his brother Nabhaga [25]. He was known for his worship of Śiva and had a hundred generous sons, contributing to the dynasty's practice of sacred rites and virtuous conduct [27][28][29].

In connection with an Aṣṭaka ritual, Ikshvaku’s son Vikukṣi was asked to secure some flesh. After partaking a portion of hare’s flesh, he presented the remainder, but it was rejected by the Purohita who claimed that it had already been tasted. As punishment for this act, Vikukṣi was banished and later became a yogin, attaining salvation [1].

Ikshvaku is also mentioned as a brāhmaṇa who led a gluttonous life but was eventually saved by the grace of sage Jābāli [9]. Additionally, his lineage includes worship rights of the deity at Śriraṅgam [10][30].

The Ikṣvāku family is also linked to Dharmanatha and considered the common ancestral family of the Milakas and Asmakas as mentioned in the Vayu Purana. They are described as a group connected with the Asmakas in the Bhagavatapurana [20][21][22].

In the Shiva Purana, Ikshvaku is one of nine sons of Vaivasvata Manu and the founder of the solar dynasty [24]. He is also noted for taking his share of family property, leaving none for his brother Nabhaga, and for being the ancestor of virtuous kings like Mitrasaha. His lineage practiced sacred rites and virtuous conduct [25][26].

In medical contexts, Ikshvaku (as a substance) serves various purposes in treatments such as emetics and medicinal ingredients for treating alcohol-induced ailments [31][32]. It is also used in preparing Anaha-Varti for Udara treatment and identified as a plant whose seeds produce oils with emetic properties [33][34].

In the Garuda Purana, Ikshvaku is described as one of the sons of Manu and an ancestor in the solar dynasty. He is noted as the ancestor of Vrihadvala and Raghu [36][37][38].

The Harivamsha Purana details that Ikshvaku was the progenitor of the Ikshvaku lineage, including kings such as Sagara, for whom Narayana grants eternal glory. He is also mentioned in relation to Madhu’s wife and the dynasty from which Haryashva and Yadu's family originates [40][41][42][43][44][45].
