## Mathura

Mathura is a historically significant city located on the Yamunā River in Uttar Pradesh, India. It is renowned for its religious sanctity among both Brāhmans and Buddhists and holds particular importance as the birthplace of Kṛṣṇa. The city's prominence is evident from ancient texts such as the Brahma Purana, where Mathura is described as a crucial location where Kaṃsa plotted to lure Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma into a fatal wrestling match after Keśin's death [1]. This plot culminated in the defeat of Kaṃsa by Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma [2].

Mathura, also known as Muttra in modern times, is situated on the right bank of the Yamunā River near Govardhana hill in Vṛndā-vana, a site significant for Kṛṣṇa's exploits. According to the Viṣṇu Purāṇa XII.1, Mathura was founded by Śatrughna and continues to be an important pilgrimage destination due to its sacred association with Kṛṣṇa [5].

The city of Mathura is part of the larger Mathura district in Uttar Pradesh, which had a population of 2,541,894 as per the 2011 census. The district covers an area of 3,340 square kilometers and has a sex ratio of 858 females per 1,000 males [6]. Within this district, Mathura itself is notable for its rich cultural heritage, including famous temples such as the Chitragupt Mandir and Jain Mandir. Educational institutions like Prasad Public School and Rajiv International School also contribute to the town's significance.

Several other locations named Mathura exist across India, each with unique characteristics. For example, Mathura in Kalahandi, Odisha, is known for its temples dedicated to Radha Krishna and Shiv Mandir, while another Mathura in Junagarh, Orissa, is recognized for its proximity to the Jeypore Airport [6]. These various locations add to the diverse cultural tapestry of India.
