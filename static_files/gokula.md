## Overview of Gokula

Gokula, literally meaning "the abode of cows," is a village associated with the childhood of Krishna and Balarāma in Hindu mythology. It was a fertile and prosperous community inhabited by cowherds on the banks of the Yamunā river [2][3]. The chieftain Nanda lived there with his wife Yaśodā, who raised Krishna after he was transferred from Mathurā for safety reasons [3].

### Historical and Mythological Significance

In Hindu mythology, Gokula is particularly significant as it marks the place where many pivotal events in the life of Krishna occurred. These include his upbringing amidst threats from demons sent by Kansa, who were ultimately defeated by him [2]. The village was also the site of several legendary episodes such as the lifting of Govardhana mountain to protect the villagers from a rainstorm caused by Indra’s wrath [7][8].

The area is associated with various deities and events. For instance, Rohiṇī resided there according to the Vishnu Purana, and it was also where Garga secretly performed initiatory rites for Rāma and Kṛṣṇa [1]. Additionally, Gokula is mentioned in the Harivamsha Purana as a joyful place transformed by Krishna's presence [7].

### Modern Locations

In modern times, there are several places named Gokula across India. One of these is located approximately 8 kilometers from Mathurā in Uttar Pradesh and serves as the headquarters for the Vallabha sect of Vaiṣṇavas [2]. It features significant temples such as the Gokulnāth Temple [3].

Another contemporary location, named Gokula, is found in Bounsi Tehsil, Banka District, Bihar. This village is known for its temples like Durga Maa Mandir and Shiva Temple, and is situated near rivers like Sakri Chir [9]. There are other villages named Gokula in various regions of India such as Jamtara District, Jharkhand, which has the Hanuman Mandir Kasitanr temple [9].
