## Etymology

Śakti (शक्ति) literally means ‘Divine Power as Goddess’ [1][2]. It refers to the divine feminine energy or power that is central to numerous Indic knowledge traditions and texts.

### Conceptual Foundations

The universe, according to modern science, is a bundle of packed and unpacked energy. This discovery has demolished the distinction between matter and energy, suggesting a basic energy behind all forms [1]. Religious philosophy, particularly based on Vedānta and Tantras [3], postulates that this same energy also manifests as mind and life at different levels of vibration.

The source and sustenance of creation at various levels — whether it be material, mental, or life-related — is Śakti. Brahman of the Vedānta and Śakti (or Devī) of the Tantras are identical [2]. When static, it is called Brahman; when evolving into creation, sustaining it, and withdrawing it back into itself, it is Śakti.

### Narratives

In religious mythological literature, this energy is personified as a female deity, Devī, often depicted as the consort of male deities. Each member of the Trinity has his Śakti or Devī as his consort:
- Sarasvatī of Brahmā
- Lakṣmī of Viṣṇu
- Pārvatī of Śiva

Mother-worship is deeply rooted in Vedic religion, with early origins found in texts like Aditi (the mother of gods), Ambhrnīsūkta, and Rātrisūkta of the Ṛgveda [1].

## Puranas and Itihasas

### Shiva Purana

Śakti is multifaceted within the Śiva Purana. She represents the divine feminine energy associated with Śiva:
- In Section 1 - Vidyeśvara-saṃhitā, Chapter 16, Śakti is represented by Bindu as the power aspect of creation [4].
- In Section 2.1 - Rudra-saṃhitā (1): Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa, Chapter 6, she is described as Prakṛti, Māyā, Guṇavatī, and Parā [5].
- In Section 2.3 - Rudra-saṃhitā (3): Pārvatī-khaṇḍa, Śakti is mentioned as one of the learned sages performing rites for Śiva's marriage at Kailāsa [6].

Śakti also symbolizes power and creativity in various forms:
- In Section 2.1 - Rudra-saṃhitā (1): Sṛṣṭi-khaṇḍa, Chapter 10, she is the entity into which Rudra merges when Sadāśiva exhales [7].
- In Section 3 - Śatarudra-saṃhitā, Chapter 17, she bestows desired fruits and pleasure on devotees as the female energy associated with each of Śiva's incarnations [8].

### Garuda Purana

In the Garuda Purana:
- Sakti is one of the deities in the middle pericarp of the lotus of the heart, symbolizing energy and power [9].
- In Chapter XXXI, she is mentioned as one of the objects saluted during the worship of Vishnu [10].
- In Chapter XXXII, Sakti is identified as an attribute of Vishnu, saluted in adoration rituals [11].

### Lalitopakhyana (Lalita Mahatmya)

In the Lalitopakhyana:
- Śakti encompasses multiple aspects of divine power and manifestation.
- In Chapter 5, she is worshipped for achieving liberation and spiritual growth [12].
- In Chapter 18, she symbolizes a javelin representing sovereignty and authority over divine forces [13].
- In Chapters 29 and 41, Śakti is described as a divine weapon wielded by various deities in battles against Bhaṇḍa and his followers [14][15].

## Kavya

### Literary Representations

Śakti is often represented as the consort of male deities like Indra (Indrāṇī) and Subrahmaṇya, who holds a Śakti weapon [16]. In various contexts, she symbolizes the active force in creation, sustenance, and destruction [17][18].

## Dharma

### Philosophical Interpretations

Śakti embodies the creative, sustaining, and dissolving forces of the universe. She is synonymous with the Divine Mother and central to numerous religious traditions and texts [19]. In addition to being a major goddess in Indic knowledge traditions, Śakti encompasses specific weapons or symbols like the spear held by Durgā [16].

## Artha

### Practical Applications and Worship

Śakti worship is deeply rooted in tantric practices and rituals such as Śrīcakra ritualistic worship [20]. Her concept encompasses a wide range of meanings, from spiritual practices to powerful energy pervading the universe.

## Geography

### Locations Associated with Śakti

In historical geography:
- Sakti refers to the chief town of a feudatory state in Chattisagada division of the Central Provinces [21].
- In modern times, there are several places named Shakti across India. For example, one Shakti is located in Jharbandh Tehsil, Bargarh District, Odisha, and another is situated in Zemithang-dudunghar Tehsil, Tawang District, Arunachal Pradesh [22].
