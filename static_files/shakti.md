## Shakti

Śakti (literally meaning 'Divine Power as Goddess') is a concept central to Indic knowledge traditions, representing the divine feminine energy that underpins creation, sustenance, and dissolution [1]. Modern science has discovered that the universe consists of various forms of energy and matter, but it remains uncertain about their interrelationships. Religious philosophy based on Vedānta and Tantras posits a unified basic energy that manifests as matter, mind, or life at different levels of vibration [2][3].

### Conceptualization

According to the Vedānta tradition, Brahman is the source and sustainer of all creation, which in its dynamic form is called Śakti. When static, it is referred to as Brahman; when evolving into creation, sustaining it, and withdrawing back into itself, it becomes Śakti [2]. This duality is inseparable: one in two, two in one. In religious mythological literature and Tantras, this energy is depicted as a female deity, Devī, who serves as the consort of male deities such as:

- Sarasvatī with Brahmā
- Lakṣmī with Viṣṇu 
- Pārvatī with Śiva

The worship of mother-goddesses like Aditi and Ambhrnīsūkta in Ṛgveda indicates the roots of mother-worship [1].

### Divine Manifestations

Śakti, also known as Devī, is personified in various forms such as Durgā, Kāli, and Pārvatī. She represents the creative, sustaining, and destructive forces of the universe and is central to numerous texts like tantra and Śrīcakra ritualistic worship [4][5]. Śakti embodies the active, dynamic force in the universe, symbolizing the power behind creation, sustenance, and destruction [6].

### Weapons and Symbols

Śakti can also refer to specific weapons or symbols such as:

- The spear held by Durgā
- The Śakti weapon of Subrahmaṇya [8][9]

In some contexts, it denotes the female counterparts or spouses of deities like Indrāṇī to Indra [7].

### Shiva Purana Insights

The Shiva Purana elaborates on various aspects of Śakti:

- **Bindu**: Representing creation in conjunction with Śiva [17].
- **Prakṛti, Māyā, Guṇavatī, and Parā**: Described as the generating cause of all by Īśvara [18].
- **Union of Rudra and Sadāśiva**: When Rudra merges into Śakti during inhalation, symbolizing divine union [19].
- **Creation of Pārvatī**: Created by Brahmā to serve as Śiva's consort and daughter to Dakṣa [20][44].
- **Mother of the Universe**: Described as Satī, bestowing good fortune [21].

Śakti is also invoked in various rituals:

- One of the learned sages performing rites for Śiva’s marriage at Kailāsa [22].
- Divine feminine energy associated with Śiva, representing power and creativity [23][24][36][37].
- Female form complementing Śiva [25].
- Part of Vidyātattva or Śivatattva, symbolizing divine energy [34].

### Other Textual References

The Garuda Purana mentions Śakti in various contexts:

- One of the deities in the middle pericarp of the lotus of the heart, symbolizing power and energy [56].
- Object saluted during worship of Vishnu and one of Vishnu's attributes [57][58].

The Lalitopakhyana describes Śakti as:

- Supreme power worshipped for spiritual growth [60].
- Great goddess supporting everything through Brahmā’s meditation and assisting in rites [61].
- Collective force of divine feminine warriors preparing for battle against Bhaṇḍa Daitya [62][63][65].
- Divine weapon wielded by various deities [64].
- Part of the Pañcatrika Mahā-Vidyā, expressed by Śiva and other deities [66].

### Geographical References

In a geographical context:

- A village in Jharbandh Tehsil, Bargarh District, Odisha.
- Another Shakti is located near Tawang, Arunachal Pradesh.
- There are additional localities named Shakti in Dhurki Block, Garhwa District, Jharkhand; Isagarh Tehsil, Ashoknagar District, Madhya Pradesh; and Jodhpur, Rajasthan [68].
