## Introduction

Vṛndāban (also spelled Vṛndāvan, Bṛndāvan, or Bṛndāban) is a significant pilgrimage site in Uttar Pradesh, India. It is located approximately 10 kilometers from the city of Mathurā on the bank of the Yamunā river and is closely associated with Lord Kṛṣṇa's childhood days [1].

## Name Origin

The name Vṛndāban is derived from Vṛndā, the daughter of King Kedāra. Vṛndā practiced severe austerities here to get Lord Viṣṇu as her husband and was later reborn as Rādhā, the daughter of Vṛṣabhānu and Kalāvatī [1].

## Temples and Sacred Sites

Vṛndāban is dotted with numerous temples and ghāṭs (river banks provided with steps for bathing) that are connected to Kṛṣṇa’s life. Some notable temples include:
- Kṛṣṇagopala Mandir
- Baṇkebihāri Mandir
- Shāh Bihārījī Mandir
- Rādhā-ramaṇ Mandir
- Srīraṅgajī Mandir of Rañganātha, built in the South Indian style
- Gopāleśvaranātha Mandir of Śiva [1].

## Associated Locations

### Gokula and Govardhana Hill

Gokula, where Kṛṣṇa spent his childhood days, is located on the opposite bank of the river Yamunā. The main temple in Gokula is the Gokulnāth temple.

Govardhana hill, about 34 kilometers from Mathurā, is a small hillock spread over approximately 6 to 7 kilometers. It has an ancient temple dedicated to Govardhananātha. About 5 kilometers away from this hill lies Rādhākuṇḍa, surrounded by two large tanks known as Rādhākuṇḍa and Kṛṣṇakuṇḍa [1].

## Historical Significance

Vṛndāban is renowned for its association with various important events in the life of Lord Kṛṣṇa. It was here that he suggested to the villagers to worship Govardhana instead of Indra, performed Rāsalīlā with the gopīs, and where Uddhava informed the gopīs about his welfare [15].

The town is also notable for being the place where Jīva Gosvāmi spent his entire life and Sanātan Gosvāmin spent the latter part of his life. It is referenced as the home of the cowherd boys, who serve as archetypal figures of sākhyabhakti (friendship devotion), and also the place where Nimbārka migrated and spent the later years of his life [23].

## Festivals

Vṛndāban is particularly known for celebrating Holi, initiated by Rājā Indradyumna, which involves swinging a Kṛṣṇa idol. Additionally, the Dolpurṇimā festival is celebrated here [18, 17].

## Literature and Puranas

The Harivamsha describes Vrindavana as a charming forest on the bank of the Yamunā, abounding in grass, fruits, and kadamba trees where Kṛṣṇa sported with the milkmaids. The Bhagavatapurana also mentions it [1].

## Other Locations Named Vrindavan

Another Vrindavan is located in Kasargod city in Kerala, known for its temples such as Pady Sri Mahavishnu Temple and educational institutions including Anganvadi-Baladukka Chengala. Another village named Vrindavan exists in Mirzapur district, Uttar Pradesh, situated near the rivers Belan and Kalkaliya [9].
