## Overview of Skanda Purana

The Skanda Purana is considered one of the most extensive and detailed religious texts in Hindu literature. It provides a comprehensive account of various aspects of Hindu mythology, including stories about deities, rituals, and sacred places. The text is divided into seven major sections or Khandas, each containing numerous chapters that delve into different themes [ref1]. Each Khanda focuses on specific regions and their associated myths and histories. For instance, the Māheśvara-khaṇḍa narrates tales about Lord Shiva and his devotees, while the Vaiṣṇava-khaṇḍa contains stories related to Lord Vishnu.

One of the significant features of the Skanda Purana is its geographical descriptions. It mentions various places that are considered holy or have spiritual significance in Hindu tradition. For example, it refers to Padmāvati as another name for Ujjayini [ref9], Pokhar as a holy place [ref10], and Kusasthali as a holy place [ref14]. These descriptions not only add to the rich tapestry of Indian mythology but also serve as guideposts for pilgrims who visit these sites. The text also provides detailed accounts of rituals and practices associated with various tirthas (sacred places), emphasizing their spiritual benefits.

The Skanda Purana is notable for its narrative style, which often blends mythological stories with historical events, creating a complex interweaving of past and present. It serves as a repository of ancient knowledge, providing insights into the cultural and religious practices that have shaped Hinduism over centuries. For instance, it describes the construction of places from gold [ref1], which reflects the grandeur of ancient architectural achievements.

This reference highlights the importance of these places not only from a religious perspective but also from an archaeological and historical standpoint.

The Valuvahini is described as a tributary of the Yamunā [ref5], indicating the interconnectedness of various water bodies within the Indian subcontinent and their importance in Hindu mythology.
