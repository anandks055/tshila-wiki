## Jayadratha: The King of Sindhu in Hindu Epics

### Overview
Jayadratha was the king of the Sindhu region, a significant figure in several ancient Indian epics including the Mahabharata and various Puranas. He is often portrayed as an ally of the Kauravas during the Kurukshetra War and is known for his prowess in warfare and strategic leadership.

### Rule Over Sindhu
Jayadratha ruled over the Sindhu region, a territory that was central to many military campaigns and alliances described in Hindu epics [1]. His reign is prominently mentioned in texts such as the Mahabharata and Harivamsha Purana.

### Military Campaigns and Alliances
In the Harivamsha Purana, Jayadratha is noted for his involvement in Jarasandha’s military campaigns. He was part of Jarasandha's alliance and instructed to besiege Mount Gomanta [2].

### Abduction Attempts and Confrontations with Pandavas
Jayadratha attempted to abduct Draupadi during the exile of the Pandavas, leading to a confrontation where he was defeated by Bhima and other Pandava brothers [3]. This incident is detailed in various chapters of the Mahabharata.

### Battle at Kurukshetra
During the Kurukshetra War, Jayadratha allied himself with the Kauravas. He commanded formidable forces and engaged in fierce battles against the Pandavas. His actions were significant enough to cause internal strife within the Kurus [4]. Notably, he used a boon to prevent Abhimanyu from receiving support during a critical battle, contributing to the latter's death [5].

### Death and Consequences
Jayadratha was ultimately killed by Arjuna in retaliation for his role in Abhimanyu’s death. His death marked significant consequences for the Kauravas, leading to inquiries about subsequent actions of the Kaurava army during the night battle at Kurukshetra [6].

### Genealogical References
In the Vishnu Purana and Bhagavata Purana, Jayadratha is described in genealogical contexts. According to these texts, he was a son of Vrihanmanas or Bṛhanmanas and fathered several sons, including Vijaya and Dhritavrata [7]. Additionally, he is noted as one of the powerful warriors slain by Arjuna, an achievement that contributed to Arjuna's sense of pride [8].

### Literary Mentions
Jayadratha appears in various texts like the Vāmakeśvaratantravivaraṇa and Alaṃkāravimarśiṇī, written by scholars such as Subhaṭadatta and Śiva. These works further contribute to his character depiction in Hindu literature [9].
