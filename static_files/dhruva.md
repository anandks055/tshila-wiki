## Etymology

Dhruva literally means 'the steady one.' The term dhruva is used to describe something fixed and stationary, often contrasted with moving entities [1].

## Shruti and Smriti

### Dhruva: Child Devotee and King

Prahlāda and Dhruva are celebrated as exemplary child devotees in Indic knowledge traditions. Dhruva was the elder son of King Uttānapāda from his first wife Sunīti [2]. Despite being neglected by his father due to favoritism towards his second wife, Suruci, Dhruva sought solace and guidance from his mother. Sunīti advised him to pray to Lord Viṣṇu through rigorous austerities. When he embarked on this path, the sage Nārada met Dhruva and instructed him in meditation [2].

After enduring severe penance, Dhruva was blessed by Lord Viṣṇu with a kingdom and a long life both here and in the heavenly regions after death [3]. His position in the sky is marked by the pole star known as Dhruvanakṣatra, which he occupies after his earthly reign [4].

### Dhruva's Military Campaign

Once, Uttama, Dhruva’s younger brother, was killed by yakṣas during a hunting expedition. Enraged by this incident, Dhruva attacked and killed the yakṣas in large numbers. Kubera was forced to fight with Dhruva until Svāyambhuva Manu intervened and reconciled them [5].

## Purana

### Shiva Purana

In the *Shiva Purana*, Dhruva is a multifaceted figure, appearing as the Pole Star honored during Śiva and Pārvatī's wedding ceremonies. He is positioned a hundred thousand Yojanas above the constellation of the seven sages and serves as the prop for the circle of luminous bodies [6]. Additionally, Dhruva is described as a king who worships Śiva, a son of Uttānapāda and Sunīti renowned for his penance lasting three thousand divine years. He was rewarded with a stable position by Lord Brahmā [7]. Dhruva is also one of the Vasus whose son Kāla influences the worlds, and he is noted as the son of Puṣyanāman and father of Agnivarṇa [8].

### Garuda Purana

According to the *Garuda Purana*, Dhruva is known for his spiritual achievements and royal lineage. He attained a high station through devotion to Janardhana, and later became the king of the insular continent of Plaksha. Dhruva also attained the highest region of bliss and Vishnu through his devout worship [9][10].

### Harivamsha Purana

In the *Harivamsha Purana*, Dhruva is multifaceted, appearing as a son of Uttanapada and Sunrita known for ascetic practices and granted a permanent place in the heavens by Brahma. He is also one of the Vasus who gives birth to Divine Kala and part of Surabhi’s lineage [11][12][13]. Dhruva is mentioned as a sacrificial vase made of Indian fig-leaf wood crafted by Vishnu, and as a celestial position where a sacrifice in the form of a deer finds refuge [14][15].

## Itihasa

### Mahabharata

In the *Mahabharata*, Dhruva appears in various contexts. He is one of the Vasus with a son named Kala, the destroyer of worlds. Additionally, he is depicted as a royal sage in Yama's assembly house and a warrior fighting for the Pandavas [16][17][18]. Dhruva is also recognized as a form of Krishna symbolizing stability and unchanging essence, and identified with the pass through which the river Ganga flows. The inability to see the star Dhruva is an omen indicating one has only one year left to live [19][20]. Furthermore, Dhruva appears among the seven regenerate Rishis including Visvamitra and as one of the great Rishis who visited Bhishma on his deathbed. He is invoked in Mantras for constancy and blessings [21][22].

## Dharma

Dhruva’s intense devotion and ascetic practices are highlighted in *Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra*. As an incarnation of the Lord known as Dhruva-vara-pradātā, he is renowned for his unwavering devotion and receiving divine boons from the Lord [23]. His name symbolizes permanence and eternal glory in spiritual achievement [24].
