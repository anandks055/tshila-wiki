## Yamunā: Sacred River of India

Yamunā, also known as Kālindī, is one of the most sacred rivers in Hinduism, second only to Gaṅgā. Originating from the Himālaya range near Mount Kamet and flowing through northern India, it eventually joins the Ganges at Prayāga (modern-day Allahabad) [1][2]. The river's name literally means "that which rests in the Gaṅgā river after merging" [3].

### Historical and Mythological Significance

Yamunā is mentioned in ancient texts such as the Rgveda, Atharvaveda, and Aitareya Brahmana. According to these sources, Yamunā was an important location during historical events like the battles between the Tṛtsus and Sudāsa [4]. The river has been closely associated with various kings and sages throughout history, including Bharata, Ambarīṣa, Śantanu, Agastya, and others who performed significant sacrifices on its banks [5][6].

### Mythological Connections

In Hindu mythology, Yamunā is often linked to spiritual purity and sanctity. She is considered a devatīrtha (divine river) and is closely connected with the boyhood days of Lord Kṛṣṇa. The Rāsalīlā (dancing with the gopīs) took place on its banks, and according to legend, Yamunā split into two parts to allow Vasudeva to carry baby Kṛṣṇa safely across [7][8]. In the Shiva Purana, Yamunā is described as one of the three rivers forming the Triveṇī (along with Gaṅgā and Sarasvatī), symbolizing spiritual purity [9].

### Geographical Importance

Yamunā forms significant geographical boundaries in northern India. It defines the region of Antarvedi alongside the Ganges and flows through various important locations such as Mathurā, Vṛndāvana, Indraprastha, and Gokula [10][11]. The river is rich in natural resources, including grasses, fruits, and Kadamba trees [22].

### Religious Practices

Yamunā holds immense religious significance. Devotees perform various rituals such as ablutions under specific zodiac conditions to gain spiritual benefits [12]. It is also a site of important pilgrimage events like the Māghamelā bathing festival and Kumbhamelā, which attract millions of devotees annually [5][6].

### Tributaries and Confluence

The river has several tributaries including Carmaṇvati (modern Chambal), Vetravati (modern Betwa), Ken, Pahuj, and others. It joins the Ganges at Prayāga to form the Triveṇī confluence [25][16].

### Cultural Representation

Yamunā is a prominent feature in various cultural narratives. For instance, she appears in stories involving Aṁbarīṣa's dvādaśī vow and Bīrbal’s demonstration of divine love for devotees [7]. Yamunā is also recognized as the sister of Yama and deified as a goddess in Purāṇic literature [8][9].
