## Jarāsandha: Overview

Jarāsandha was a powerful monarch of Magadha whose capital was Girivraja (also known as Rajagaha or Rajagriha). He is renowned for his unique birth story and his fierce enmity with Kṛṣṇa. His name literally means 'one whose body was joined by Jarā,' referring to the ogress who combined two halves of a baby into a complete child.[1][2]

## Birth and Early Life

Jarāsandha’s origins are unique: he was born as two halves of a baby, each half birthed by one of King Bṛhadratha's wives. These halves were found and joined together by an ogress named Jarā, giving him his name 'Jarāsandha.'[1]

## Reign and Military Campaigns

As the monarch of Magadha, Jarāsandha was a formidable ruler who repeatedly invaded Mathurā, the capital of Kṛṣṇa.[2][3] He led vast armies against the Yādavas and Kṛṣṇa, engaging in prolonged battles with divine weapons. Despite his military prowess and strategic alliances, he suffered eighteen defeats at the hands of Kṛṣṇa and Bhīma.[4][5]

Jarāsandha was also known for imprisoning numerous kings to sacrifice them to his deity, Bhairava (an aspect of Śiva). When Kṛṣṇa learned about this practice, he arranged for Jarāsandha's death in a duel with Bhīma. Following his defeat and death, his son Sahadeva succeeded him on the throne.[1][3]

## Religious Significance

Jarāsandha was a staunch worshipper of Bhairava, an aspect of Śiva. His repeated attempts to conquer Mathurā and eliminate Kṛṣṇa were driven by personal vendettas involving his daughters' husbands.[4][5] He is described as a powerful king who dominated the middle region and posed a significant threat to the performance of the Rajasuya sacrifice.[6]

## Portrayal in Epics

In various texts, Jarāsandha is portrayed as an invincible king who was eventually defeated by Kṛṣṇa and Bhīma. He was also known for possessing a celestial chariot until it was reclaimed by Bhīma.[1][7] Despite his power and influence, he ultimately met his end at the hands of Bhīma in a prolonged wrestling match, causing widespread fear among the citizens of Magadha.[8]

## Legacy

Jarāsandha’s legacy includes being one of the foremost adversaries of Kṛṣṇa and the Yādavas. His defeat by Kṛṣṇa and Bhīma is seen as necessary to eliminate a tyrant who was a burden on the earth, thus alleviating the world's suffering.[5][9]
