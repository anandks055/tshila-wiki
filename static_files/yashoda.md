## Yashoda: A Significant Figure in Hindu Literature

Yashoda is a prominent figure in Hindu literature, particularly known for her role as the mother of Krishna and her deep devotion to him. According to the Pañcopacāra Pūjā, Yashoda’s breasts are metaphorically offered to the Lord, highlighting the profound affection and love she holds for the deity [1]. In the context of food offerings (Naivedya), Yashoda is identified as Krishna's mother, emphasizing a personal and affectionate bond in the act of offering [2].

In the Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra Part-3, Yashoda is described both as the foster mother and biological mother of Krishna. She cared for him with great love and devotion, experiencing divine visions of the Lord [1]. Despite witnessing his mischievous acts and divine powers, her devotion remained steadfast [2].

Yashoda is celebrated as a goddess of liberation who celebrates Krishna’s birthday with affection and care [3]. She plays an essential role in various narratives involving Krishna's childhood adventures. For example, she watches over him during his confrontation with the serpent Kāḷiṅga [4] and laments alongside other Gopālas when Nanda is believed to have drowned in the Yamuna [5].

Yashoda also serves as a maternal figure for the girl who escaped from Kaṁsa [6], and she plays a significant role in the upbringing of both Krishna and Balarāma [7]. She is deeply affected by Krishna’s absence, weeping upon seeing Uddhava, who came as Krishna's emissary [8]. During Balarāma’s visit to Nandagokula, he honors Yashoda [9], and she is embraced by both Krishna and Balarāma during a meeting with Vasudeva and Devakī [10][11].

Yashoda is also recognized as a nurturing figure in Krishna's early life. She is to whom Krishna prostrates before meeting the Gopikas, symbolizing her central role in his upbringing [12].

In other sources, Yashoda is mentioned in Sanskrit lexicons and dictionaries with various meanings and contexts. In Vācaspatyam, she is referred to as a woman who is married to Nandagopa [3]. Other lexicons list "Yasoda" as referring to quicksilver, though this usage is not typically associated with the mythological figure in Hindu literature.
