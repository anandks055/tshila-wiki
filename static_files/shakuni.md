## Shakuni: The Master Plotter in Hindu Epic Literature

### Overview
Shakuni, a prominent figure in the epic Mahabharata, is known for his villainous role and cunning strategies. He was born as the eldest son of King Subala of Gāndhāra and was the maternal uncle to Duryodhana through his sister, Queen Gāndhārī.[1][2] Shakuni's character is often likened to Satan in terms of his villainy and craftiness. His masterful plotting and wicked intentions were key factors in instigating many conflicts within the epic narrative.

### Family Background
Shakuni was born out of the anger of the devas or gods, destined to destroy righteousness.[1] He lived in Hastināpura after Gāndhārī's marriage to King Dhrtarāṣtra. His lineage is also referenced in other texts like the Garuda Purana where he is mentioned as a descendant of Hiranyaksha and one of his sons.[3]

### Major Roles
#### Advisor to Duryodhana
Shakuni advised Duryodhana on various nefarious schemes, including cheating Yudhiṣṭhira in dice games which led to the Pāṇḍavas' loss of everything and contributed significantly to the onset of the Kurukṣetra war.[2][3] He was also accused by Vidura as an evil influence alongside Duryodhana, Duśśāsana, and Karṇa.[4]

#### Key Events
- **Cheating in Dice Games**: Shakuni played a crucial role in cheating Yudhiṣṭhira during the dice games. This included winning not only wealth but also stakes like brothers and Draupadi through unfair means.[1][2]
- **Kurukṣetra War Participation**: During the war, he was defeated by Nakula on the 14th day and ultimately killed by Sahadeva on the last day.[6]

### Other Appearances
Shakuni appears in various other texts with different identities:
- In the Brahmanda Purana, he is mentioned as a wife of Nāka, mother to numerous Rākṣasas, one of Ikṣvāku's fifty sons, son of Sanadvāja, and also referred to as the son of Ekādaśaratha.[4][5]
- In the Harivamsha Purana, he is identified as Dasharatha’s son, father of Karambha, a demon slain by Krishna, and one of the kings supporting Brahmadatta's Yajna.[6]

### Medical Context
In the medical text Sushruta Samhita, "Shakuni" refers to two distinct conditions affecting infants characterized by symptoms like looseness of limbs and bird-like smell. It also describes a condition where a child is believed to be possessed by a deity or spirit named Shakuni requiring specific treatments.[7][8]

### Conclusion
Shakuni’s role as a master plotter and his cunning tactics played a significant part in the unfolding events of the Mahabharata, contributing to the adversarial relationship between the Kauravas and Pandavas. His actions ultimately led to the devastating Kurukṣetra war.
