## Overview

Narmada is a significant river in Indian mythology and geography, as well as the name of a district in Gujarat. In mythological contexts, Narmada is mentioned in the Brahmanda Purana, where it serves as a location for quenching thirst after a hunt by Śūra and his companions [1]. Additionally, Narmada is noted as one of the places visited by Jyāmagha after he left his kingdom [2].

## Etymology and Mythology

The name "Narmada" has roots in Sanskrit, where it means "causing pleasure," reflecting its significance in ancient texts. In the Brahmanda Purana, Narmada is personified as a river that brings joy and refreshment to those who encounter it [3]. This association with pleasure aligns with its description in other sources as a sporting or fun-causing term [4][5].

## Musical Significance

In Carnatic music, "Narmada" refers to a janya raga (derived scale) of nāmanārāyaṇi. The specific ascending and descending scales are:

**Arohanam:** S R1 G3 M2 D1 N2 S  
**Avarohanam:** S N2 D1 M2 P M2 G3 R1 S

## Geographical Details

Narmada is also the name of a district in Gujarat, India. The administrative headquarters of this district is Rajpipla, located approximately 204 kilometers north of Gandhinagar, the state capital [6]. Spanning an area of about 2,749 square kilometers, it has a population of 590,379 and ranks 23rd in Gujarat by population. Gujarati and Hindi are spoken in this region. The district is divided into five talukas, with Nandod being the largest in terms of population [6].

## Connectivity

The Narmada district shares borders with Surat and Vadodara districts. It has access to nearby railway stations and airports; notably, Vadodara Airport is 65 kilometers away from Rajpipla.
