## Introduction

Vidura is a significant figure in Hindu mythology, particularly within the epic narrative of the *Mahābhārata*. He is celebrated for his wisdom, righteousness, and unwavering adherence to dharma. Vidura's life and actions exemplify moral integrity and spiritual devotion.

## Birth and Lineage

Vidura was born as a son of Vyāsa through Ambikā’s maid, making him a *śudra* by birth but renowned for his saintly nature.[1][2] According to some sources, he is considered an incarnation of Yama, the god of dharma.[3]

## Role and Contributions

### Ministerial Duties and Counsel

Vidura served as Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s minister and advisor. He provided wise counsel and often warned against unrighteous actions that could lead to calamity.[4][5] For example, he advised Dhṛtarāṣṭra multiple times not to support his sons in their deceitful schemes.[6]

### Protection of the Pāṇḍavas

Vidura played a crucial role in protecting the Pāṇḍavas from Duryodhana’s plots. He warned Yudhiṣṭhira about the dangers of Varanavata and helped the Pāṇḍavas escape.[7][8] Additionally, he advised Kuntī on how to ensure her sons' safety during their exile.[9]

### Participation in Rituals

Vidura was involved in important ceremonies such as the *Rajasūya* sacrifice where he served as a disburser.[10] He also arranged Pandu’s funeral rites and ensured that traditional customs were followed.[11]

## Pilgrimage and Spiritual Quest

After being insulted by Duryodhana, Vidura left Hastinapura to undertake a pilgrimage. During this journey, he visited various holy sites including Prabhāsā and Yamunā where he met Uddhava and Maitreya.[12][13] He inquired about the welfare of his kinsmen and received spiritual teachings from these sages.[14]

## Death

Vidura’s life came to an end after a period of intense spiritual practice. After the death of Dhṛtarāṣṭra and his wife, Vidura went on a pilgrimage and ultimately cast off his body at Prabhāsā in a forest fire.[15][16] He is revered as having realized the Yoga power of Hari.

## Legacy

Viduraniti, Vidura’s teachings to Dhṛtarāṣṭra, has become a classic text on ethics.[17] His life and actions continue to be celebrated for their profound moral and spiritual significance.
