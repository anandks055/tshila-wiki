## Keshi

### Overview
Keshi is primarily known as an Asura or demon in Hindu mythology, often depicted as being defeated by Vishnu or Krishna. The name "Keshi" appears in various ancient texts such as the Garuda Purana and Mahabharata.

### Mythological Context
In the context of Hindu mythology, Keshi is portrayed as a formidable Asura or demon. According to the Garuda Purana, Keshi has been defeated by Vishnu in several instances [1]. In one account from the Agastya Samhita (Chapter XCIII), Keshi is mentioned as being vanquished by Hari, an epithet for Vishnu [2]. Additionally, in the Brihaspati (Nitisara) Samhita (Chapter CXLIV), Keshi is identified as a monster slain by Krishna to uplift the spirits of the Gopas and Gopa-maidens in Brindaban [3]. The Dhanvantari Samhita also mentions Keshi, noting that he was destroyed by Vishnu, symbolizing Vishnu’s triumph over evil [4].

In the Mahabharata, Keshi is described as a demon slain by Krishna. This victory symbolizes Arjuna's martial skill in the Karna Parva (Section 94) and further emphasizes Krishna's prowess in the Shalya Parva (Section 63). Furthermore, in the Mausala Parva (Section 1), Keshi is linked to Janardana (another name for Vishnu or Krishna), who interprets omens and provides guidance to the Yadavas [5].

The Skanda Purana portrays Keśi as a terrifying Asura causing distress alongside other demons such as Ariṣṭa, Dhenuka, and Pralamba. In another account from the same text, Keśi is described as being torn apart by a deity during a divine dance [6].

### Etymology and Linguistic Notes
In Sanskrit, Keshi is referred to in various contexts. The term can denote a demon or Asura, often associated with Vishnu’s victories over evil forces [7]. In Kannada, the word "ಕೇಶಿ" (kēśi) has multiple meanings; it can be an adjective meaning having long or handsome hair and also a noun referring to a solitary, wandering sage from the Vedic period [8].

### Geographical Location
Keshi is also the name of a village located in the Malasa tehsil of Kanpur Dehat district in Uttar Pradesh, India. It lies approximately 10 kilometers west of Akbarpur and 8 kilometers from Malasa. Nearby villages include Tora Mohammadpur, Bihar, Gauriyapur, Kumbhi, and Bargawan. The village is surrounded by blocks such as Akbarpur, Derapur, Amrodha, and Sarbankhera [9].
