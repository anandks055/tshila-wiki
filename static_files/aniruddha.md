## Aniruddha: Mythological and Philosophical Significance

### Definition and Heritage
Aniruddha is a multifaceted figure in Hindu mythology, recognized for his various roles and attributes. He is described as unstoppable and unrestrained, symbolizing an unbound essence [1]. In the lineage of divinity, Aniruddha is the grandson of Kṛṣṇa and son of Pradyumna [1][5].

### Mythological Accounts

#### Love Story with Uṣā
In the Mahabharata and Harivamsha Purana, Aniruddha’s love story with Uṣā, daughter of Bāṇāsura, is a central narrative. Uṣā falls in love with him after seeing him in a dream, leading to his magical transportation to her palace [1][7]. Despite attempts by Bāṇāsura to thwart their union and capture Aniruddha, he ultimately fails. Kṛṣṇa resolves the conflict and arranges for their marriage [8].

#### Battles and Heroism
Aniruddha is depicted as a valiant warrior who assists other Yadavas in battles and faces imprisonment by Vana's forces before being rescued by the goddess Durga [7]. His capture causes significant distress among the Yadavas, but his eventual rescue and marriage are celebrated with great festivity [7].

### Religious Significance

#### Emanations of Viṣṇu
Aniruddha is considered one of the four vyuhas or emanations of Lord Viṣṇu in the Bhāgavata cult, symbolizing the cosmic mind [1]. In temple worship and religious practices, he represents detachment and is worshipped alongside other forms of Vishnu such as Vāsudeva, Saṃkarṣaṇa, and Pradyumna [5][6].

#### Philosophical Contributions
Aniruddha authored the Sāñkhyapravacanasutravṛtti, a commentary on Sāṅkhya philosophy, contributing to its interpretation and dissemination [3][4]. He is recognized for his interpretations of philosophical texts within this tradition.

### Divine Representations

#### Forms in Shaligrams
Aniruddha appears as a form of Lord Vishnu worshipped through the chant "OM AH OM NAMO BHAGAVATE ANIRUDDHAY NAMAH" [1] and is associated with specific types of Shaligram stones featuring eleven chakras [2].

#### Guardian Deity
He is recognized as one of the guardian deities, assigned to protect the west direction during worship rituals [3][4]. Additionally, he is invoked for protection of the skin in religious practices [13].

### Literary References

In literary sources like the Mahabharata and various Puranas, Aniruddha is referenced across numerous contexts. He attends Yudhishthira's Rajasuya sacrifice as a Vrishni warrior, teaches Pandava children, assists Arjuna, and represents aspects of creation and destruction [1-8].

### Conclusion
Aniruddha’s multifaceted nature encompasses his roles in love stories, battles, philosophical contributions, and divine representations. His importance spans across Hindu mythological texts and religious practices, making him a significant figure within the tradition.
