## Abhimanyu

Abhimanyu is one of the greatest warriors of the second generation in the Mahābhārata and a legendary figure. He was the son of Arjuna and Subhadrā, and the nephew of Lord Krishna. He was the incarnation of Varchas, the son of Soma [2]. Abhimanyu was trained in martial arts by his father Arjuna and uncle Sri Kṛṣṇa, maturing into a great warrior at an early age.

### Birth & Training

Abhimanyu's education began while he was still in Subhadra’s womb. He overheard Arjuna telling Subhadra the secrets of how to enter, exit, and destroy various battle formations. Notably, he only heard how to enter (but not exit or destroy) the secret of the Chakravyu formation as Subhadra fell asleep, thus Arjuna did not complete his explanation [3].

Abhimanyu spent his childhood in Dwaraka with Subhadra and Lord Krishna, where he was trained by Pradyumna, the son of Sri Krishna. He was brought up under the guidance of Lord Krishna and Balrama.

### Marriage

Arjuna arranged Abhimanyu's marriage to Uttara, the daughter of King Virata [27][28]. The Pandavas had been hiding incognito in the Matsya kingdom during their final year of exile. During this time, Arjuna played the role of dance teacher to Uttara. At the end of the period of exile, upon finding out who the Pandavas were, King Virata offered to marry Uttara to Arjuna; however, Arjuna had come to think of her as his daughter and asked for her to be married to Abhimanyu instead [1].

### Participation in the Mahābhārata War

Abhimanyu was sixteen years old when he fought in the Mahābhārata war. The warriors he faced were far more experienced and seasoned than him, yet he exhibited courage and valor that exceeded his age and experience.

#### Abhimanyu's Role on the 13th Day of the War

During the 13th day of the Mahābhārata war, Drona created the Chakravyu formation, which only Arjuna and Krishna knew how to defeat. Unfortunately, they were dragged into a battle with the Samsaptaka forces led by Susharma at another part of the battlefield and could not reach the Chakravyu formation in time [4].

Seeing the destruction of his army, Yudhishthira asked Abhimanyu for help. Abhimanyu informed him that he knew how to enter the Chakravyu formation but not how to exit it. Yudhisthira, Bhima, Nakula, Sahadeva, and other warriors were to follow Abhimanyu into the chakravyu to assist him in getting out [4]. However, Jayadratha, king of the Sindhus, used a boon received from Shiva to single-handedly hold off the Pandava forces for the day. Additionally, Abhimanyu’s zeal led him to rush through the enemy lines without waiting for his senior colleagues to support him [5].

#### Battle and Deeds

Abhimanyu attacked whoever came his way and vanquished many warriors including:

- King of Asmaka
- Sushena
- Drighalochana
- Kundavedhin
- Younger brother of Salya 
- Younger brother of Karna (younger son of Radha)
- Vasatiya 
- Rukmaratha, son of Salya 
- Lakshmana, son of Duryodhana 
- King of Kratha 
- Vrindaraka
- Vrihadvala, king of Kosala 
- Six of Karna's counsellors 
- Son of the ruler of the Magadhas
- Bhoja prince of Martikavata
- Satrunjaya
- Chandraketu
- Mahamegba
- Suvarchas
- Suryabhasa
- Suvala’s son, Kalikeya [5][6][7][8][9][10][11][12]

He also fought and wounded the following maha-rathis:

- Karna 
- Salya 
- Dushasana 
- Vrishasena 
- Duryodhana

As night drew near, Duryodana asked Drona how Abhimanyu could be killed. Drona observed Abhimanyu's incredible skill and noted that he was almost as formidable as Arjuna himself:

> "Observing him with vigilance, have any of you been able to detect any defeat in this youth? He is careening in all directions. Yet have any of you been able to detect today the least hole in him? Behold the lightness of hand and quickness of motion of this lion among men, this son of Arjuna. In the track of his car, only his bow drawn to a circle can be seen, so quickly is he aiming his shafts and so quickly is he letting them off. Indeed, this slayer of hostile heroes, viz., the son of Subhadra, gratifieth me although he afflicteth my vital breath and stupefieth me with shafts. Even the mightiest car-warriors, filled with wrath, are unable to detect any flaw in him. The son of Subhadra, therefore, careering on the field of battle, gratifieth me greatly. I do not see that in battle there is any difference between the wielder of Gandiva himself and this one of great lightness of hand, filling all the points of the horizon with his mighty shafts." [4]

Karna also expressed his difficulty in fighting Abhimanyu:

> "Exceedingly afflicted with the shafts of Abhimanyu, I am staying in battle, only because (as a warrior) I should stay here. Indeed, the arrows of this son of great energy are exceedingly fierce. Terrible as they are and possessed of the energy of fire, these arrows are weakening my heart." [5]

Drona advised Karna to cut off Abhimanyu's bow, bow-string, reins of his steeds, the steeds themselves, and two Parshni charioteers. With this strategy, they deprived him of his car and divested him of his bow.

Karna quickly cut Abhimanyu’s bow. Kritayarman killed his horses and Kripacharya killed his two Parshni charioteers [4]. The others all simultaneously attacked him with their arrows. Bowless and carless, Abhimanyu took up a sword and a shield and attacked. However, Drona cut his sword at the hilt and Karna destroyed his shield [14]. He then picked up a chariot wheel from the ground and rushed at Drona. The kings all attacked and broke the wheel into small fragments. Abhimanyu than picked up a mace and rushed at Aswatthaman, who jumped out of his chariot and ran as Abhimanyu destroyed his chariot. He then attacked and killed Kalikeya and seven hundred and seventy of his army. He attacked Duhasasana’s son and destroyed his chariot. They both knocked each other down. Duhasasana's son got up first and hit Abhimanyu on the head, killing him as he was getting up [14][35].

### Death and Aftermath

Abhimanyu’s death was considered a turning point in the Mahābhārata war. On the 14th day, the Pandavas retaliated with all their might and fury—holding nothing back. Bhimasena killed twenty-five of Duryodhana's brothers and rendered Karna helpless and ineffective [37]. Arjuna killed Jayadratha [54]. Collectively, the Pandavas destroyed eight of the eleven Akshauhini Kaurava army [36][38].

### Legacy

Abhimanyu was the most illustrious of the Pandavas/Kurus of his generation and deemed to be the most eligible and qualified heir to the throne of Hastinapura. His son, Parikshit, succeeded Yudhishthira to the throne [21][36]. It is for King Parikshit that the Bhagavata was first recited.
