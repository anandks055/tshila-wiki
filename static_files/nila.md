## Overview

Nila is a multifaceted term with varied significances across different contexts, including mythological references and geographical locations in India.

### Mythology

In Hindu mythology, Nila has multiple representations. According to the Markandeya Purana, it refers to one of the seven great mountain ranges in Jambudvīpa, situated centrally and extending into the sea [1]. Additionally, in another section of the same text, Nila is identified as a mountain located on the northern side of Meru [2]. Furthermore, Nila is described as a Nidhi (a type of treasure) associated with goodness and passion, contributing to wealth accumulation and establishing pleasure spaces [3].

In the Ramayana, Nila is portrayed as a monkey warrior created by Agni. He is noted for his exceptional bravery and leadership, leading large contingents of monkeys in various missions, such as searching for Sita and attacking Lanka. Nila played significant roles in battles, including confronting Prahasta and weakening Kumbhakarna before Rama's final assault [37-38]. Despite facing numerous challenges, he was celebrated and rewarded by Rama after the war for his contributions.

### Geography

In a geographical context, Nila is the name of several villages located in the Marathwada region of Maharashtra, India. The primary Nila village is situated in Nanded Tehsil within Nanded District, with a pin code of 431605 [29-38]. It is known for its religious sites such as Temple Nila and Pataleshwar Shiv Mandir, located approximately 15 kilometers north of Nanded. Another notable village named Nila is in Purna Tehsil within Parbhani District with a pin code of 431511 [29-38]. This village is surrounded by rivers like the Purna and Borna. There are also other villages called Nila located in Sonpeth Tehsil, Parbhani District (pin code 431516) and Loha Tehsil, Nanded District (pin code 431708). Each of these locations offers various educational institutions and religious sites.
