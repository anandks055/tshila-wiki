## Setubandha

### Overview

Setubandha is multifaceted, encompassing both geographical and literary connotations within Hindu tradition. It refers to a physical location in Tamil Nadu where the Jyotirliṅga Rāmeśvara is situated, as well as to various literary works focusing on the construction of Rama's bridge across the sea to Lanka.

### Geographical Significance

In terms of geography, Setubandha denotes Adam’s Bridge between India and Ceylon (modern-day Sri Lanka). According to Hindu mythology, this bridge was constructed by Lord Rāma with the assistance of Sugriva and his Vanara army for crossing over to Lankā. This location is particularly significant as it houses two Jyotirliṅgas: Rāmeśvara and Rāmeśa, according to the Shiva Purana [1][2]. The Skanda Purana further highlights Setubandha as a revered site where Brāhmaṇas sought visions of Rāma and Hanumān, and it is also noted for its sacredness conducive to achieving Siddhis and chanting the Pañcākṣara Mantra [3].

### Literary Works

Setubandha has inspired numerous literary works. One notable commentary on Nityasodaśikārnava from the Vāmakeśvaratantra, authored by Bhāskararāya Makhin, is titled Setubandha [2]. Additionally, it appears as a name in various poetic and scholarly compositions:

1. **Bhaṭṭikāvya**: The 13th book of this epic poem is named Setubandha.
2. **Prakrit Poems**: Several Prakrit poems on the history of Rama, attributed to authors like Pravarasena and Kālidāsa, are titled Setubandha or Rāmasetu [3][6].
3. **Mantras and Commentaries**: A mantra by Paṇḍitācārya is named Setubandha, as well as commentaries on texts such as the Rasataraṅgiṇīṭīkā and Kādimata-tantraṭīkā [4][5].

### Mythological Context

According to the Shiva Purana and the Skanda Purana, Setubandha is not just a physical location but also holds spiritual significance. The construction of the bridge by Rama and his allies underscores its importance in Hindu mythology. In the Vācaspatyam, it is described that Lord Śiva manifested as Rāmeśvara at this site during the building of the bridge [7].
