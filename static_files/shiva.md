## Etymology

Śiva (शिव) literally means "the auspicious one." Śiva is a major deity in Indic knowledge traditions and is often referred to as Rudra, especially in his terrifying aspect [1].

## Shruti and Smriti References

### Garuda Purana
In the Garuda Purana, Shiva is depicted as a multifaceted divine figure who interacts with Lord Vishnu extensively. He meditates on Vishnu at Kailash Mountain and directs deities to seek deeper knowledge from Vishnu [2]. Additionally, Vishnu addresses Shiva as 'O Rudra,' discussing his omnipotence and elucidating the origins of the world and genealogies of dynasties [3][4]. Shiva advises Sati against attending Daksha Prajapati's Ashwamedha Yagya and subsequently curses Daksha after learning of her death [5].

### Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra
In the Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra, Śiva is portrayed as a warrior, yogi, and protector. He slays the Tripurāsuras, earning the title 'Tripurāri,' and assists gods against asuras with Lord Viṣṇu's help [6]. During the churning of the ocean, he consumes the poison Hālāhala, bearing it on his throat [7]. He also prostrates before Vāmana due to his divine radiance [8].

## Puranic Narratives

### Śivapurāṇa
The Śivapurāṇa narrated by Sūta to Śaunaka emphasizes the sacred nature of its tales, believed to grant salvation upon hearing them [9]. Śiva is described as auspicious, unmatched, and eternal, with stories that purify minds and enable devotees to attain his region [10][11].

Śiva's role in resolving conflicts includes manifesting as a column of fire between Brahmā and Viṣṇu, later punishing Brahmā for lying [12][13]. He explains the five-fold duties (sarga, sthiti, saṃhāra, tirobhāva, anugraha) and the significance of the Oṃkāra mantra to Brahmā and Viṣṇu [14].

### Shiva Purana
The Shiva Purana narrates Śiva's central role in various aspects including dissolution and creation. He is revered as the consort of Pārvatī, with stories that grant liberation and fulfillment of desires [15][16]. His abode, Kailāśa, provides refuge to Devas after witnessing conflicts between Brahmā and Viṣṇu [12].

Śiva's phallic form (liṅga) is described as a method of worship with specific rituals and characteristics, emphasizing his dual nature as both Sakala (embodied) and Niṣkala (formless) [17]. Śiva's temples and holy centers are believed to offer salvation and liberation to people [18].

## Iconography

### Portrayal
Śiva is often depicted as a handsome youth with three eyes, the third eye being on his forehead between the eyebrows. He has four arms holding various items such as the triśula (trident) and ḍamaru (drum). He also wears the crescent moon and holds Gaṅgā in his hair [1].

### Attributes
Śiva is associated with numerous symbols including:
- Triśula - trident
- Cakra - discus
- Paraśu - battle axe
- Damaru - drum
- Akṣamālā - rosary
- Mṛga - deer
- Pāśa - noose
- Daṇḍa - staff
- Pināka or ajagava - bow
- Khatvāṅga - magic wand
- Pāśupata - spear
- Padma - lotus
- Kapāla - skull-cup
- Darpaṇa - mirror
- Khaḍga - sword

The triśula symbolizes the three guṇas and cosmic processes. The ḍamaru represents sound, grammar, and creation [1].

### Worship Practices
Śiva is worshiped both in his embodied form and as the liṅga due to his dual nature as Sakala (embodied) and Niṣkala (formless). Hearing Śiva's glory grants merit until food consumed is digested. His worship can prevent premature death, grant longevity, and provide liberation from rebirth [19][20].

## Geographical Presence

### Notable Temples
Śiva is revered in numerous temples across India:
- Amaranātha shrine in a cave in the Bhairavaghati range [21]
- Largest temple in Cambā State [22]
- Renowned temple in Kumbhakoṇam celebrated across Southern India [23]

He is also associated with significant sites such as Aimbundī, Jambukeśvara, Mahābalipuram, Nandivelugu, Paramagudi, Tandikoṃḍa, Tirukkuḍamukkila, Tirumaṇikuli, Tirupati, Candranātha, Gauḍa, Gṛdhrakūṭaparvata, Hajo, Kāmakhyā, Viṣṇupura, Kārli, and Puṣkara [26-38].

### Villages Named Shiva
Shiva is the name of villages in Gujarat, Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Bihar, and Rajasthan. Each village has unique geographical and cultural features including local languages, educational institutions, and religious sites [24].

## Similar Words

The term Śiva is often written as ṣiv, śivā, śīva, śīvā, ṣiva, śivaḥ, śivam, śivaṃ, śīvan [25].
