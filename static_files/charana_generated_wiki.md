## Etymology

Caraṇa (चरण) is derived from the Sanskrit root "car" (to move), literally meaning 'that by which one moves'. It has diverse meanings across various contexts within Indic knowledge traditions.

## General Meaning

The term caraṇa has been used in several senses, including:
- Foot [2][6][9]
- A quarter of verse [1][4][5][8][9]
- Pillar or foundation of a temple [9]
- Part of a musical composition in Carnatic music and bilāsanda music [3][4]

## Vedic Terminology

In the Vedic context, caraṇa refers to a branch (śākhā) of the Veda and encompasses not only the particular śākhā but also its allied literature like law-books. This includes groups of people who study it [1].

## Buddhism

In Buddhist tradition, caraṇa denotes eight aspects of character such as fearlessness and self-control, which are deemed fit to be cultivated.

## Sanskrit Grammar

Caraṇa is explained as a synonym for the word "śākhā," meaning a branch or school of Vedic learning [5].

## Vācaspatyam

Vācaspatyam elaborates on several meanings of caraṇa:
1. Foot, specifically in poetic usage like "Suta with Dhata's feet" [6]
2. A branch or division of the Vedas [6]
3. Ray from a celestial body like the sun [6]
4. One-fourth part of a stanza or verse [6]
5. Part of a musical composition [6]
6. Conduct, behavior, or manners [6]

## Kannada Usage

In Kannada, caraṇa (ಚರಣ) can refer to:
1. The act or instance of moving; movement.
2. Behavior or conduct.
3. Foot.
4. Stanza in poetry.
5. Symbol for the number two.
6. Part of Jain scriptures dealing with conduct and procedures [7].

## Vedic Rituals

In rituals, caraṇa signifies a mode of behavior or practice (vyavahāra). For instance, it is used to describe the common practices among various sacrificial fires like Agniṣomīya [8].

## Similar Words

The term has several variations in usage:
- carana
- cāraṇa
- caraṇaḥ
- cāraṇaḥ
- caraṇam
- caraṇaṃ
