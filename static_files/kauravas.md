## Kauravas

The Kauravas are a prominent clan in Indic knowledge traditions, particularly noted as descendants of King Kuru and specifically applied to the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra [8]. Led by Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Duryodhana, they feature prominently both as adversaries of the Pandavas and participants in shared religious activities [1][2].

In the Skanda Purana's Book 1 - Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Section 2 - Kaumārikā-khaṇḍa, Chapter 63, the Devas warn the Kauravas that obtaining sacred ash could be perilous for them [2]. In Chapter 65 of the same section, they are portrayed as the rival clan of the Pandavas, embodying the opposition that the Pandavas must overcome [3].

Additionally, in Chapter 66, the Kauravas are depicted as the army opposing the Pandavas under Duryodhana's leadership during the battle of Kurukṣetra [4]. Meanwhile, in Book 6 - Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Section 1 - Tīrtha-māhātmya, Chapters 72 and 73, Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Duryodhana travel with the Pandavas to perform religious ceremonies at Hāṭakeśvara. They also participate in the marriage of Duryodhana with Bhānumatī and the installation of Liṅgas [5][6].

In Chapter 74, the Kauravas demonstrate their devotion by installing Liṅgas alongside the Pandavas and Yadavas [7]. These instances illustrate both the conflicts and shared religious practices involving the Kaurava clan within Indic texts.
