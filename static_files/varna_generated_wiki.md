## Etymology

The term **varṇa** (वर्ण) has multiple meanings and uses within Indic knowledge traditions. It can refer to social divisions, phonemic units, and specific deities.

## Vārṇa as Social Division

### Shruti and Smriti Perspectives

In the context of societal structure, **varṇa** refers to the fourfold division of ancient Bhāratīya society into distinct social classes based on occupation and duties [3][4][5]. These divisions became rigid and hereditary over time, leading to the creation of various castes and subcastes through intermixing, including the Niṣāda caste [7].

Each varṇa has specific duties (svadharma) one is bound to perform according to their social standing. For instance, brāhmaṇa householders are expected to bathe twice daily as part of their religious observances [5][6]. The concept of **varṇasaṅkara** denotes the mixing or intermarriage between different varnas, while sāmanya dharmas apply universally beyond these divisions [8][13].

### Purana Perspectives

The Bhagavata Purana describes varṇa as the four social classes in Hindu society, each with specific religious duties that contribute to the pursuit of liberation from Saṃsāra [20][21]. According to the Agni Purana, Varna is one of the gods worshipped on the southern side of a building site layout and also refers to the God of Death, responsible for determining the fate of souls after death based on their deeds [23][24].

In the Skanda Purana, varṇa plays a crucial role in both societal structure and religious practices. It dictates that Śrāddha rituals should adhere to the traditions specific to one's caste [26]. Additionally, Viṣṇu is personified as Varna, emphasizing his embodiment of societal order and his central role in maintaining the caste-based social hierarchy [27].

### Transcendence and Elevation

Individuals within the varṇa system can transcend their social divisions after realizing the Self, as described in texts like the Avadhuta Upaniṣad and Devīgītā [11][12]. Opportunities for elevation within the varṇa system exist through adherence to dharma, impacting prescribed duties and practices in conjunction with āśrama and ācāra [14][15].

## Vārṇa as a Phonemic Unit

In linguistic contexts, **varṇa** refers to phonemic units or letters of the Sanskrit alphabet. The term was used in ancient times and found usage generally in the masculine gender but occasionally in the neuter gender too [18]. Examples include references like "upadiṣṭā ime varṇāḥ" (M.Bh. Ahnika 1) and "mā kadācidavarṇe bhūt" (M.Bh. on Siva Sutras 3, 4).

## Vārṇa as a Deity

**Varṇa** is also the name of a god worshipped in some texts. For instance, it appears in The Purana index where Varṇa refers to Sudharmāṇa God [1].

## Geographical and Administrative Contexts

Varna can also refer to several villages in India, including locations in Kaprada Tehsil, Valsad District, Gujarat; Jintur Tehsil, Parbhani District, Maharashtra; and Dholka Tehsil, Ahmedabad District, Gujarat. These areas are characterized by their proximity to rivers such as Sabarmati and Dhaman Par, and offer various educational institutions including schools and colleges [28].

## Similar Words

The term **varṇa** has several similar forms in Sanskrit:
- varṇ
- varṇā
- vārṇa
- varṇaḥ
- varnam
- varṇaṃ
