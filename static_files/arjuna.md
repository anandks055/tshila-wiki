## Etymology

Arjuna (अर्जुन) literally means "white; fair; clear; bright" and is used to describe someone who is "fair in visage, pure in mind and body; illuminating, enlightening; silvery; made of silver; the peacock." [1]

## Shruti

### Arjuna as a Key Pāṇḍava Hero
Arjuna was one of the five Pāṇḍava princes and is most widely known for his valor and skill in archery. Born as the youngest son of Kuntī, with Indra's assistance, he became the most favorite disciple of Droṇācārya, the master of archery [2]. Bent upon settling scores with King Drupada, who had humiliated him earlier, Droṇācārya sought Arjuna’s help in defeating Drupada. Arjuna successfully fulfilled this mission and later won Draupadī's hand through a contest disguised as a brāhmaṇa [1].

### Personal Interactions with Deities and Sages
Arjuna assisted Agni in burning down the Khāṇḍava forest, earning divine weapons as rewards from Indra. He learned the Brahmāstra from Droṇa and the fire missile (āgneyāstra) from a Gandharva named Citraratha [3][4]. Arjuna also performed severe penance at the Indrakīla mountains to propitiate Lord Śiva, who appeared in disguise as a hunter chieftain and granted him the celestial weapon pāśu-patāstra after a fierce battle [5][6].

## Smriti

### Arjuna's Role in Kurukṣetra War
Arjuna played a pivotal role in the Kurukṣetra war, receiving spiritual teachings from Kṛṣṇa in the Bhagavadgītā. He was known for his valor and expertise with the Gāṇḍīva bow [2][7]. Before the war, he approached Srī Kṛṣṇa for help and preferred him as his charioteer over the entire Yādava army without Kṛṣṇa [8][9].

### Spiritual Teachings
Kṛṣṇa taught Arjuna various aspects of spiritual wisdom and the path to mokṣa, covering a wide range of topics from dharma to devotion [10]. During his exile, he assumed the guise of Bṛhannalā and taught dancing to Virāṭa’s daughter Uttarā [11][12].

### Divine Encounters
Arjuna's character embodies the ideals of a kṣattriya, fulfilling his svadharma by fighting to defend righteousness against evil. He exemplifies forms of divine encounter and devotion such as sākhyabhakti and rāgānugabhakti [13][14].

## Itihasa

### Arjuna's Marriage and Children
Arjuna had four wives: Draupadī, Subhadrā, Ulupī, and Citrāṅgadā. His children include Abhimanyu (son of Subhadrā), Babhruvāhana (son of Citrāṅgadā), Śrutakīrti (son of Draupadī), and Irāvān (son of Ulupī). After the Kurukṣetra war, Parīkṣit succeeded to the royal throne after Yudhiṣṭhira's abdication [1].

### Geographical References
Arjuna is mentioned in connection with various holy sites such as Badarikatirtha and Prabhasatirtha. He is also associated with Madura during the Bharata war, where his son Babhruvāhana ruled [15][16][17].

### Historical Accounts
There are accounts of a Sendpati named Arjuna who usurped the throne of Thaneswar, leading to the end of a Vaisya dynasty in the region [18]. Additionally, Arjuna is known as the father of Harivyāsa and guru of Mahādeva Bhaṭṭa [19].

## Purana

### Shiva Purana
In the Shiva Purana, Vyāsa selected Arjuna to perform penance and recite the lore of Indra in honor of Śiva. Arjuna assumed unmatched brilliance through the Śiva mantra and embarked on a penance advised by Vyāsa [20][21]. He faced and recognized Śiva's intervention during a battle with the demon Mūka, subsequently receiving the Pāśupata missile from Śiva [22][23].
