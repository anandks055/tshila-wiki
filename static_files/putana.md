## Putana

Putana is a multifaceted figure in Hindu mythology, primarily known as a demoness who attempted to kill infant Krishna but was instead killed by him. She appears across various texts, including the Sushruta Samhita, Garuda Purana, Harivamsha Purana, Mahabharata, Brahmanda Purana, Vishnu Purana, Brahma Purana, and Bhagavata Purana.

### Mythological Context

In Hindu mythology, Putana is described as a demoness sent by Kansa to eliminate infant Krishna. According to the Garuda Purana, she was one of several demons dispatched by Kansa to kill the child Krishna but was ultimately defeated by his divine powers [1][2]. Similarly, in the Harivamsha Purana, she serves multiple roles: as an enemy destined for defeat by Madhusudana (an epithet for Vishnu), a demoness commanded by Kamsa to destroy enemies, and ultimately, a nurse who attempts to poison Krishna but is killed by him [1][2][3].

### Medical Context

In the Sushruta Samhita, Putana is also described in two contexts. In one instance, she is listed as one of nine diseases affecting infants, characterized by symptoms such as loosening of limbs, disturbed sleep, vomiting, and a crow-like smell emanating from the body [1]. In another context, Putana is portrayed as the presiding deity of this disease, depicted as a slovenly, shag-haired goddess dressed in dirty clothes who frequents lonely dwellings and emits foul odors [2].

### Historical and Cultural References

Putana's role extends beyond mythology into historical narratives. The Mahabharata mentions her death at the hands of Krishna, which Sisupala uses to cast doubt on Krishna’s righteousness [1]. In the Brahmanda Purana, she is identified as a daughter of Bali and later described as a demoness killed by Kṛṣṇa, an avatar of Viṣṇu [1][2]. Additionally, in the Vishnu Purana, Putana is one of Kansa's Asura followers tasked with executing his plans against gods and threats; she attempts to murder Krishna through breastfeeding but is ultimately killed by him [1][2].

### Symbolic Significance

Her defeat by Krishna symbolizes an early display of his divine power and protection. In the Brahma Purana, her actions are noted as ominous signs that precede the relocation of the cowherd community to Vṛndāvana [3]. The Bhagavata Purana highlights Putana's liberation through Krishna’s mercy, transforming her into a revered figure despite her initial malicious intent [1][2].
