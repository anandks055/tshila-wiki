## Akrura: Overview

Akrura is a significant character in Hindu mythology, particularly known for his roles as a messenger, warrior, and devotee. He is the son of Śvaphalka (also referred to as Shaphalka) and Gāndinī [1][2], and he has several sons including Satyaketu, Devavat, Upadeva, and possibly Vasudeva [1][3]. Akrura is described as generous, learned, and a devotee of Krishna [1-4].

## Roles in Mythological Narratives

### Messenger to Mathurā
Akrura was appointed by Kamsa to bring Krishna and Balarāma from Vraja (Vrindavan) to Mathurā with the intent of eliminating them. However, upon meeting Krishna and Balarāma, Akrura became a devoted follower [3][5]. He communicated this news to Kamsa, who subsequently took aggressive actions against Krishna [4-7].

### Devotion and Service
Akrura is portrayed as deeply devoted to Krishna. When he was sent by Kamsa to bring the divine brothers to Mathurā, Akrura ended up becoming a devoted follower upon meeting them [3][5]. He later served as an emissary between Krishna and Kamsa, driving them to Mathurā and witnessing Krishna's divine form [4-6].

### Military Roles
Akrura participated in various military campaigns. He fought against Shalva in the defense of Dvārakā [1], and supported the Yadu family and their backing of the Pandavas during the Kurukshetra war [2][5]. Akrura also assisted the Pāṇḍavas, defended Dvārakā against Śālva's aerial car Saubha, and was involved in planning strategies against Jarāsandha’s army [6-8].

## Personal Achievements
Akrura is noted for acquiring the Syamantaka gem from Satadhanvan. He held onto it for six decades before returning it under pressure from Krishna [3][4]. In the process, he instigated Śatadhanvā to murder Satrājit and retain possession of the Syamantaka jewel [9].

## Later Life and Demise
Akrura participated in the fatal conflict at Prabhāsa that led to the destruction of the Yadava race. His involvement resulted in his own demise [6][13].

## Worship and Rituals
According to specific rituals mentioned in the Agni Purana, Akrura is also recognized as one of the presiding deities who should be worshipped [2].
