## Kurukshetra

Kurukshetra is an ancient site established by Kuru, son of Samvaraṇa and Tapatī of the Candravanśa lineage [1][2]. It is located between the rivers Sarasvatī and Dṛśadvatī [45] and is considered a sacred 'dharmakṣetra' or field of righteousness [3][20][21]. The site has been mentioned in various Indic categories such as Shruti, Smriti, Purana, Itihasa, and Kavya. It is renowned for its spiritual significance and historical events.

### Religious Significance

Kurukshetra has been hailed as the ‘dharmakṣetra’ or field of righteousness [1][2]. It is considered a very sacred spot from ancient times due to numerous yāgas or sacrifices performed by gods here. The region was also the heart of Vedic culture [3].

The site has been recognized for its spiritual significance, being synonymous with Syamantapañcaka and mentioned in the Rāmottaratāpaniya Upaniṣad [29][17]. It is noted as a dharmakṣetra where taking a bath during solar eclipses is recommended for religious merit [3][14].

### Historical Events

Kurukshetra is famously known as the battlefield where the great war described in the Mahābhārata took place, involving the Pāṇdavas and Kauravas. The war was triggered by a gambling incident and characterized by intense moral dilemmas faced by Arjuna, who sought guidance from Krishna [3][7]. Notable events include:

- The teaching of Bhagavadgītā to Arjuna [12].
- The killing of Śakuni by Sahadeva [33].
- The death of Abhimanyu [26][35].

The war saw numerous significant battles and the participation of various historical figures such as Śaṅkaradeva, who visited Kurukshetra [30], and Mudgala, who performed austerities there [34]. Additionally, the Pāṇḍavas constructed a Śiva temple in the region [17].

### Geographical Context

Kurukṣetra is located between the rivers Sarasvatī and Dṛśadvatī [2][45], near modern-day Thanesvara (present Thanesar), Sonepat, Amin, Karnal, Panipat, and Pehova. The region's boundaries were defined by the Drisadvati River according to the Mahābhārata [3][45]. It is also noted for its proximity to the Yamuna River [52].

### Nearby Locations

The forests mentioned near Kurukṣetra include Kāmyakavana, Aditivana, and Vyāsavana. The famous cities situated nearby are:
- Hastināpura (104 km northwest of Delhi).
- Indraprastha (now a part of old Delhi).
- Sthāṇeśvara (present Thanesar in Haryana).
- Pṛthudaka (present Pehova).

### Holy Spots

Holy spots near Kurukṣetra include:
- Varāhatīrtha - where Viṣṇu stood in his incarnation as a boar.
- Vyāsatirtha - where Vyāsa wanted to give up his body but was dissuaded from doing so.
- Saptasarasvata - where Śiva appeared before the sage Maṅkaṇaka.
- The place where Bhagavadgītā was taught by Kṛṣṇa to Arjuna.

### Modern Context

Kurukshetra is a town in the Kurukshetra district of Haryana, India. It falls under the Ambala division and is part of the Kurukshetra tehsil [67]. Its postal code is 136118. Notable educational institutions include Wisdom World School and Kurukshetra University.

### Conclusion

Kurukṣetra holds significant religious, historical, and geographical importance in Indic knowledge traditions. It has been a site for numerous spiritual practices, sacrifices, and the great war of Mahābhārata. Its significance is recognized through various texts and modern pilgrimages.
