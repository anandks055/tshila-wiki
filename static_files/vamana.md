## Vamana

### Definitions and Etymology

**Vamana**, a term derived from Sanskrit, has multiple meanings that span various contexts. In its neuter form, it can refer to vomiting or the act of ejecting contents from the mouth (Sources 4, 5, 6, 7). It also denotes an emetic substance that induces vomiting (Sources 1, 3, 4, 6, 7), and the condition of undergoing pain (Source 3). Additionally, it can signify offering oblations to fire in religious contexts (Sources 2, 6, 7).

In its masculine form, **Vamana** refers to hemp (Sources 6, 7), while in its feminine form, it signifies a leech (Sources 4, 6, 7). The etymology of the term is rooted in the verb "vama" meaning to vomit, with various affixes added for different grammatical cases and meanings (Source 8).

### Geographic Context

**Vamana** also denotes a village situated within the Pichhore tehsil of Shivpuri district in Madhya Pradesh, India. This location is approximately 52 kilometers east of the district headquarters, Shivpuri, and 269 kilometers from Bhopal, the state capital (Source 9). The region around Vamana includes several nearby cities such as Shivpuri, Jhansi, Datia, and Prithvipur. Geographically, it is bordered by Khaniadhana Tehsil to the south, Karera Tehsil to the north, Babina Tehsil to the east, and Narwar Tehsil to the north (Source 9).

### Cultural and Religious Significance

In Hindu mythology and religious texts, **Vamana** symbolizes one of the two gods that form a boat to cross the ocean of samsāra (the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth) (Source 1). The village itself is known for its religious sites, including Manikpur Mandir and Mata Seravali Temple, which reflect the cultural and spiritual heritage of the area.

### Educational Facilities

The educational landscape of Vamana includes schools like Govt. Primary School Todi and Higher Secondary Government School Bhonti (Source 9). These institutions play a crucial role in providing education to the local community.

### Natural Features

The region surrounding Vamana is characterized by natural features such as the rivers Ghurari and Betwa, which contribute to the area's ecological diversity and agricultural activities. The local language spoken in the village is Hindi (Source 9).
