## Vyasa: A Revered Sage and Compiler of Sacred Texts

Vyāsa, also known by several names such as Kṛṣṇadvaipāyana, Vedavyāsa, and Pārāśarya, is a highly revered ṛṣi in Hindu tradition. He is often considered an avatar due to his significant contributions to sacred texts and knowledge [7]. As the compiler and editor of the Veda, Vyasa divided them into four parts: the Ṛgveda, Yajurveda, Sāmaveda, and Atharvaveda [31][32].

### Contributions to Literature

Vyasa is traditionally recognized as the author of the Mahābhārata, an epic that includes the Vanaparva as its third book. He expressed deep concern over the neglect of dharma and used stories like those of Rājā Hayagrīva to teach moral lessons [17]. Additionally, he authored the Purāṇas, including the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas such as the Bhāgavata and the eighteen Upapurāṇas like the Gaṇeśapurāṇa [14][15].

### Other Literary Works

Vyasa is credited with composing the Bhiṣusūtra, which may be an early version of the Brahmasūtras [6]. He also authored the Bhikṣusūtra and composed commentaries on Patañjali's Yogasūtras, writing both the Vyāsabhāsya and a Bhāsya [27][25].

### Lineage and Role in Mythology

Vyasa played a significant role in the lineage leading to the Mahābhārata conflict. He fathered Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Pāṇḍu through niyoga, an ancient practice of appointing a surrogate father [28][29]. Vyāsa also bestowed divyadṛṣti upon Sañjaya, enabling him to narrate the Kurukṣetra battle to Dhṛtarāṣṭra [11].

### Spiritual Teachings

In the Devibhāgavata, Vyasa taught cleansing rituals to cleanse Rajā Janamejaya of sins [8]. He is also known for his role in various spiritual contexts, including his intervention during the birth of Gāndhārī’s sons and his prayers in the Prapannagītā [16][24].

### Hermitage Location

The hermitage of Vyasa is located in a village named Manal near Badrinatha in Garhwal, Himalayas [37].
