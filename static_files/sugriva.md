## Sugriva: A Multifaceted Character in Hindu Mythology

### Etymology and Birth
Sugriva, whose name means "one with a beautiful neck," is one of the sons of Rkṣarajas, the vānara king of Kiṣkindhā. He was born through the grace of Surya (the sun-god) and Indra (king of gods), making him a divine figure within Hindu mythology.[1][2]

### Relationship with Vāli
Sugriva is the younger half-brother of Vālī, both sons of Rkṣarajas. After being exiled by his brother Vālī, Sugriva sought refuge on Ṛṣyamuka hill.[5][8] This event marks a significant turning point in Sugriva’s narrative and sets the stage for his alliance with Rama.

### Alliance with Rama
Sugriva formed an important alliance with Rāma, who led him back to reclaim his kingdom from Vālī. As part of this alliance, Sugriva dispatched his army, including notable figures like Nala (a brave monkey-general) and Hanumān (his wise minister), in the search for Sita.[6][12] This quest culminated in the eventual triumph over Rāvaṇa and the rescue of Sītā.

### Military Campaigns
Sugriva is described as a powerful leader who dispatched his monkey soldiers to various regions including dāsarṇa, the Pāṇḍya country, and beyond. He also placed the region of Yavanas and the cities of Sakas between the lands of Kurus and Madras and the Himalayas.[1][2]

### Cultural Depictions
Sugriva is mentioned in various texts such as the Adbhuta rāmāyaṇa, Adhyātma Rāmāyana, and Rāmarahasya Upaniṣad. These sources highlight his importance not only as a warrior but also as an ally to Rama.[3][4][11]

### Religious Significance
In the Shiva Purana, Sugriva is portrayed as a powerful monkey king who forms an alliance with Rāma after receiving assistance from Hanumān. He aids Rāma in rescuing Sītā and also appears in other roles such as a messenger sent by Śumbha to retrieve the goddess.[1][2][3]

In the Garuda Purana, Sugriva is one of twenty-two deities worshipped during the adoration of the Vastu deity. He is also depicted as Rama's close friend who assists him in various missions.[1][2]

### Literary Appearances
Sugriva’s character appears in several Puranas and epics, including the Mahabharata where he is known both as a powerful monkey king and as one of Krishna’s swift horses. He plays a significant role in assisting Rama and Lakshmana and participates in numerous battles.[1-30]

In the Ramayana, Sugriva is a central figure who becomes an ally to Rama after being exiled by his brother Vālī. His story includes forming alliances, leading his army, engaging in battles against Ravana and his allies, and eventually helping Rama rescue Sita.[1-80]

### Conclusion
Sugriva’s multifaceted role as a divine figure, warrior, and ally to Rama makes him an important character in Hindu mythology. His story spans various texts, emphasizing his contributions to the epic battles of ancient India.
