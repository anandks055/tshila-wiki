  SOURCE 3 — Concise Encyclopedia of Hinduism by Swami Harshananda, (extracted 2024), hindupedia.org:
  Varṇa refers to the social divisions in hindū society that have existed for thousands of years, denoting the fourfold division of ancient bhāratīya society into distinct social classes based on occupation and duties [4][5][6]. These divisions became rigid and hereditary over time, leading to the creation of various castes and subcastes through intermixing, including the Niṣāda caste [7]. Each varṇa has specific duties (svadharma) one is bound to perform according to their social standing, and specific bathing rituals, such as brāhmaṇa householders bathing twice daily [6][5]. The concept of varṇasaṅkara denotes the mixing or intermarriage between different varnas, while sāmanya dharmas apply universally beyond these divisions [8][13]. Varṇa also refers to the letters of the saṃskṛta alphabet, totaling 63 or 64 when considering different intonations (udātta, anudātta, svarita) [9]. In some contexts, the leaders of the baṅgāla School of vaiṣṇava dharma disregarded the varṇa system in favor of cultivating primary moral and social values [10]. Individuals within the varṇa system can transcend their social divisions after realizing the Self, as described in texts like the Avadhuta Upaniṣad and Devīgītā [11][12]. Opportunities for elevation within the varṇa system exist through adherence to dharma, impacting prescribed duties and practices in conjunction with āśrama and ācāra [14][15].
