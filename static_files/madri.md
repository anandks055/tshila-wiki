## Overview of Mādrī

Mādrī is a prominent figure in Hindu mythology, known for her roles in the epic narratives such as the *Mahabharata* and various Puranas. She appears as the second wife of Pandu, with whom she has twin sons named Nakula and Sahadeva, conceived through divine intervention by the Aśvins [1][2][3]. Mādrī also plays a significant role in other mythological contexts where she is mentioned as one of Krishna's wives or in various family lineages.

### Family Lineage

Mādrī is identified as Pandu's second wife, securing her position after Bhishma arranged the marriage [4]. She is also the sister of Śalya and is a notable character in the *Harivamsha Purana* where she appears as one of Krausthu’s wives who bore Yudhajit and Devamidusha [5][6]. Her lineage further extends to her role as Pandu's wife destined to conceive portions of Dharma [7], and later as a mother to Nakula and Sahadeva, who are renowned for their valor and skills in combat [8-12].

### Mythological Roles

In the *Vishnu Purana*, Mādrī is referred to both as Pandu's wife and one of Krishna’s wives. She gives birth to twins through divine intervention by Nāsatya and Dasra, respectively [13][14]. In the *Brahma Purana*, she holds multiple roles: she is initially identified as Kroṣṭṛ’s wife, giving birth to Yudhājit and Devamīḍhuṣa [15], later also mentioned as one of Krishna's wives bearing Vrika among others [16][17].

### Literary References

In the *Kathasaritsagara*, Mādrī is a significant character appearing in various contexts. She is mentioned as Pandu’s wife by Nārada and in another narrative, she appears as the wife of Tārāvaloka and mother to twin sons Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa [18][19].

### Death and Funeral

Mādrī resists following Pandu onto his funeral pyre but ultimately fulfills her promise and dies alongside him. Her body is carefully wrapped and honored in the funeral rites, highlighting her reverence within the family lineage [20][21].

## Historical Context

The name "Madri" also refers to a village in Jewargi Tehsil, Gulbarga District, Karnataka, India, located 40 km south of Gulbarga. The village is known for its temples and educational institutions such as primary and high schools [22]. Other places named Madri exist across various states including Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, each with their own unique features like temples, schools, and nearby cities.
