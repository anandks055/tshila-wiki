## Bakāsura: Overview and Mythological Context

Bakāsura is a demon figure found in Hindu mythology, primarily known for his role as an antagonist who terrorized the residents of Ekacakranagara. According to the narrative, he attacked nearby villages, indiscriminately consuming people until he was slain by Bhīma at the behest of Kuntī [1]. This account highlights Bakāsura's portrayal as a malevolent entity causing havoc and suffering for local inhabitants.

In another tradition, Bakasura is associated with Lord Krishna. Here, his death led to the establishment of the Maheshwar Nath Temple in the region where he resided [1]. Moreover, an idol of Bakasura has been installed at Tirukkuvalai (Tirukkolili), alongside Bhima, symbolizing both their roles and the events surrounding Bakāsura's demise [2].

Additionally, Bakasura is also identified as a demon slain by Bhīmasena in the forest near Pāṭaṇ. This specific location is linked to spiritual practices such as Tapasana performed by King Surath and Samadhi Vaishyaka, which are mentioned in the Durga Saptashati [1].

## Spiritual Significance

The site where Bakasura was defeated holds significant religious importance. It is recognized for its connection with the establishment of a temple dedicated to Lord Maheshwar Nath and as a sacred place linked to spiritual practices and mythological narratives.
