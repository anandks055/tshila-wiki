## Rukmini: Divine Consort and Principal Wife of Krishna

Rukmini, also known as Rakhumāyi, is a prominent figure in Hindu mythology, primarily recognized as the principal wife of Lord Krishna. She is an incarnation of Lakshmi, the divine consort of Vishnu, who accompanies him during his incarnations on earth [1][2].

### Background and Marriage

Rukmini was the daughter of King Bhishmaka of Vidarbha and sister to Rukmi [1][3][4]. Despite her brother's opposition, she expressed a strong desire to marry Krishna. She sent a secret message through a trusted Brahmin to convey this wish, leading Krishna to abduct her and wed her in an unconventional rākṣasa-vivāha ceremony, which involves the abduction of the bride [1][2].

### Family and Children

Rukmini is the mother of Pradyumna, who is considered the rebirth of Kāma (also known as Manmatha) [1][3]. Additionally, she had a daughter named Cārumatī [1]. The Harivamsha Purana elaborates that Rukmini plays an influential role in Krishna's life and is celebrated for her unmatched beauty and celestial vision. She also has significant involvement in household affairs and major events such as the construction of Dwaraka [5].

### Roles and Significance

Rukmini holds a significant place within the Yadava lineage, particularly as she is mentioned in various texts detailing her role and contributions to Krishna's life and deeds [6]. In the Mahabharata, she is described entering Krishna’s apartments upon his return to Dwaraka and is noted for her spiritual journey and maternal duties towards Pradyumna [1][3].

### Devotion and Self-Sacrifice

Rukmini demonstrates profound devotion to Krishna throughout her life. According to sources like the Vishnu Purana, she initially mistakes Pradyumna for Krishna but later recognizes him as her son [7]. At the end of their lives, Rukmini exhibits ultimate devotion by embracing Krishna's body and immolating herself in a funeral pyre following his demise [1][2][8].

### Iconography and Cultural Depictions

In religious iconography, Rukmini is often depicted with an oval-shaped bun hairstyle called Keśabandha. She is also mentioned in Madhvācārya's Dvādaśastotra for her devotion to the image of Śrī Kṛṣṇa found in Uḍupi [2].
