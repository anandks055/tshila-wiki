## Bhishma

Bhishma, also known as Bhiṣma, was the grandsire of both the Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas. He was born to King Śantanu and the goddess Gaṅgā, making him a significant figure in Hindu lore [1][2]. Bhishma played a crucial role in advising Dhṛtarāṣṭra on matters related to governance and took charge of the Pāṇḍavas' upbringing after their father, Pāṇḍu, died. He is noted for his vow of celibacy and unmatched bravery [3].

Bhishma served as a mentor and advisor to both the Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas, delivering teachings from his bed of arrows to Yudhiṣṭhira in the Śāntiparva section of the Mahabharata. He also taught various texts such as the Hārītagītā, Viṣṇusahasranāma, Vāmadevagītā, Yamagitā, Śampākagītā, and Utathyagitā to Yudhiṣṭhira [4][5].

Bhishma's prowess was praised even by Paraśurāma; he fought with Paraśurāma but could not be defeated by him. He is also mentioned in the Devibhāgavata as a significant figure, and his role in restoring Yudhiṣṭhira’s rights and dividing the kingdom between the Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas is well-documented [6][7].

During the Mahabharata war, Bhishma was the chief commander of the Kaurava army. He joined Duryodhana's forces and commanded the army for ten days before being mortally wounded. While on his deathbed, he welcomed sages and royal guests who visited him. He advised Yudhiṣṭhira that the Pāṇḍavas would be safe under Krishna’s guidance [8].

Bhishma is also celebrated in the Mahabharata epic for his teachings on dharma and mokṣa (liberation). His discourse was completed when it was uttarāyana, after which he cast off his mortal coil with his mind fixed on Kṛṣṇa Vāsudeva [9].

In addition to these roles, Bhishma visited a hermitage where he was entertained by its inhabitants. The term "Bhishma" also refers to a section of the Mahabharata that includes details about the Kurukṣetra war and the Kariigas [10][11].
