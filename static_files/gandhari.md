## Introduction

Gāndhārī is a prominent character in Hindu mythology, particularly within the Mahābhārata epic. She was the eldest daughter of King Subala of Gāndhāra and became the chief queen of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, the blind prince of Hastināpura [1][2]. As personified by numerous virtues including wifely devotion, she voluntarily bound her eyes to share in her husband's blindness [3].

## Family Background

Gāndhārī was born into a royal family and had thirteen brothers and ten sisters. Among them, Śakuni, known for his cunning role during the Kurukṣetra war, was her elder brother [1][2]. All of Gāndhārī's sisters also married Dhṛtarāṣṭra, making her part of an extended royal family.

## Marriage and Children

Gāndhārī’s marriage to Dhṛtarāṣṭra was a significant alliance. With the grace of Śiva, she secured a boon for one hundred sons [1][2]. She gave birth to one hundred sons and one daughter after a delayed delivery, which was managed with proper care by Vyāsa [3].

## Role in Conflict

Despite her efforts to counsel her sons, particularly Duryodhana, Gāndhārī could not prevent the animosity between her children and the Pāṇḍavas. This conflict ultimately led to the Kurukṣetra war [2][6]. During the war, she refused to bless Duryodhana for victory but was consoled by Kṛṣṇa and Yudhiṣṭhira following her profound grief over the loss of her sons [4][5].

## Post-War Life

After the Kurukṣetra war, Gāndhārī accompanied Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Kuntī into exile in the forest. She was taken care of by Yudhiṣṭira until she eventually perished in a forest fire along with Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Kuntī [1][7].

## Symbolism and Cultural References

In some texts, Gāndhārī is also mentioned as one of Kroṣṭṛ’s wives and the mother of Anamitra [5], and she holds roles in various contexts within Hindu scriptures such as being among Krishna's sixteen thousand wives or a goddess representing energy [8].

## Modern Context

Gandhari village, located in Lonar Tehsil, Buldhana District, Maharashtra, India, is named after her. The village is approximately 19 kilometers from Lonar and 100 kilometers south of Buldhana and falls within the Vidarbha region and Buldana Division [9].
