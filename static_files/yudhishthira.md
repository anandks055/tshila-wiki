## Yudhishthira: The Just and Righteous Leader of the Pandavas

Yudhishthira, the eldest son of Kunti and Pandu, is renowned for his unwavering commitment to dharma (righteousness) and wisdom. Born with the blessings of Dharma, he was destined to become a just ruler and lead the Pandavas through numerous trials and battles. He earned titles such as Dharmarāja ("king of dharma") and Ajātaśatru ("one who had no enemies"), underscoring his exceptional qualities of righteousness and equanimity [1][2].

### Early Life and Education
Yudhishthira was educated alongside his four younger brothers by Kṛpācārya and Droṇācārya under the tutelage of Bhīṣma, the grandsire. After the death of their father Pāṇḍu, Yudhishthira was crowned as yuvarāja (crown prince), a decision that ignited jealousy and hatred from his cousin Duryodhana [1][2].

### Exile and Return
Yudhishthira and his brothers were tricked by Duryodhana into a game of dice, which resulted in their exile to the forest for twelve years followed by one year incognito. Upon returning, Yudhishthira staked his claim for the throne but was denied by Duryodhana, leading to the Kurukṣetra war [1][2].

### Leadership and Battles
During the Kurukṣetra war, Yudhishthira fought alongside his brothers and with the assistance of Kṛṣṇa. He demonstrated remarkable leadership and wisdom throughout the conflict, making strategic decisions that led to victory over the Kauravas [35][12]. Following the war, he ruled benevolently for thirty-six years, performing significant rituals such as the Rājasūya sacrifice and three Aśvamedha sacrifices [1][2].

### Spiritual Guidance
Yudhishthira sought spiritual guidance from various sages including Bhīṣma, Vyāsa, Pippalāda, Mārkaṇḍeya, and Nārada. He was instructed on the Viṣṇusahasranāma and other sacred teachings [34][25][29]. His deep spiritual understanding is also evident in his participation in significant rituals like Sraddhas and his interactions with sages such as Bhīṣma regarding moral and ethical questions [16][10].

### Post-War Reign
After the war, Yudhishthira took care of Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī. He performed the Aśvamedha sacrifice and was advised by Vidura to be given the throne due to his rightful claim and virtue [36][27]. His rule was characterized by prosperity, with abundant rains and crops, and an absence of diseases and difficulties [1].

### Transition to Heaven
Yudhishthira prepared to leave for heaven after installing his grandson Parīkṣit on the throne. He embarked on a journey clad in bark and fasting, reaching heaven through friendship and adherence to dharma [3][4][2].
