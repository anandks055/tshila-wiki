## Janamejaya: A Ruler and Figure in Hindu Epics

Janamejaya, son of Parīkṣit and grandson of Arjuna, is a significant figure in ancient Hindu texts. He was a king from the lunar race who performed several important sacrifices, including the Sarpayāga (serpent sacrifice) to avenge his father's death at the hands of Takṣaka [5, 6, 7, 10, 14]. Janamejaya also conducted the Aśvamedha sacrifice as advised by Śaunaka (also known as Indrota) [15], and he underwent the Mahābhiṣeka ceremony to signify his royal inauguration [8]. Additionally, he sponsored the recitation of the Mahābhārata during a snake sacrifice, which helped preserve the epic [9].

### Genealogy and Family
According to some sources, Janamejaya is one of Parīkṣit's four sons through Bhadravatī [11]. He is mentioned in various texts as having multiple children: Candrapida, Suryapida, Pramati, and Sumantri. In the Garuda Purana, he is described as the father of Sumantri who ruled in the city of Vishala [4], and also identified as the son of Puru with a detailed genealogical lineage extending through many generations [2].

### Rituals and Sacrifices
Janamejaya performed several significant sacrifices, including the Sarpayāga until it was halted by Jaratkāru [7]. He received teachings on the Devibhāgavata from ṛṣi Vyāsa to mitigate the sins from his actions [3] and worshipped the Guruvāyur deity to cure leprosy [4].

### Historical and Mythological Context
Janamejaya frequently engaged with Vaishampayana and other sages to seek detailed accounts of various historical and mythological events. He inquired about the history of the Pandavas and Vrishnis, origins of divine beings, and stories of Vishnu's incarnations and Krishna’s deeds [16-33]. He also asked about future conditions during Kali-yuga and the protection of humanity during times of moral decay [67-71].

### Literary Appearances
In the Brahmanda Purana, Janamejaya is portrayed as both a king and the father of Pramati. Additionally, he performs a horse-sacrifice reaching the final stage known as Avabhṛtha before his death [2]. In the Vishnu Purana, he is identified as the son of Purañjaya and grandfather of Mahāmani [1], and listed among Parīkṣit’s four sons [3].

### Contemporary References
The name or title "Janamejaya" was used by King Mahabhavagupta in his copperplate inscriptions from Jambugrāma and Kālibhāna, as well as mentioned in the Sonepur Plates [1][2][3].
