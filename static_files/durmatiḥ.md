## Durmatiḥ

**Durmatiḥ** is a term with multiple interpretations within Indic knowledge traditions [1][2]. Primarily, it refers to foolishness and a muddled intellect [1].

### Etymological Contexts:
- **Personification**: In some contexts, durmatiḥ denotes one of the sons of Dhritarashtra, indicating an individual characterized by misguided thinking or folly [2].
- **Time Period**: Durmatiḥ is also used to denote a specific time period associated with famine and scarcity. Specifically, it refers to the fifty-fifth year in a cycle where natural calamities like droughts are expected [2]. The text from Bhavishya Purana mentions that during this year, normal transactions do not occur as usual due to adverse conditions: "Durmatau samupasthite vyaharo na vartate" (भवेद् वै मध्यमा वृष्टिर्दुमतौ समुपस्थिते’). Additionally, it is noted that rainfall during this period is meager [2].

### Descriptive Attributes:
- **Moral and Intellectual Folly**: Durmatiḥ also signifies a person with a wicked mind or a muddled intellect. This attribute is highlighted in the phrase "Durmatih" (दुर्मतिः), which describes someone who rejects wholesome food and resorts to begging, indicating a lack of wisdom [2].

### Linguistic Variants:
- **Gender Variations**: The term extends to feminine forms as well, such as "Durmataa" (दुष्टा मतिः) and "Durmatiḥ" in its masculine form. Both denote someone with an evil or misguided intellect [2].
- **Adjectival Usage**: As a descriptive adjective, durmatiḥ can be applied to individuals who exhibit wicked thoughts or actions: "Durmatih yasya" (दुष्टा मतिः यस्य), which means someone with an evil mind [2].
