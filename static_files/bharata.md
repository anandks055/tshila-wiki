## Introduction to Bharata

The name "Bharata" is associated with numerous figures and concepts in Hindu mythology, literature, and historical contexts. This article explores various incarnations and significances attributed to Bharata across different texts.

### Emperor Bharata

One of the earliest references to Bharata is as an emperor who ruled a vast territory known as 'Ajanābhavarṣa' or 'Bharatavarṣa'. The country that he governed came to be referred to by this name, which continues to be used for India today. This Bharata was the son of Ṛṣbha and Jayantī [1][3].

### Bharata Son of Duṣyanta and Śakuntalā

Another notable figure named Bharata was the son of King Duṣyanta and Queen Śakuntalā, also known as Sarvadamana. The Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas are believed to be his descendants. Some scholars suggest that this Bharata is the source of the country's name 'Bharatavarṣa' [1][3].

### Bharata in Rāmāyaṇa

The most widely recognized Bharata is the younger brother of Rāma from the epic *Rāmāyaṇa*. He was born to King Daśaratha and Queen Kaikeyī. When Kaikeyī compelled Daśaratha to exile Rāma for 14 years, Bharata refused to accept the kingdom and instead persuaded Rāma to return. Upon Rāma's insistence that he must abide by their father’s wishes, Bharata ruled on Rāma’s behalf from Nandigrāma, establishing Rāma's wooden sandals on Ayodhyā’s throne [1][2][3]. He demonstrated remarkable devotion and loyalty to his brother throughout this period.

### Author of the Nātyaśāstra

Bharata is also credited as the author of the *Nātyaśāstra*, a foundational treatise on dramaturgy. This work, comprising 6000 ślokas in 36 chapters according to Abhinavagupta's commentary, laid the groundwork for formal studies in alaṅkāra-śāstra and developed the theory of rasa [2][4].

### Bharata as a Clan

The Ṛgveda mentions Bharatas as a clan of people. Additionally, Bharata is listed among those who performed the mahābhiṣeka ritual in the Aitareya Brāhmana and is referenced in texts such as the Rāmarahasya Upaniṣad [1][3].

### Bharata in Jainism

In Jain tradition, Bharata is one of the first two Tīrthaṅkaras mentioned in the Ādipurāna. He also appears in the context of various Puranas and epics like the Garuda Purana and Shiva Purana [1][3].

### Bharata as a Geographical Entity

The term "Bharatavarṣa" or "Bhārata" refers to the land that Bharata ruled, which is synonymous with India. The term also denotes specific regions in geographical contexts like those described in the Garuda Purana [1][3].

### Conclusion

Bharata's multifaceted identity encompasses a rich tapestry of mythological and historical references, ranging from emperors to authors and clan leaders. His most enduring legacy is perhaps his embodiment of brotherly love and loyalty as seen in the epic *Rāmāyaṇa*.
