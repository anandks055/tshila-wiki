## Prayāga: Overview

Prayāga, now known as Allahabad in Uttar Pradesh, is one of the most significant pilgrimage sites in Hinduism. It is renowned for being the confluence of three rivers—Gaṅgā, Yamunā, and Sarasvatī (the latter mythical)—and has been celebrated since ancient times as a holy site where numerous religious rituals are performed.

### Historical Significance

Prayāga is believed to have been named after Brahmā's excellent sacrifice conducted at this location. According to Hindu texts like the Matsyapurāṇa, Prayāga remains undestroyed even during cosmic dissolution (pralaya) [1]. The Ṛgveda also confirms its ancient heritage [2], and the Rāmāyaṇa mentions a forest at this confluence [3].

### Religious Significance

The site is considered extremely auspicious for various religious activities, including bathing in the Triveṇī-saṅgama (the actual confluence of rivers), tonsure of the head, performing śrāddha to ancestors and dāna (gift-giving), and Veṇīpradāna (offering hair into the confluence) [4]. 

A bath in the Triveṇī-saṅgama during the month of Māgha is particularly auspicious. It also hosts the Kumbha-melā festival every twelve years, attracting millions of pilgrims and sādhus [5].

### Religious Suicide

Prayāga has historically been a place where individuals who have realized the Self or are suffering from incurable diseases perform religious suicide by jumping into the river from Akṣayavāta, an ancient Banyan tree on its bank [6]. This practice is encouraged by both heretics and evil spirits, as noted by Hiuen Tsiang in his travel accounts [7].

### Other Attractions

The site also includes several other holy places such as Veṇīmādhava temple, Bharadvājāśrama, Nāgamandira or Vāsukimandira, and Akṣayavāṭa. The Citrakuṭa hill mentioned in the Rāmāyaṇa is situated about 100 kilometers from Prayāga [8].

### Cultural and Religious Associations

Prayāga is associated with significant religious events such as the birth of Rāmānanda, and it has been praised for its clay used to make gopicandana. It is also one of the four locations where Jayanta dropped the Amṛta Kumbha [9].

### Geographical Context

Prayāga marks the eastern boundary of Madhyadeśa and serves as the starting point of the antarvedi region, being located at the confluence of Gaṅgā and Yamunā. It is also part of the territory where the Kurus and Panchalas meet [10].
