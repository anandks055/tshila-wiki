## Etymology

The term "Bhāgavata" refers to various contexts and texts within Indic knowledge traditions. It is most prominently known as Śrīmad Bhāgavatam, a sacred text that serves as a primary scriptural authority for the Suddhādvaita school [1].

## Bhagavata Purana

The Bhāgavata Purana is considered a well-known Vaiṣṇava scripture that narrates the life and teachings of Lord Kṛṣṇa, promoting devotion to Viṣṇu [2]. It includes references to the Veda, such as in the 12.6.50 section [3], and is referenced in Jaina literature [4].

## Purana

The Bhāgavata is a sacred text first recited for Raja Parikshit and honored in stotras like Bhagawatha Vandanam [5][6]. It recounts the tale of Ajāmila, glorifying the power of devotion to Viṣṇu [7], and features carvings on the walls of the Angkor Vat temple [8].

### Stories and Teachings

- **Prahlāda**: The text details his devotion and teachings on the nine modes of devotion [9].
- **Gajendra-mokṣa**: It narrates Gajendra's 31 verses of prayer [10].
- **Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Syamantaka Gem**: The story of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and the syamantaka gem is included [11].

## Historical References

The Bhāgavata Purana discusses various aspects of Indian history and mythology, including:

- **Origin of Pancala**: It explains the origin of the name Pancala [12].
- **Descent of the Ganges**: It refers to the descent of the Ganges [13].
- **Manu’s Austerities**: The text mentions Manu performing austerities at Akolha's hermitage [14].
- **Ugrasena and Kukuras**: It identifies Ugrasena from the Kukuras [15].
- **Mahiṣmati**: Refers to Mahiṣmati as a city of the Haihayas [16][17].

## Alternative Names

The term "Bhāgavata" can also refer to:

- Bhagabhadra, a Sunga prince who ruled Vidiśā and received the column from Heliodoros [18].
- An alternative name for the Brahadeesvara temple, known for its impressive architecture and large Shiva lingam [19][20].
- An ancient name for the region known as Vanavasi mentioned in the Periplus [21].

## Composition

The Bhāgavata Purana is composed by Sage Vedavyasa and recited by Śukabrahmarṣi to King Parīkṣit, serving as a treatise on the science of bhakti and devotion to the Lord [22]. It embodies Lord Kṛṣṇa in scriptural form and is considered the epitome of spiritual wisdom, akin to the Ganges being the body of Lord Śiva [23].

### Narratives

- **Varāhasvāmi**: The text narrates stories of Lord Viṣṇu's incarnations, including Varāhasvāmi [24].
- **Kapilamaharṣi**: It provides teachings on sāṅkhya-yoga darśana and the story of Kapila’s incarnation and philosophy, explained in a dialogue between Vidura and Maitreya [25].

## Dharmic Teachings

The Bhāgavata Dharma emphasizes devotion to Vishnu and worship of gods and ancestors leading to heavenly realms [26]. The text is known for its role as a sacred narrative about the Supreme Lord, requested by Śaunaka [27], and contains stories and teachings about Hari and God [28].

### A Sacred Text

- **Purifies Sinners**: It purifies sinners and brings salvation in the Kali age [29].
- **Bhāgavata-week**: Focuses on spiritual enlightenment and liberation during the Bhāgavata-week [30].
- **Highest Knowledge**: The text imparts the highest knowledge from Kṛṣṇa to Brahmā at the beginning of creation [31].

## References

### Dynastic Reference
Bhagavata is also a son of Vajramitra and father of Devabhūti, ruling for 32 years [32]. He belongs to the Kaṇva dynasty [33].
