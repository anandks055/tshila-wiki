## Daśaratha: King and Father of Rāma

Daśaratha was a prominent king in ancient Indian mythology, best known as the father of Rāma, a revered figure who is considered an incarnation of Lord Viṣṇu. He belonged to the Ikṣvākuvamśa or Suryavaṅśa dynasty and was the ruler of Kosal with its capital at Ayodhyā.

### Family and Lineage

Daśaratha was the son of King Aja and had three queens: Kauśalyā, Sumitrā, and Kaikeyī. He performed a Putrakāmeṣṭi sacrifice under the guidance of sage Rṣyaśṛṅga to obtain worthy sons. Through this sacrifice, he was blessed with four sons:
- **Rāma** from Kausalyā
- **Bharata** from Kaikeyī
- **Lakṣmaṇa** and **Satrughna** from Sumitrā

He also had a daughter named Sāntādevī, who was adopted by King Romapāda.

### Administration and Allies

Daśaratha ruled with the assistance of eight distinguished ministers led by Sumantra. His kingdom allied with Janaka of Mithilā, and he conducted significant yajñas like the Aśvamedha-yāga on the banks of the Sarayū river, involving many Brahmins.

### Personal Traits and Actions

Despite being depicted as a noble and righteous ruler in the Rāmāyaṇa, Daśaratha had an impulsive nature that led to his downfall. He was known for driving his chariot skillfully in ten directions, signifying his valor and prowess. His close relationships included friendships with Romapāda and Guhā, the chieftain of forest tribes.

### Significant Events

Daśaratha initially refused but later agreed to send Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa with sage Viśvāmitra to guard a sacrifice against demonic attacks. After Rāma's victory in Mithilā where he married Sītā, Daśaratha planned to enthrone Rāma as the crown prince.

However, Kaikeyī demanded two boons: making Bharata king and exiling Rāma for fourteen years. Unable to change her mind, Daśaratha passed away after fulfilling these demands. His death was also foretold through a curse by a sage-couple whose son he had mistakenly killed during his youth.
