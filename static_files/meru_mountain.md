Mount Meru is a significant and multifaceted mountain in Indian literature and mythology. Revered as the sacred site where the embryo of Kārttikeya was placed and guarded by Śiva's attendants, Mount Meru appears throughout various texts as both a geographical and metaphysical landmark [ref1]. It serves not only as a benchmark for grandeur but also as an essential element in cosmological frameworks, symbolizing the center of the universe surrounded by continents like Jambudvipa (India) [ref3]. In Indian cultural narratives, Mount Meru is often used to illustrate magnificence and spiritual significance, such as its depiction in the Ramayana where it serves as a metaphor for the royal palace approached by Shri Rama [ref6], further emphasizing its profound importance.

## Sacred Geography and Mythological Significance

Mount Meru holds an essential place within Indian sacred geography. According to Dhara Summary, Mount Meru is a central mountain that is not only the starting point of the Ganges' journey before it divides into four streams but also a mythical peak referenced in various ancient texts [ref2]. The mountain's significance extends beyond geographical symbolism; it is often associated with spiritual and cosmological narratives. For instance, Kathasaritsagara mentions Mount Meru as the site where Kārttikeya’s embryo was placed and guarded by Śiva’s attendants, highlighting its role in divine narratives [ref1]. In addition to this, Mount Meru serves as a benchmark for illustrating magnificence; Sundarapura is often described with reference to Mount Meru's grandeur [ref2].

The mountain’s symbolic importance extends beyond the physical realm into the spiritual and metaphysical. In Hindu cosmology, Mount Meru represents the center of the universe surrounded by continents such as Jambudvipa (India), Uttara Kuru (Chinese Turkestan), and others, indicating its central role in ancient Indian texts' geographical knowledge [ref3]. This centrality is further reinforced by its depiction in various myths where it serves not only as a physical landmark but also as the dwelling place of gods and a site for spiritual quests.

## Mount Meru in Literary Texts

Mount Meru's presence in Indian literature spans a wide range of texts, including the Ramayana, Kathasaritsagara, and Yoga Vasistha. In the Ramayana, Mount Meru is used as a symbol to describe the grandeur and importance of various elements within the narrative [ref6]. For example, in Ayodhya-kanda, Chapter 33, it is likened to the royal palace that Shri Rama approaches, emphasizing its majesty. Similarly, in Bala Kanda, Chapter 40, Meru mountain serves as a metaphor to describe Sita's constant companionship with Shri Rama [ref6]. The Ramayana also uses Mount Meru as a metaphor for the inlaid walls of Shri Rama’s palace, highlighting the beauty and craftsmanship of the structure [ref6].

In Yoga Vasistha, Mount Meru is described through various characters' experiences. Bhusunda, a long-lived bird character, narrates his residence on the Himalayas and Malaya Mountain before finally finding his last abode on Mount Meru [ref4]. This narrative emphasizes the mountain’s importance as not just a physical peak but also a spiritual destination. In Yoga Vasistha's Book VI - Nirvana prakarana part 1 (nirvana prakarana), Chapter XIV, Mount Meru is described in vivid detail, appearing as if formed by all kinds of colors on earth, giving it the appearance of a variegated sky at sunset [ref5].
