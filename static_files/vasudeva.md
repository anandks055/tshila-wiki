## Vasudeva: Overview

Vasudeva holds significant importance in Hindu mythology as a name for Viṣṇu signifying His universal presence and omnipotence [1]. He is also known as the father of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, husband to Devakī and Rohiṇī, and played crucial roles in ensuring the safety of Kṛṣṇa from threats posed by Kañsa [2][7][12]. Vasudeva's role extends beyond mythology; he is revered through stotras like vāsudeva Sthuthi and invoked as one of the four aspects of Chatur-vyūha, representing the creator aspect of Viṣṇu [1][13].

## Mythological Significance

In various texts such as the Shiva Purana and Mahabharata, Vasudeva is depicted as Kṛṣṇa's father. The Shiva Purana mentions his role in protecting the virtuous and alleviating Earth’s burden through the birth of Kṛṣṇa [1][2][3]. In the Mahabharata, Vasudeva (also known as Krishna) plays a central role, deeply involved in pivotal events such as the marriage of Subhadra to Arjuna and helping Agni consume the Khandava forest [4][5][6].

## Religious Practices

Vasudeva is worshiped through specific chants like "OM VASUDEVA AASANAY NAMAH," according to the Garuda Purana's Vishnu Mantra, emphasizing his roles in creation, protection, and destruction [2]. He is also recognized as a significant figure in the Brahmanda Purana, being one of the five heroes of the Vṛṣṇis race and the chief protector of Dvārakā [3][4].

## Historical and Cultural Presence

The village named Vasudeva is located in the Sohanv tehsil of Ballia district, Uttar Pradesh, India. It is known for its temples such as Jay Babu Ray Bramha Baba Mandir and Siv Mandir Chaura, along with educational institutions like SICE Computer Education Laxmanpur [9].

## Conclusion

Vasudeva’s significance spans across religious texts and cultural practices, making him a prominent figure in Hindu mythology and history. His roles encompass divine protection, familial duty, and spiritual reverence, highlighting his multifaceted importance.
