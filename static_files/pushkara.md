## Pushkara: Overview and Significance

Pushkara, also known as Pokṣara or Pokhrá, is a town located approximately 12 kilometers west of Ajmer in Rajasthan, India. It is renowned for its sacred lake and the unique Brahmā temple dedicated to the creator god Brahmā, which is the only one of its kind in India. The town's religious significance has been extolled in various purāṇas and epics.[1][2]

### Mythological Background

According to mythological legends, Pushkara was formed when Brahmā killed a demon named Vajranābha with a lotus that he converted into a weapon through sacred chants. The lotus rebounded at three locations before striking the demon, creating three lakes: Jyeṣṭa-puṣkara, Madhya-puṣkara, and Kaniṣṭha-puṣkara. These are presided over by Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Rudra respectively.[3]

### Religious Significance

Pushkara is a significant pilgrimage site in Hinduism, with the primary festival held on Kārttika Purṇimā day, generally in November. Pilgrims gather to take a bath in the main lake and worship Brahmā.[4] The Brahmā temple houses an image of the four-faced deity along with his two spouses, Sāvitrī and Gāyatri. Additionally, there are images of the four sages: Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanatkumāra, and Sanatsujāta.[5]

### Other Temples and Sacred Sites

The town also features temples dedicated to Nārada, Badarī-Nārāyaṇa, Varāha, Śiva (also known as Ātmeśvara-Mahādeva), Ramā-Vaikuṇṭha or Śri Raṅgaji, Venugopāla, and Sri Rāma. Other important pilgrimage sites include Yajñaparvata, the hermitage of Agastya, and five kuṇḍas (small ponds). The most well-known is Nāgakuṇda. Parikramā, or circumambulating the whole area, is considered a holy act.[6]

### Additional Meanings

Pushkara has multiple significances in Hindu mythology and tradition:
- It refers to Varuṇa's son who marries Jyotsnakali.
- It denotes one of the pairs of dvarapalas stationed at the outside enclosure in Vaiṣṇava temples.
- It signifies a festival day related to the Gaṅgā nadi and a period every 12 years when bathing in Godāvarī is considered extremely holy.[7]
- In mythological contexts, it represents one of the seven dvīpas arranged around Jambudvīpa.

### Historical Context

Historically, Pushkara has been noted as a location seven miles north of Ajmer in Rajputana and is considered very sacred by Hindus. It contains a sacred tank believed to cleanse even the most sinful souls.[8]
