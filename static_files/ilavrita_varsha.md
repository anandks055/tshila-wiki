Ilāvrita Varṣa is an ancient and significant region in the geography of Hindu cosmology and mythology. It is considered one of the nine divisions or varṣas of Bhārata-varṣa, which encompasses the Indian subcontinent. The concept of Ilāvrita Varṣa is deeply embedded within various sacred texts including the Puranas and other ancient scriptures that detail its mystical significance. Historically, Ilāvrita was also associated with the Ailas, a notable group in traditional Indian history [ref1]. This region serves as an essential component in understanding not only the geographical but also the spiritual landscape of ancient India.

## Historical Significance

According to Hindu Civilization by Radha Kumud Mookerji, Ilāvrita is historically significant for being the original abode of the Ailas. The Ailas were a prominent group within early Indian society and played crucial roles in various historical narratives [ref1]. This connection underscores the importance of Ilāvrita not just as a geographical entity but also as a cultural hub that provided a foundation for numerous traditional Indian tales.

In more recent times, the region has been referenced within broader contexts like the political and legal institutions of ancient India. The History and Culture of the Indian People discusses the role of Kātyāyana in relation to ritual texts such as the Srauta-sūtras, which mention the Nishāda-sthapati [ref2]. This indicates that Ilāvrita was not only a geographical location but also a space where significant rituals and legal practices were established.

The significance of Ilāvrita Varṣa is further highlighted in texts such as the Devi Bhagavata Purana, which provides detailed descriptions of this region. According to Maharṣi Veda Vyasa’s Mahāpurāṇam Śrī Mad Devī Bhāgavatam, Ilāvrīta contains numerous sacred sites and spiritual traditions that are integral to Hindu mythology [ref4]. The text describes Ilāvrita as a place of profound religious significance where various mythological events took place.

## Mythological Context

Ilāvrita Varṣa features prominently in several mythological texts, indicating its deep-rooted importance within the cosmology and spiritual beliefs of ancient India. In the Bhagavata Purana, Ilāvrita is described as part of a larger geographical division of Bhārata-varṣa into nine distinct regions [ref17]. This classification underscores the holistic view of the Indian subcontinent in Hindu mythology.

The Yoga Vasistha also mentions Ilavrita-Varsha within its narrative context. It describes the region during Vasishtha's aerial journey, implying a place with rich mythological significance and mystical properties. The text’s portrayal of Ilāvrita highlights its role as an essential component of ancient Indian geography and mythology [ref8].

## Geographical Descriptions

Geographically, Ilāvrita Varṣa is described as a central region within the larger territory of Bhārata-varṣa. In texts such as the Devi Bhagavata Purana, it is detailed that Ilāvrīta is located at the center of the earth and surrounded by various mountains and rivers [ref4]. This description reflects an intricate understanding of the subcontinent’s geography, which was crucial for ancient Indian society in terms of navigation and resource management.

The region also played a role in cultural integration and unity. India as a whole has been seen as a single unified nation with natural boundaries like seas and mountains, emphasizing its geographical coherence [ref18]. The depiction of Ilāvrita within this broader context highlights how the subcontinent was viewed not just as a collection of regions but as a cohesive entity.

The Puranas further elaborate on the geographical details of Ilāvrita Varṣa. For instance, it is described in Book 8 of the Devi Bhagavata Purana that Ilāvrīta contains numerous sacred sites and religious practices [ref4]. This detailed description reflects the spiritual importance placed on specific locations within the region.

The religious significance of Ilāvrita Varṣa cannot be overstated. Ilāvrita Varṣa is also mentioned in relation to other sacred rivers like Sarasvatī, indicating its interconnection within the broader framework of Hindu mythology. The Sarasvatī river is frequently referred to in the Rigveda and has played an important role in Indian religious traditions [ref9].
