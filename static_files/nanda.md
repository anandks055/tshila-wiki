## Overview

Nanda is a multifaceted term in Hindu mythology, literature, and geography. It refers to various entities including individuals, rivers, dynasties, and geographical locations. In mythological contexts, Nanda often denotes the foster father of Krishna, while geographically, it signifies several villages across India.

## Mythology

### Foster Father of Krishna
Nanda is prominently known as the chieftain of cowherds in Gokula [8] and the foster father of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma [2][3]. He was an intimate friend of Vasudeva, Kṛṣṇa’s biological father. In a previous life, Nanda and his wife Yaśodā were Vasus named Droṇa and Dharā who performed severe austerities to have God as their son [2].

### Devotional Aspect
Nanda is also revered as an untouchable saint devoted to Viṣṇu [10], noted for his devotion and recognized in the Nārada Bhaktisutras. He celebrated Śrī Kṛṣṇa's birth with great joy on Srīkrsna-jayantī [8] and accepted Kṛṣṇa’s suggestion to worship Govardhana instead of Indra [7].

### Historical Figures
Historically, Nanda refers to various figures including a king who constructed a canal connecting Khibia to a nearby river [3], dynasties ruling over regions like Kuntala [4] and succeeding the Sisunagas with Pataliputra as their capital [5]. Additionally, there is mention of a family presumed to have ruled over a region in Orissa [6].

## Literary References

### Garuda Purana
In the Garuda Purana, Nanda has multiple significances. He appears as one of the qualities worshipped during Hayagriva's worship [1], listed among the eight Nidhis supporting their families and marrying multiple times [2]. Nanda is also mentioned as a king of the insular continent Plaksha and refers to days in a fortnight [4].

### Harivamsha Purana
In the Harivamsha Purana, Nanda is depicted as the foster father of Krishna, a milkman, and leader among Gopas (cowherds). He ensures Krishna's safety in Vraja [5], cares for his kine, and witnesses Krishna’s miraculous feats [6][7]. Nanda also advises villagers to move to Vrindavana for safety [9] and leads the community during various events [12].

### Mahabharata
In the Mahabharata, Nanda appears as a river in the Naimisha forest [1], a river flowing on Deva-vana mountain [2], and one of two rivers celebrated for its purifying qualities against sin [3]. He is associated with divine lineage and listed among Surasa's offspring, indicating his connection to the Naga clan [5].

## Geography

### Villages
Geographically, several locations named Nanda exist across India. Notable villages include one in Chandrapur district, Maharashtra, situated 33 kilometers south of Chandrapur near Rajura, Jiwati, and Ballarpur talukas. This village has schools like the Aditya Birla Public School and Hanuman Temples. Another Nanda is located in Hooghly district, West Bengal.
