## Indrajit

### Overview
Indrajit, also known as Meghanāda, was the eldest son of Rāvaṇa and his chief queen Maṇḍodarī. He is renowned for his formidable martial prowess in the epic narratives such as the *Rāmāyana* and the *Mahabharata*. The name "Meghanāda" translates to "one who roared like rain clouds," while "Indrajit" signifies "one who conquered Indra, the king of gods."

### Origins and Characteristics
According to legend, Indrajit was born with thunderous sounds resembling those of the rain-clouds, which led to his name Meghanāda. He is known for his ability to wield powerful weapons such as the Brahmāstra and Nāgapāśa (serpent-weapon). His victory over Indra earned him the title "Indrajit." 

### Role in Epic Narratives
In the *Rāmāyana*, Indrajit plays a significant role as one of Rāvaṇa’s most formidable enemies. He captures Hanumān using the Brahmāstra and later uses the Nāgapāśa to make Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa swoon, an act from which they are saved by Garuḍa (the eagle-mount of Viṣṇu). Indrajit is also known for his strategic use of illusionary tactics, such as creating a false Sītā and beheading her in front of Rāma's army to demoralize them.

In the *Mahabharata*, Indrajit engages in fierce combat with Lakṣmaṇa and uses celestial arrows and powers of illusion during his battles. The Skanda Purana describes him as a warrior who strikes Rama and Lakshmana unconscious using serpent-missiles, while the Agni Purana portrays him as a powerful demon whose binding of Rāma and others is ultimately thwarted by Garuḍa.

### Defeat and Death
Despite his formidable abilities, Indrajit was eventually killed by Lakṣmaṇa. According to the *Adhyātma Rāmāyana*, Indrajit had obtained a boon from Brahmā that only someone who observed strict celibacy for twelve years could kill him. Lakṣmaṇa fulfilled this condition and thus was able to defeat Indrajit.

### Symbolic Defeat
Indrajit's symbolic defeat is also depicted in Rāmalilā, a form of Hindu performance art based on the epic *Rāmāyana*. His death at the hands of Lakṣmaṇa significantly contributes to Rāvaṇa’s ultimate downfall.
