Jambudvipa is a term deeply embedded in ancient Indian cosmology and cultural heritage, referring to the earthly realm that encompasses the Indian subcontinent [ref1]. This geographical designation appears prominently across various religious texts, ranging from Buddhist scriptures to Hindu Puranas, often highlighting its spiritual significance and physical characteristics. In these narratives, Jambudvipa is frequently described as being encircled by oceans, divided into distinct regions or continents, and associated with notable rivers like the Jambu river [ref2]. Additionally, it features prominently in historical accounts of pilgrims and scholars who have documented its religious landmarks and socio-cultural practices. The term also appears in inscriptions dating back to the Mauryan period, where it denotes not only a geographical entity but also a political realm under Emperor Asoka's rule [ref3]. These multifaceted depictions underscore Jambudvipa’s central role in ancient Indian thought and its enduring relevance in contemporary scholarly discourse.

## Geographical and Cosmological Significance

Jambudvipa, as described in various ancient texts, is a significant geographical entity with both mythological and cosmological importance. In Hindu cosmology, it is considered the earthly realm where humans reside [ref4]. The term "Jambudvipa" itself translates to “the island of Jambu trees,” referring to a legendary fruit tree that was said to grow abundantly in this land. According to the Agni Purana, Jambudvipa stretches over a lakh of yojanas and is encircled by the Kṣīroda (ocean), which measures one lakh yojanas [ref13]. This vast continent is divided into nine subcontinents or varshas, each with its own unique characteristics and divine settlements. The central region, known as Ilāvṛta, houses Mount Meru, considered the axis of the universe in Hindu cosmology [ref9].

The Skanda Purana further elaborates on Jambudvipa’s geographical layout, emphasizing that it is located at the center of the earth and divided into nine distinct regions or varshas. Each varsha has its own features, such as rivers, mountains, and sacred sites, making Jambudvipa a comprehensive cosmological model [ref16]. This detailed description underscores the importance of Jambudvipa in ancient Indian religious thought, serving not only as a geographical entity but also as a spiritual and philosophical framework.

In the Bhagavata Purana, Jambudvipa is described as one of the seven dvīpas (continents) mentioned in the text, each encircled by specific seas. It is detailed that Jambudvipa is surrounded by the Salt Sea, emphasizing its unique geographical and spiritual significance [ref6]. Furthermore, it states that Agnīdhra ruled over this land as a king, protecting his subjects like his own children [ref15], thereby linking the concept of kingship with the spiritual well-being of the people.

## Historical References in Texts

Historical references to Jambudvipa appear frequently across various ancient Indian texts, providing insights into its cultural and geographical significance. In the Bhagavata Purana, for instance, it is described as a land ruled by Agnīdhra, who protected his subjects with great care [ref15]. This portrayal of leadership highlights the interconnectedness between governance and spiritual well-being in ancient Indian societies.

The Yoga Vasistha offers another perspective on Jambudvipa, referring to it both as a geographical location and a symbolic space for spiritual teachings. In this text, Jambudvipa is depicted as a great forest where Vasishtha teaches his son, symbolizing the convergence of nature and spirituality [ref5]. This dual interpretation underscores the multifaceted role that Jambudvipa played in ancient Indian thought, serving both as a physical space and a metaphorical ground for spiritual enlightenment.

In Buddhist cosmology, Jambudvipa is also considered one of the continents, though it specifically refers to the Indian subcontinent [ref12]. The text mentions significant religious structures such as the stūpa at Purushapura, indicating that this region was not only a geographical entity but also a spiritual hub for Buddhist practices. This dual significance as both a physical and spiritual location is further reinforced by its mention in inscriptions from the Mauryan period, where Jambudvipa denotes the realm ruled by Emperor Asoka [ref3].

The Ramayana provides another layer of historical context to Jambudvipa’s importance. It is mentioned as the region where King Sagara's sons searched for a sacrificial horse that had been stolen [ref10]. Additionally, it is identified in Kishkindha-kanda as the area where Sugriva instructed his monkeys to search for Sita [ref10], emphasizing its geographical extent and strategic importance.

## Spiritual and Mythological Narratives

Spiritual narratives surrounding Jambudvipa are abundant in ancient Indian texts, reflecting its deep religious significance. In Hindu cosmology, Jambudvipa is often associated with Mount Meru, considered the axis of the universe [ref14]. This connection places Jambudvipa at the center of a cosmic order, where spiritual and geographical realities intertwine. The Agni Purana describes Jambudvipa as divided into nine varshas or regions, each with its own unique characteristics and divine settlements [ref13].

The Shiva Purana provides additional details about the origin of the name "Jambudvipa." According to Sanatkumāra's account in this text, there are great trees called Jambū shining within this land, lending their name to the continent [ref7]. The presence of these trees and their significance in spiritual practices underscores the role of natural elements in Hindu cosmology.

Similarly, the Linga Purana describes Jambudvipa as a vast land containing sacred mountains like Meru, encircled by nine subcontinents inhabited by various divine settlements [ref14]. This portrayal highlights the continent's central role in religious narratives and its symbolic importance in spiritual practices. The text also notes that it was one of the continents where Dharma (righteousness) was practiced, emphasizing its moral and ethical significance.

In Buddhist texts, Jambudvipa is often described as a region where significant religious events took place. According to the Dhara Summary, Jambudvīpa is both the earthly realm according to Buddhist tradition [ref2] and specifically denotes the Indian subcontinent as described by Chinese pilgrims who noted significant religious structures such as the stūpa at Purushapura [ref2]. These narratives highlight not only the geographical but also the spiritual importance of Jambudvipa in ancient Indian thought.

Preservation efforts for ancient manuscripts and texts related to Jambudvipa are critical in maintaining our understanding of its historical and cultural significance. The Asiatic Society, founded by Sir William Jones in 1794, has been instrumental in promoting Indological studies and preserving knowledge about ancient India [ref20]. Established in 1917, it houses over 82,000 volumes of valuable books and continues to publish scholarly works on topics ranging from epic literature to historical dictionaries. This institute has been particularly notable for its critical edition of the Mahābhārata and other important publications.

Institutions such as these not only preserve ancient texts but also engage in ongoing research projects aimed at documenting and analyzing Jambudvipa’s multifaceted significance.
