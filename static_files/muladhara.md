Mūlādhāra is one of six centers of psychic energy in the human body known as chakras, situated at the base of the spine where the suṣumnā-nāḍī begins [ref1]. As the foundational chakra, it plays a crucial role in yogic and tantric practices and is associated with the mundane sphere of action and terrestrial activities. Mūlādhāra is described in various texts including the Yogacudāmam Upaniṣad and Dhyanaupaniṣad [ref1], where it represents the base from which the prāṇavāyu rises during meditation, and serves as a location for the Brahmagranthi [ref1]. In tantric practices, this chakra is often depicted as a four-petalled lotus, signifying its foundational role in supporting all other chakras and indicating its importance in mantra and yogic exercises [ref2].

The term Mūlādhāra literally translates to "root support" or the primary base from which higher spiritual energies emanate. In the Dhara Summary of the Devi Bhagavata Purana, it is described as a key component among six Cakras that are critical in yogic and mantra practices [ref2]. This foundational aspect underscores its significance not only in physical alignment but also in spiritual grounding, making Mūlādhāra essential for practitioners seeking to balance their energies and achieve higher states of consciousness. The chakra’s location between the genital organ and anus further emphasizes its role as a point of convergence for both mundane and spiritual forces within the body [ref4].

In addition to its physical and spiritual significance, Mūlādhāra is also associated with various mythological and historical contexts that highlight its profound influence in Indian heritage.
