## Duryodhana

Duryodhana was the eldest son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī [1]. He is a central figure in the Mahābhārata, known for his greed, jealousy, and arrogance, making him a key antagonist to the Pāṇḍavas. At the time of Duryodhana's birth, there were numerous ill-omens, prompting well-wishers of the Kuru-Pāṇḍava race to advise Dhṛtarāṣṭra to abandon this evil boy [1][2]. However, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's infatuation towards his son led him to ignore their wise counsels, ultimately leading to disastrous consequences.

Duryodhana and the Pāṇḍavas underwent training under Kṛpācārya and then Droṇācārya. The brilliance and remarkable progress of the Pāṇḍavas roused envy and hatred in Duryodhana, prompting him to devise numerous schemes to eliminate them [2][3]. He attempted to poison and set fire to a house of lac to burn the Pāṇḍavas but was thwarted by timely interventions from Vidura and other well-wishers.

Duryodhana contrived with Śakuni to invite Yudhiṣṭhira for gambling through a game of dice. The result was an utter debacle for the Pāṇḍavas, who lost everything [3][8]. In another incident, Duśśāsana tried to molest and disrobe Draupadī but her honor was saved by Śrī Kṛṣṇa's divine intervention [10].

Duryodhana is renowned for his skill in wielding the gadā (mace) and his expertise in gadā-yuddha (mace combat). He was cursed by Sage Kaṇva and Maitreya to die with broken thighs, and he faced Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s Viśvarupa form after rejecting peace [4][9][12][20]. Duryodhana also played a significant role in orchestrating the death of Abhimanyu and enlisting Śalya to fight against the Pāṇḍavas [4].

According to the Shiva Purana, Duryodhana is portrayed as a formidable and influential antagonist, among Arjuna's most significant adversaries alongside other notable figures such as Droṇa, Bhīṣma, and Karṇa [26][27][28]. He is also characterized as the orchestrator behind the demon Mūka who was dispatched to cause harm to Arjuna [29].

In the Harivamśa Purana, Duryodhana is described as a powerful king with significant military prowess and strategic alliances. He received instructions in club fighting from Balarama and later became Baladeva's disciple after his city was whirled by Baladeva due to his anger [31][33]. Duryodhana stood with his chariots in the Asura army during the war between Krishna and the Asuras, and he led a sacrifice in Hastinapur inviting kings to witness Krishna [34][35].

The Mahābhārata portrays Duryodhana as born under ominous signs, becoming the ruler of the earth, and plotting against the Pāṇḍavas by sending them into exile. He is also described as a formidable warrior, rivaling Arjuna in archery [1-26][37]. However, his actions such as mistaking crystal decorations for water led to embarrassment and contributed to the impending destruction of the Kṣatriya race according to Vyāsa [34-35].

According to Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra, Duryodhana is portrayed as one of the two fools in the world, characterized by his ignorance and foolish actions. He is known for his enmity, arrogance, and significant role in the Mahābhārata war [68]. Additionally, he is described as an ego-ridden ruler who insults Draupadī in the royal court and orders Vidura to leave the country, with his actions tacitly supported by Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s silence [70].

Duryodhana was met by Kṛtavarman and taken leave of; he went from Balabhadra and learnt gadā from Balarāma at Mithilā. He mistook the color of the flooring of the Pāṇḍava's sabhā (built by Maya) for a sheet of water, had a nasty fall, which enraged him when Bhīma and the ladies laughed [1]. Duryodhana kept under influence Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti; banished the Pāṇḍavas and Draupadī to the forest for defeat in the game of dice. He resented Sāmba's action in taking away his daughter Lakṣmaṇā at svayamvara and wanted to punish him [1]. Duryodhana did not feel happy over the success of the Rājasūya, where he was in charge of the treasury; failed to hit the fish mark in Lakṣmaṇā's svayamvara [1].

At Kurukṣetra, he met the Pāṇḍavas in war and arranged his army in vyūha at Syamanta pañcaka. He fought with Bhīmasena who killed him with his gadā; Duryodhana lay in the battlefield with his thigh fractured along with eleven Akṣauhiṇis [1].

Duryodhana allied with Bagadatta and led a group that stole cattle from King Virāṭa, an act later avenged by Arjuna. He was also supported by the Salvas during the Bharata battle against the Pāṇḍavas [24][25].
