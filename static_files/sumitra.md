## Sumitra

### Queen and Mother

Sumitra is primarily known as a queen of Ayodhya and mother to Laxman and Shatrugna, according to the Concise Encyclopedia of Hinduism by Swami Harshananda. She is mentioned in several texts, including the Harivamsha Purana, which identifies her as the mother of both Satrughna and Lakshmana [1]. In this context, she plays a significant role in the Ramayana narrative.

### Historical and Mythological Contexts

In historical geography, Sumitra is also recorded as a king of Mithila who was dedicated to the practice and study of the true Law (Dharma) [2].

Sumitra appears in various other Puranic contexts. In the Brahmanda Purana, she is noted both as one of the sages accompanying Śuṣka on a mission [1] and as a son of Gāndhārī who was beloved among his companions [2]. These references highlight her multifaceted presence across different mythological texts.

### Linguistic Interpretations

The Kannada word "ಸುಮಿತ್ರ" (sumitra) has several definitions, including that of a good friend and the sun [5].

### Dynastic and Contemporary References

According to the Puranic Encyclopaedia by V. Mani, Sumitra was also considered as the son of King Suratha and is noted to be the last king of the Ikṣvāku dynasty during Alexander's conquest in India [7]. Additionally, another reference in the same work mentions Sumitra as a son of Śrī Kṛṣṇa by Jāmbavatī who died in the Yadava war [8].
