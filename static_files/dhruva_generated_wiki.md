## Etymology

Dhruva (ध्रुव) literally means 'the steady one' [1][36]. The term is also used to denote fixed and stationary positions in Sanskrit grammar [5].

### Dhruva as a Child Devotee
Prahlāda and Dhruva have carved out a permanent place in the annals of child-devotees in our purāṇas. Dhruva was the elder son of King Uttānapāda from his first wife Sunīti [1][37]. Enamored by Suruci, his second wife, the king had completely neglected Sunīti and her son Dhruva. Once, snubbed by his step-mother Suruci in the presence of his father, Dhruva became despondent and approached his mother for solace [1][37]. She advised him to pray to Lord Viṣṇu by performing austerities. When he was going to the forest, the sage Nārada met him and instructed him to meditate [1].

### Dhruva’s Penance
After severe penance, Dhruva was blessed by Lord Viṣṇu with a kingdom and a long and happy life both here and in the heavenly regions after death. That is now known as 'Dhruvanakṣatra' [1][3]. His position in the sky is marked by the pole star, Dhruvanakṣatra, and he resides in the tail-region of the Śiśumāra celestial region after death [4].

### Dhruva’s Kingdom
Once Uttama, the younger brother of Dhruva, went to the Himalayas on a hunting expedition but was killed in an encounter with the yakṣas (a kind of demigods) [2]. Enraged by this, Dhruva attacked and killed them in large numbers. Kubera was forced to fight with Dhruva, when Svāyambhuva Manu (the forefather of Dhruva) appeared on the scene and stopped the fight. Then the two became friends. Dhruva ruled his kingdom according to the highest principles of dharma for many years [2].

## Purana References

### Shiva Purana
In the Shiva Purana, Dhruva is a multifaceted figure appearing in various contexts. He is identified as the Pole Star, which the brahmins honor during the wedding ceremonies of Śiva and Pārvatī [7][8]. Positioned a hundred thousand Yojanas above the constellation of the seven sages, he serves as the prop of the circle of luminous bodies [6][9][10]. Additionally, Dhruva is described as a king who worships Śiva, a son of Uttānapāda and Sunīti renowned for his penance lasting three thousand divine years, rewarded with a stable position by Lord Brahmā [10], one of the Vasus whose son Kāla influences the worlds [11], and also the son of Puṣyanāman and the father of Agnivarṇa [12].

### Garuda Purana
According to the Garuda Purana, Dhruva is known for his spiritual achievements and royal lineage. He is described as the son of Uttanapada and Suniti, who attained a high station through his devotion to Janardhana [14]. In Chapter LVI, he is identified as the youngest of King Medatithi's seven sons and later becomes the king of the insular continent of Plaksha [15]. Finally, in Chapter CCXXXV, Dhruva is noted for attaining the highest region of bliss and Vishnu through his devout worship [16].

### Harivamsha Purana
In the Harivamsha Purana, Dhruva appears in various contexts. He is initially described as the son of Uttanapada and Sunrita, known for his ascetic practices and later granted a permanent place in the heavens by Brahma [18]. In another account, he is one of the Vasus who gives birth to Divine Kala [19], and also depicted as a sacrificial vase made of the wood of the Indian fig-leaf crafted by Vishnu [20]. Dhruva is mentioned as one of Surabhi's sons [21] and in a different context, as a celestial position where a sacrifice in the form of a deer finds refuge [22].

## Itihasa References

### Mahabharata
In the Mahabharata, Dhruva appears in various contexts. He is one of the Vasus with a son named Kala, the destroyer of the worlds [24], and also one of Yayati's sons [25]. Additionally, Dhruva appears as a royal sage in Yama’s assembly house [26], a Kalinga prince slain by Bhima in single combat [27], and a warrior fighting for the Pandavas [28]. Dhruva is identified as a form of Krishna symbolizing stability and unchanging essence [29] and as the pass through which the river Ganga flows [30]. The inability to see the star Dhruva is seen as an omen indicating one has only one year left to live [31]. Dhruva is also listed among the seven regenerate Rishis, including Visvamitra [32], and recognized as one of the great Rishis who visited Bhishma on his deathbed [33]. Lastly, he is invoked in a Mantra for constancy and blessings [34].

## Dhruva's Incarnation and Lineage

According to Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra Part-1, Dhruva is an incarnation of the Lord known as Dhruva-vara-pradātā, renowned for his intense devotion and receiving a divine boon from the Lord [36]. He is also a prince who, after being dishonored by his stepmother, performs rigorous penance and gains the favor of Lord Vāsudeva [37]. His name symbolizes permanence, making him a symbol of eternal glory and spiritual achievement, known for his unwavering devotion and ascetic practices [37][38].
