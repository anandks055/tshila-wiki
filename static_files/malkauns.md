Malkauns is an ancient rāga (melodic mode) in Indian classical music that holds significant cultural and historical importance. Its origins can be traced back to the Vedic period, where it has been mentioned in various texts as having deep spiritual connotations. Malkauns is not just a musical scale but also embodies the rich tapestry of India’s musical heritage, encompassing its diverse traditions from North Indian Hindustani classical music to South Indian Carnatic music. It is known for its melancholic and introspective mood, often associated with devotion and spirituality [ref1]. The rāga Malkauns has evolved over centuries, influenced by various dynasties and historical events that shaped the cultural landscape of India. This article delves into the history, significance, musical structure, and contemporary relevance of Malkauns in Indian classical music.

Over time, as Indian civilization evolved, so did its musical traditions, with Malkauns becoming a key element in both Hindustani and Carnatic classical music.

The influence of various dynasties on the development of Indian classical music cannot be understated. Dynasties such as the Kalachuri, who were mentioned for their historical and political references during the Age of Imperial Kanauj [ref3], played a significant role in patronizing the arts. ## Musical Structure

Malkauns is classified as an Asavari thaat rāga, which means it uses the notes Sa, Re Komal (flat), Ga Komal, Ma Komal, Pa, Dha Komal, and Ni Komal. This unique combination of natural and flat notes gives Malkauns its distinctive melancholic character [ref1]. The rāga is generally performed in the late evening or early morning hours, as these times are believed to enhance its introspective and spiritual qualities.

The structure of Malkauns includes several important phrases that define its melodic identity. One such phrase is "Sa Re Ga Ma Pa," which is often repeated to establish the mood of the rāga [ref1]. Another key phrase is "Pa Dha Ni Sa," which introduces a sense of resolution and completion within the melody.

Malkauns has specific rules for ascending (Arohana) and descending (Avarohana) patterns, which are crucial for maintaining its characteristic sound. The Arohana typically follows the pattern: Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni Sa, while the Avarohana is: Sa Ni Dha Pa Ma Ga Re Sa [ref1]. These patterns ensure that the rāga maintains its distinctive flavor and emotional depth.
