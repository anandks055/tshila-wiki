## Yadavas

The Yadavas are a clan of divine warriors descended from Vaivasvata Manu and live in the city of Dvaravati under the protection of the Bhoja and Vrishni races [1]. They are known for their respect for elders, valor in battle, and proficiency in Srutis [2][3][4].

According to the *Harivamsha Purana*, Yadava warriors such as Krishna and Balarama demonstrate exceptional combat skills, often engaging in prolonged battles against formidable opponents like Kamsa and Jarasandha [1]. The term **Yadava** specifically denotes a lineage associated with notable characters such as Shrikrishna and Vasudeva. In *Jnaneshwari* (*Bhavartha Dipika*)'s discussion, it emphasizes Krishna's roles as both an instructor and guiding force within the Yadava community [3]. Additionally, according to *Jnaneshwari*'s elaboration in Chapter 9 - *Raja-vidya and Raja-guhya Yoga*, the Yadavas represent a broader community characterized by strong family ties and societal connections [4].

The Yādavas are also referred to as Sātvatas, a term synonymous with their devotion to Kṛṣṇa. This clan is characterized by their martial prowess, leadership, and loyalty, often led by Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, who protect them from external threats and internal strife [5]. They have significant involvement in various conflicts and battles, including strong alliances with other clans like the Pāṇḍavas.

The *Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra* further elaborates on several key events involving the Yādavas. According to Chapter 9 of Skandha-10, they are chieftains and subordinates of Kaṁsa, invited to Mathura under false pretenses for a yajña where Kaṁsa plans to eliminate them [7]. In Chapter 12 of Skandha-10, the Yādavas are described as being targeted by Jarāsandha and Kālayavana but protected by Śrīkṛṣṇa who built Dvārakā to safeguard them [8].

Moreover, in Chapters 17 and 20 of Skandha-10, the Yādavas are involved in events such as Sāṁba’s wedding, battles with the Kauravas, and the destruction of Vāraṇāsi by the Sudarśana Cakra. They were also frightened by Śālva’s aerial vehicle, Saubha, during an attack on Dvārakā [9][10].
