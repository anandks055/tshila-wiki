## Overview

Nala is a name that appears multiple times across ancient Indian literature and geography. In mythology, Nala refers to both a king renowned for his virtues and a skilled monkey chieftain. Additionally, the name also denotes various geographical locations in contemporary India.

### Mythological Contexts

#### King of Niṣadha
In the Mahābhārata and other epics, Nala is depicted as the virtuous and learned king of the Niṣadha country. He married Damayantī, a princess from Vidarbha (Mahabharath by Kisari Mohan Ganguli). His story involves significant trials including losing his kingdom in a game of dice to his brother Puṣkara under the influence of Kalipuruṣa, an evil spirit (Concise Encyclopedia of Hinduism, Swami Harshananda).

After banishment and living incognito as Bāhuka, Nala regained his original form and sovereignty through various events and challenges. He eventually returned to his kingdom and pardoned Puṣkara (Mahabharata Chapters 49-77). The story of Nala and Damayantī is also celebrated in romantic literature such as the epic poem 'Naiṣadhacarita' by Śriharṣa, highlighting themes of love and perseverance (Concise Encyclopedia of Hinduism, Swami Harshananda).

#### Monkey Chieftain
In the Rāmāyaṇa, Nala is described as a monkey chieftain in Sugrīva's army who aided Rāma in his quest to defeat Rāvaṇa. He possessed a unique boon from his father Viśvakarma that allowed objects he threw into water to float (Rāmāyaṇa 6.22). This ability proved invaluable during the construction of the bridge across the sea leading to Laṅkā.

### Medical and Ritual Contexts
Nala is also recognized in medical literature, particularly in the Sushruta Samhita where it serves as an ingredient in various medicinal applications for treating head diseases and urinary complaints (Sushruta Samhita). In religious texts like the Garuda Purana, Nala appears in contexts related to Hayagriva worship and auspicious years (Garuda Purana).

### Geographical References
Geographically, "Nala" is the name of several villages across various Indian states including Karnataka, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, and Rajasthan. Notable examples include Nala in Dakshin Kannada near Mangalore International Airport and Narimogaru railway station, and another in Jamtara, known for its Bengali-speaking community (Location Summary).

### Legacy
In texts like the Yoga Vasistha, Nala is remembered as a notable king who exemplified noble qualities and achieved liberation while actively governing his kingdom (Yoga Vasishta).
