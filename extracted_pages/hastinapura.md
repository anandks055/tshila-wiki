## Hastinapura

### Historical Capital of the Kauravas

Hastinapura, renowned as the capital city of the Kauravas, was the site for the epic war described in the Mahābhārata. According to some sources, the city was founded by Hastin, son of the first Bharata, hence its name; however, other texts like the Mahābhārata and the Vishnu Purana refer to it as the "elephant city," derived from "kastin," which means elephant in Sanskrit [1]. The ruins of this ancient city can be traced near an old bed of the Ganges river, approximately 57 miles northeast of Delhi. Local traditions have preserved its name, and historical accounts suggest that the city was eventually washed away by the Ganges [1].

### Modern Location

There is also a small village or hamlet named Hastinapura located in the Kanakapura tehsil of Ramanagara district in Karnataka, India. This contemporary settlement comes under the Ramapura Panchayat and lies 35 kilometers south of Ramanagara and 64 kilometers from Bangalore [3]. The village is surrounded by Thally Taluk to the east, Channapatna Taluk to the west, Ramanagara Taluk to the north, and Malavalli Taluk to the west. Nearby cities include Ramanagaram, Malavalli, Maddur, and Bangalore. The local population of 1166 individuals resides in 232 houses across an area of 534 hectares. The primary language spoken is Kannada [3].

### Etymology

The name "Hastinapura" has been interpreted as both "City of Hasti" (referring to its founder) and as "Elephant City," reflecting the significance of elephants in ancient Indian culture [1][2].
