## Bhagavata: A Sacred Hindu Text and Historical Figure

### Overview
**Bhagavata**, also known as Śrimad Bhāgavatam, is a revered sacred text in Hinduism that narrates the life and teachings of Lord Kṛṣṇa. It serves as a primary scriptural authority for the Suddhādvaita school alongside the Bhagavadgitā, Veda, and Brahmasutras [1]. The scripture is also significant within Vaiṣṇava traditions and includes references to various aspects of Hindu mythology, such as the story of Ajāmiḷa, which highlights principles of devotion [2].

### Historical Context
In addition to its spiritual significance, Bhagavata also holds historical connotations. It discusses Indian history and mythology, including the origin of the name Pancala and the descent of the Ganges [1][2]. The text mentions Manu performing austerities at Akolha's hermitage and identifies Kartaviryarjuna and Mahiṣmati as significant figures [3][4].

### Composition and Recitation
The Bhāgavata Purana is composed by Sage Vedavyāsa and recited by Śukabrahmarṣi to King Parīkṣit. It serves as a treatise on the science of bhakti (devotion) to the Lord [1]. The text narrates stories of Lord Viṣṇu's incarnations, such as Varāhasvāmi and Kapilamaharṣi, and provides spiritual and philosophical insights into various avatāras [3][4].

### Mythological Narratives
The Bhagavata Purana includes significant mythological narratives such as the tale of Ajāmiḷa, which illustrates principles of devotion to Viṣṇu [5], and the story of Prahlāda, detailing his unwavering faith and teachings on nine modes of devotion [6]. It also recounts the saga of Purañjana, conveying profound truths through stories [7].

### Historical Figures
**Bhagavata** can refer to historical figures such as Bhagabhadra, a Sunga prince who ruled Vidiśā and received the column from Heliodoros [8]. Additionally, it is an alternative name for the Brahadeesvara temple, known for its impressive architecture and large Shiva lingam in Tanjore [9].

### Scriptural Authority
The text is also noted for purifying sinners and bringing salvation during the Kali age. It is considered one of the eighteen major Purāṇas and contains eighteen thousand verses that glorify the sportive activities of Lord Hari [10][13].
