## Yadavas

The Yadavas are a significant clan in ancient Indian history, renowned for their martial prowess and divine lineage. They are descendants of Vaivasvata Manu and are associated with notable figures such as Krishna and Balarama [1][3]. The Yadava community is characterized by strong alliances with other clans like the Pāṇḍavas and significant involvement in various conflicts and battles, often engaging formidable opponents such as Kamsa and Jarasandha [2].

According to the *Harivamsha Purana*, the Yadavas are known for their respect for elders, valor in battle, and proficiency in Srutis [1][3]. They reside in the city of Dvaravati and are protected by the Bhoja and Vrishni races. The term **Yadava** also refers to the lineage to which Shrikrishna belongs, emphasizing his roles as both an instructor and a guiding force for his community, characterized by strong family ties and societal connections [1][2].

The Yadavas are often referred to as Sātvatas, a term synonymous with their devotion to Krishna [3]. They are led by Krishna and Balarama, who protect them from external threats and internal strife. In the *Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra*, the Yadavas are described as chieftains and subordinates of Kamsa, invited under false pretenses to a yajña where Kamsa planned their elimination [1]. Additionally, they were targeted by Jarasandha and Kalayavana but protected by Krishna who built Dvaraka to safeguard them [2].

The Yadavas are involved in various significant events such as Sāmba’s wedding, battles with the Kauravas, and the destruction of Vāraṇāsi by the Sudarśana Cakra. They were also frightened by Śālva's aerial vehicle, Saubha, during an attack on Dvārakā [3][4].
