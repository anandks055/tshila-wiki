## Overview of Lanka

Lanka is a significant location in Hindu mythology and geography, often depicted as an island kingdom with various mythological associations. It is described both as the capital city of Rāvaṇa and as Ceylon (modern-day Sri Lanka). The origins and evolution of this kingdom are detailed in several ancient texts.

### Mythical Origins

According to religious mythology, Lanka was initially built by Māli with the help of the divine architect Viśvakarma. It became a prosperous city under Māli until he was killed by Viṣṇu due to his terrorizing nature. The city then passed into the possession of Kubera before being seized by Rāvaṇa, who ruled it alongside his brothers Kumbhakarṇa and Vibhiṣaṇa (1).

### Historical and Mythological Events

Lanka is well-known for its role in the Ramayana epic. It was here that Sītā was held captive by Rāvaṇa until she was rescued by Rāma, who led an invasion across a bridge constructed with Nala's help (2). The city was eventually destroyed as part of this conflict, marking significant events such as the vanquishing of Laṅkiṇī, the demon-protectress, and the return of Rāma to Ayodhya using the Puṣpakavimāna from Lanka (3).

### Geographical Descriptions

Geographically, Lanka is often described as an upadvīpa within Jambūdvīpa. It is also depicted in texts like the Garuda Purana as a three-peaked mountain on the southeast corner of Ceylon, where Rāvaṇa resided (4). The city was known for its wealth and splendor but was also notorious for its citizens' ruthless nature and indulgence in sense pleasures and even cannibalism.

### Other References

Lanka has several other mythological associations. For instance, it is mentioned as one of the places where Rudrākṣa plants grow (5). Additionally, Rāvaṇa attempted to secure a Śivaliṅga but was stopped by Gaṇapati, leading to the creation of the Gokarṇa Śivaliṅga. The city also features in various other Puranic texts and historical records.

### Modern Locations

In modern contexts, Lanka is recognized as several villages across India, including Bijapur in Chhattisgarh, which offers educational facilities; Hojai tehsil in Nagaon district near Tezpur Airport; Darbhanga, Bihar, known for its temples and proximity to rivers like Kamala and Burhi Gandak; and Ghazipur in Uttar Pradesh, notable for Hindu temples and proximity to the Ganges river (6).
