## Bali: Mythological Figure and Geographical Locations

### Mythological Figure

In Hindu mythology, **Bali** is a significant figure known for his unwavering devotion and truthfulness despite his demonic lineage. He is the son of Virocana and grandson of Hiranyakashipu [42]. According to various sources, including the Garuda Purana and Shiva Purana, Bali was an Asura king who performed grand sacrifices and was renowned for his heroic and pious nature [31]. His devotion is evident in his cooperation with the gods during the churning of the ocean [1] and his generous offering of lands to Vishnu’s dwarf incarnation Vāmana despite warnings from Shukra [46].

Bali’s relationship with Lord Vishnu culminated in his exile to Patala, where he was bound by Vamana [2]. Despite being exiled, Bali is revered for his devotion and truthfulness. He is also mentioned as the father of Bāṇa, an important figure in Hindu mythology [5][6][7][8].

### Geographical Locations

**Bali** also refers to various geographical locations across India, each with distinct administrative details:

- **Rajasthan**: The most prominent location is in Rajasthan, where it includes several tehsils such as Bali, Bhim, and others within the Pali district. These areas are known for their proximity to rivers like Jawai and Koliwara [9]. The region features numerous temples, including Mahadev Temple and Shri Hanuman Balaji Mandir [10].
  
- **Jharkhand, Bihar, and Odisha**: Other states also have locations named Bali, each with its unique cultural and geographical features. For example, these areas are known for their own temples, rivers, and educational institutions.

### Historical Context

Historically, Bali is referenced in ancient Indian lore as an attacker of Ravana [1] whose sons founded various regions including Aṅga, Vaṅga, Kaliṅga, etc. [2]. Additionally, Bali is depicted in epic battles in stone images at Paharpur [3], and the name also refers to a district containing a village mentioned in historical records [4].

### Temples

Bali town contains two temples, one of which is a Jaina temple with an inscription from the 12th century A.D. It is located about five miles south-east of Falna railway station [2].
