## Kamsa: The Tyrant King and Antagonist in Hindu Mythology

Kamsa, also known as Kaṃsa or Kañsa, is a significant figure in Hindu mythology, primarily portrayed as a tyrannical ruler of Mathurā. He was the son of Ugrasena but was actually born from the demon Kālanemi or Drumila using his magical powers to assume Ugrasena's form [1]. Kamsa was known for his cruel and oppressive rule over the kingdom, which he usurped by dethroning his father and imprisoning him. He is depicted as an embodiment of evil, a villainous rājā who attempted to eliminate any threat to his power.

### Early Life and Rise to Power
From childhood, Kamsa was known for his tyrannical behavior. As he grew older, he overthrew his father Ugrasena and imprisoned him [1]. When Devakī, his younger sister, married Vasudeva, a voice from the void prophesied that Devakī's eighth child would be responsible for Kamsa’s death. This prophecy led Kamsa to attempt to kill all of Devakī's children, sparing only her life after Vasudeva promised to hand over each child [1].

### Attempts to Eliminate Krishna
Kamsa tried repeatedly to eliminate the threat posed by his nephew, Kṛṣṇa, who was predicted to be the cause of his demise. He attempted to kill all six of Devakī's children and sent powerful demons like Muṣṭika and Cāṇura to attack Kṛṣṇa [3]. Despite these efforts, Kṛṣṇa survived due to divine intervention. The seventh child was miraculously transferred to the womb of Rohiṇī in Gokula, and the eighth child, Kṛṣṇa, was hidden away from Mathurā.

### Defeat by Krishna
Ultimately, Kamsa's attempts to eliminate Kṛṣṇa were futile. Kṛṣṇa returned to Mathurā as an adult and confronted Kamsa during a public game where Kamsa had arranged for wrestlers like Muṣṭika and Cāṇura to kill him [1]. Despite the odds, Kṛṣṇa defeated these opponents and subsequently killed Kamsa, fulfilling the prophecy. This act restored Ugrasena to power and freed Devakī from imprisonment [3].

### Other Roles and Contexts
Kamsa is also mentioned in various other contexts within Hindu mythology:
- He is referred to as a powerful demon king who was slain by Kṛṣṇa, an avatar of Viṣṇu [2].
- In some texts, he is described as the ruler of Kaliṅga where Dantāvakra was the prince [3].
- Additionally, Kamsa is noted for his attempts to kill Yogamāyā and Bhadrakāli but failed after they transformed into other divine forms [4].

Kamsa's reign and eventual defeat by Kṛṣṇa are central themes in numerous Hindu texts such as the Mahabharata, Vishnu Purana, Harivamsha Purana, Garuda Purana, and Brahmanda Purana. His portrayal emphasizes his role as a villainous ruler and an embodiment of evil who was ultimately vanquished by Kṛṣṇa, symbolizing the triumph of good over evil.
