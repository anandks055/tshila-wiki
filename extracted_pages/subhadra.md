## Subhadra

### Etymology and Terminology
The term "Subhadra" has various meanings across different contexts:
- It signifies something auspicious or promising success [7].
- As a feminine noun, it can denote a woman's name or good fortune [7].
- In Kannada, the word is associated with being firm, steady, safe from danger, and auspicious [3][4].

### Mythological References
In the Skanda Purana, Subhadra appears in two distinct roles:
1. **Sage Offering Rites**: According to Book 1 - Māheśvara-khaṇḍa, Section 2 - Kaumārikā-khaṇḍa, Chapter 3, Subhadra is a sage who offers to perform Tarpaṇa rites for Devaśarmā's ancestors. This addresses Devaśarmā's predicament [1].
2. **Shelter Provider**: In Book 6 - Nāgara-khaṇḍa, Section 1 - Tīrtha-māhātmya, Chapter 200, Subhadra is identified as the individual who sheltered Candraprabha and was subsequently directed by Bhartṛyajña to undertake a hundred Cāndrāyaṇas and relinquish all his possessions to Brāhmaṇas [2].

### Geographical Location
Subhadra also refers to a village located in the Balijipeta tehsil of Vizianagaram district, Andhra Pradesh, India. It is situated approximately 66 kilometers north of Vizianagaram and 6 kilometers from Balijipeta. Notable landmarks include temples like Dhevathamma Putta Subhadra and Ramalayam Barli [9].

### Cultural Significance
- In Jainism, Subhadra can also denote one of the nine regions above the sixteen heavens.
- Additionally, it is mentioned in musical contexts as the name for one of the 108 kinds of time-cycles [4].
