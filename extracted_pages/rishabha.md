## Rishabha: An Overview

Rishabha is a multifaceted figure appearing in various Hindu texts and traditions, notable for his roles as a king, sage, and musical note. He was the son of King Nābhi and Queen Merudevī, and he had a hundred sons from Queen Jayantī [1][3]. After handing over his kingdom to his eldest son Bhārata, Rishabha performed austerities in the hermitage (āśrama) of sage Pulaha on the mountain peak called Rṣabhakuṭa. Due to his desire for solitude, he cursed the area, causing it to become silent and lonely [1][3]. Through his tapas (austerity), he gained yogic powers and eventually attained the avadhuta state, a stage where one is detached from material existence [2][4].

### Musical Significance

In the context of music, Rishabha represents one of the seven basic musical notes known as 'Svara', equivalent to the note 'ri' derived from the second note 'dvitīya' in sāmagāna [5][6]. This musical association highlights his multifaceted nature beyond his role as a king and sage.

### Jain Tradition

In Jainism, Rishabha is recognized as the first Tirthankara. He was an inhabitant of Hastinapura who installed Bharata on the throne. His birthplace is considered one of the six sacred sites in Ayodhya [3][7]. Additionally, his sons are noted for attaining perfection at Astapada, and his image is prominently featured in various temples across India [2][8].

### Hindu Mythology

In Hindu texts such as the Shiva Purana, Rishabha appears as a king who worships Śiva, one of the filament-mountains located north of Meru, and one of the great sages during the Svārociṣa Manvantara [1][9]. In the Harivamsha Purana, he is invoked in protective mantras and depicted as a mountain shaped like a bear covered with golden sandalwood trees and flowers [10][11].

### Mahabharata

In the Mahabharata, Rishabha has multiple roles. He is described as a tirtha (sacred place) in Kosala where fasting for three nights grants the merit of the Vajapeya sacrifice. Additionally, he is mentioned as a mountain near the sea, and his name is used to denote a musical note blown by Krishna’s conch [12][13].

### Brahmanda Purana

In the Brahmanda Purana, Rishabha is one of the seven sages in both the Svārociṣa and Uttama Manvantaras. He is also identified as one of the musical notes used in songs [14][15].
