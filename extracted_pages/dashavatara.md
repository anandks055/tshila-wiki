## Dashavatara: The Ten Incarnations of Lord Vishnu

The term **Dashavatara** literally means "ten incarnations" and refers to the ten primary avatars (incarnations) of Lord Vishnu, the Preserver in Hinduism. These incarnations are said to appear whenever dharma declines and adharma prospers, to restore balance in the world regardless of time or place [1]. 

### List of Dashavatara

The list of ten avatars is as follows:

1. **Matsyāvatāra (Fish Incarnation)**
   - Vishnu incarnated as a fish to save Manu and other beings from a great flood, ensuring the survival of humanity [1].

2. **Kurma (Tortoise Incarnation)**
   - Vishnu took the form of a tortoise to support Mount Mandara during the churning of the ocean (samudramathana), aiding both devas and asuras in their quest for amrita (nectar) [1].

3. **Varāhāvatāra (Boar Incarnation)**
   - In this form, Vishnu killed the demon Hiranyaksha and lifted the earth out of floodwaters, symbolizing the extrication of the world from sin [1].

4. **Narasimha (Man-Lion Incarnation)**
   - Vishnu appeared as Narasimha to save Prahlada from his father Hiranyakashipu, who was a demon and non-believer in God. This avatar represents divine omnipresence and valor [1].

5. **Vāmana (Dwarf Incarnation)**
   - Vishnu incarnated as Vāmana, a young Brahmin boy, to outwit the demon king Bali by asking for three steps of land and then covering the earth and heaven with his steps, sending Bali to the netherworld [1].

6. **Paraśurāma (Rama with the Axe Incarnation)**
   - Paraśurāma was a sage who exterminated tyrannical Kṣattriyas led by Kartavirya, freeing people from their oppression [1].

7. **Śrī Rāma**
   - Śrī Rāma is one of Vishnu's most popular avatars and the hero of the epic Ramayana. He is considered a role model for an ideal man and his name is often invoked as a mantra to overcome life’s challenges [1].

8. **Balarāma (Strong One)**
   - Balarāma, the elder brother of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, performed many heroic deeds including slaying demons and redirecting rivers [1].

9. **Śrī Kṛṣṇa**
   - Perhaps the most popular incarnation, Kṛṣṇa is considered a Purṇāvatāra (complete incarnation) and is revered as a supreme statesman, warrior, philosopher, and teacher [1].

10. **Kalki**
    - The tenth avatar, yet to come, will appear at the end of the current age (Kali Yuga). Kalki is believed to ride a white horse with a drawn sword to destroy enemies of dharma and restore it in its full glory [1].

### Variations

The standard list varies among different traditions. Some lists replace Śrī Kṛṣṇa with Buddha or include other avatars like Haiṅsa, Sātvata, Yajña, Dattātreya, and Vedavyāsa while maintaining the total count of ten [1].

### Cultural Significance

These avatars are celebrated in various stotras (hymns) such as Dasavathara Keerthanam and are central to Vaiṣṇava dharma [3][4]. They symbolize divine intervention in human affairs, each addressing unique challenges and restoring cosmic balance.
