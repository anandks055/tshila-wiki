## Bāṇāsura

Bāṇāsura is a prominent figure in Hindu mythology, known as a powerful demon king (Dānava rājā) who was the eldest son of Bali. He is also recognized as the father of Uṣā and was a devout follower of Lord Śiva [3]. His devotion to Śiva led him to receive several boons from the deity [2].

Bāṇāsura played a significant role in various mythological narratives. According to one account, he attempted to disrupt the relationship between his daughter Uṣā and Aniruddha but was ultimately defeated by Lord Kṛṣṇa in a war [4][5]. Another tradition recounts that Bāṇāsura tried to marry Puṇyakeśī and was subsequently killed by Devī [3].

In addition, Bāṇāsura is associated with the destruction of his nagara (city) by an arrow from Lord Śiva. The ruins believed to be remnants of this city are reportedly located at Amarakaṇṭaka [1]. Furthermore, Uṣā's palace, situated in Guptakāśī, underscores Bāṇāsura’s connection to this region [4].

Bāṇāsura is also described as a powerful demon who took everything from the Devas and was sought to be destroyed by them. He engaged in battles with various deities, including Kṛṣṇa, which resulted in significant mythological narratives such as the creation of a Jvara associated with Śiva [1][2]. In another tradition, Bāṇāsura is depicted performing penance and receiving an invincibility boon from Lord Śaṅkara, except against a maiden. His reign caused havoc in Trilokī (the three worlds), leading the devas to seek aid from Lord Viṣṇu, culminating in his defeat by Devī [3].

Bāṇāsura is also believed to have ruled Bānapur and Shivakashi as its capital, where the marriage of Aniruddha with Upa (Uṣā) took place [1][2]. This event underscores his significance in both historical and mythological contexts.
