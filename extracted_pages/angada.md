## Angada: Multifaceted Figure in Hindu and Sikh Traditions

### Definitions and Heritage

Aṅgada is a multifaceted figure appearing in various Hindu and Sikh traditions, representing different roles across these narratives. In the context of Hindu mythology, Aṅgada is known as one of Lakṣmaṇa's sons from Urmilā, later crowned ruler of Kārupatha [1]. He is also recognized as the son of King Bāli of Kiṣkindhyā and an important character in the Ramayana [2]. In Sikh tradition, Aṅgada succeeds Guru Nānak after his death in 1538 AD, becoming the second Sikh Guru. As such, he is credited with improving the Guru-mukhi script and strengthening Sikh institutions like laṅgar (community kitchen) and saṅgat (congregation) [3][4]. Additionally, Aṅgada appears as a character from the Ramayana, with his mantras discussed in the Rāmarahasya Upaniṣad [5].

### Literary References

Aṅgada is mentioned in literary works such as the Padyāvalī by poet अङ्गद [2]. In the Mahabharata, he is portrayed as a significant figure involved in various missions and battles. He plays a crucial role in searching for Sita, guarding Rama's army during their journey, delivering messages to Ravana’s council, and participating in critical battles against adversaries like Indrajit [3][4]. Following the conclusion of these events, he is installed as the prince-regent of Kishkindhya by Rama [7].

In the Ramayana, Aṅgada emerges as a brave and strategic leader. As son to Bali and Tara, he informs his mother about Sugriva's alliance with Rama, demonstrating early awareness of political alliances and their implications [1]. After his father’s death, Aṅgada becomes a central figure in the monkey kingdom, leading various missions and advising Rama during the quest for Sita. He faces challenges but remains steadfast, eventually being honored by Rama and installed as king in Angadiya [4][9][10].

### Puranic References

In the Bhagavata Purana, Aṅgada is described as a young monkey prince who fought alongside Rāma and his allies [1]. He is also mentioned as one of Lakṣmaṇa’s sons [2]. The Agni Purana portrays him as a key warrior, delivering messages to Ravana during the Ramayana narrative [1][2].

In the Skanda Purana, Aṅgada is celebrated for creating a Tīrtha on Rāmasetu that grants divine status to those who take a dip. He is also depicted as a valiant warrior assisting in the installation of Liṅgas and accompanying Rama during various rituals [1][2][3][4]. Additionally, he is one of the ten monkeys who accompanied Rāma in Puṣpaka to Laṅkā [5].

### Geographical Context

Angada is also the name of a small village/hamlet located in Gumma tehsil of Gajapati district in Odisha, India. It is approximately 31 kilometers south of Paralakhemundi and 5 kilometers from Gumma. The area is known for its proximity to temples like the Taratrini Temple and Jagannath Temple, as well as educational institutions such as Jisu Prem English School and Govt. High School [9].
