## Overview

Kishkindha is a significant location in Indian mythology as well as a contemporary locality in Karnataka, India. In ancient texts such as the Mahabharata and Ramayana, Kishkindha is depicted as both a region and a fortified city associated with monkey-chiefs.

### Mythological Significance

In the Mahabharata, Kishkindhya (as it is also known) appears in various contexts. According to Sabha Parva, Digvijaya Parva, Section XXX, Kishkindhya is where Sahadeva battles against the monkey-kings Mainda and Dwivida [1]. In Vana Parva, Draupadi-harana Parva, Sections CCLXXX and CCLXXXIX, it is described as both a city and a kingdom governed by Sugriva [2][3].

In the Ramayana, Kishkindha is explicitly identified as the kingdom of the monkeys, initially ruled by Bali and later by Sugriva. The city is described with golden walls and a rampart of monkeys, bristling with instruments of war and numerous banners. It is adorned with precious stones, jewels, and flowery gardens [1][2][3]. Kishkindha serves as the setting for significant events involving Bali and Dundubhi, as well as Sugriva's fight for the throne against Bali [8].

After Sugriva ascends to power, he is enthroned in a purificatory bath and ceremonial rites. The city also becomes the site where Rama and Lakshmana form an alliance with the monkey forces. Additionally, Kishkindha is described as an unconquerable place even by gods or demons [25].

### Contemporary Location

Kishkindha is a locality in Gangavathi city within the district of Koppal, Karnataka, India. It falls under the Gangavathi tehsil and is part of the Gulbarga division. Nearby localities include Mahboob Nagar, Prashanthi Nagar, and Oddarahatti Camp. The area is known for its temples such as Maruthi Mandhir and Hanuman Temple. Additionally, there are several schools in the vicinity, including Shree Kendhole Ramanna School and Alfarooq Urdu High School.

Kishkindha is located near rivers like Kencha Halla and Tungabhadra. The primary language spoken in this area is Kannada.
