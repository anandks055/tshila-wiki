## Overview

Kunti, also known as Pritha or Pṛthā, is a central figure in the Mahabharata and other ancient Indian texts. She is renowned for her role as the mother of the Pandavas—Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna—and her significant contributions to their lives. Kunti's origins are varied across different sources, with some naming her daughter of Kuntibhoja and others identifying her as Kratha’s descendant from the lineage of Jyāmagha.

## Family and Origins

Kunti is primarily known as Pandu’s wife and mother of the Pandavas. According to various sources, she was initially chosen by Pandu (ref 1), and through divine intervention, bore him three sons—Yudhishthira, Bhima, and Arjuna (refs 2-7). In some texts, Kunti is also mentioned as the daughter of Kratha and an ancestor of Vṛṣṇi (ref 1). She plays a crucial role in restoring her son Arjuna’s children to him with Krishna's assistance (ref 2).

## Life Events

Kunti's life is marked by significant involvement in various events, including the escape from the house of lac and Draupadi's Swayamvara (refs 23-25). She shares a secret mantra with Pandu to summon celestial beings for conception (refs 8-9) and plays an essential role in raising her sons after Pandu’s death (ref 10).

Kunti is deeply concerned about her family's welfare. After Pandu's demise, she takes charge of the household, performs their last rites, and decides to follow Pandu (refs 10-11). Throughout her sons' exile, she remains a pillar of strength, providing them comfort and guidance (ref 26).

## Later Life

During the later stages of her life, Kunti attains perfection along with her sons at Śatruñjaya (ref 1), a significant spiritual site. According to some sources, she perishes in a forest fire alongside Dhritarashtra and Gandhari (ref 3). Kunti is also known for expressing a deep desire to see her son Karna after his death (ref 2).

## Cultural Significance

Kunti's story highlights themes of maternal love, devotion, and resilience. Her role in the Mahabharata extends beyond mere motherhood; she actively participates in significant events and decisions affecting her family’s welfare.
