## Gaṅgā: The Sacred River

### Definition and Origin
Gaṅgā is a revered river that literally means “one who descended to this earth” [1]. It has captivated the minds and hearts of people in India for centuries. The river holds immense significance as both a natural entity and a divine goddess, embodying prosperity and purity.

### Scriptural References
The Gaṅgā is prominently mentioned in numerous scriptures such as:
- **Ṛgveda** [1]
- **Śatapatha Brāhmana** [2]
- **Aitareya Brāhmana** [3]
- **Rāmāyana**
- **Mahābhārata** [4]
- **Padmapurāṇa**
- **Nāradīyapurāṇa**
- **Agnipurāṇa**
- **Matsyapurāṇa**
- **Bhagavadgītā** [5]

These works contain hundreds of verses eulogizing the river's sanctifying power and its significance in religious practices.

### Mythological Depictions
Gaṅgā is depicted as a beautiful lady with white complexion, riding a crocodile and holding a pot and a lotus. If shown with four hands, she may also exhibit protective (abhaya) and boon-giving (varada) mudrās or carry a noose (pāśa) and goad (aṅkuśa).

In the Mahābhārata [6], Gaṅgā was cursed to be born as a human being. She married King Śāntanu, who had also been cursed similarly. Their last son was Bhīṣma.

### Historical Origin
According to mythological accounts, Gaṅgā originated from the left foot of Viṣṇu in his incarnation as Vāmana-Trivikrama [1]. The river initially flowed only in celestial regions until King Bhagīratha, a descendant of Sagara, brought it down by pleasing Śiva through severe penance. Śiva captured the river with his matted locks to prevent its destructive speed and then released it into the world.

### Geographical Parameters
Geographically, Gaṅgā originates near Gangotri in the Tehri Garhwal district of Uttar Pradesh. The main tributaries include:
- Bhāgirathi
- Alakanandā

The river flows through various regions and joins the Brahmaputra near the Bay of Bengal. Major tributaries like Mandākinī, Yamunā, Ghāgrā (Sarayu), Sone, Dāmodar, Gaṇḍak, and Kosī converge with it along its course.

### Religious Rituals and Practices
#### Gaṅgāsnāna: Bathing Procedure
Gañgāsnāna, the ritual bath in the river, is believed to cleanse both body and soul. The steps include:
1. Saṅkalpa (pious resolve)
2. Uttering "Om namo nārāyanāya"
3. Ācamana (ceremonial sipping of water)
4. Āhvāna (inviting the river goddess by uttering her names)
5. Prokṣaṇa (sprinkling water on one's head)
6. Mṛttikālepana (applying clay from the bottom of the river)
7. Snāna (bath)
8. Dressing up and offering prayers

#### Special Ceremonies
- **Phallic Images:** Various phallic images of Śiva are located along Gaṅgā, including Saṅgameśa at its confluence with the sea [16].
- **Pilgrimage Months:** The river is highly sacred during certain months or zodiac positions [1].

### Historical and Cultural Significance
#### Descent from the Sky
Gaṅgā descends from the sky with all the fury of her rushing waters, borne by Śiva on one of his matted locks [6]. She plays a significant role in various rituals and ceremonies, such as being one of the rivers where sages performed great sacrifices alongside Kālindī (Yamunā) [2].

#### Purity and Sanctity
Gaṅgā is often compared to Śiva's name in terms of purity and sanctity [4]. She flows from Śiva’s head during his manifestation to Satī [5] and symbolizes spiritual cleansing as she flows through Kāśī [6].

### Modern Recognition
#### Localities in India
There are several settlements named Gaṅgā, including:
- A village in Bathinda Tehsil, Bathinda District, Punjab (Pincode: 151201)
- A locality within Dabwali city, Sirsa District, Haryana (Pincode: 125103)
- Smaller settlements in Jaisalmer District, Rajasthan, and East Singhbum District, Jharkhand.

### Conclusion
Gaṅgā is not only a vital natural resource but also a revered deity in Hindu mythology. Her mythological origins, geographical importance, and religious significance make her an integral part of Indian culture and spirituality.
