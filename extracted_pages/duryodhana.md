## Duryodhana

Duryodhana, also known as Suyodhana, was the eldest son of Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhāri. He is a prominent figure in the Mahābhārata, serving as the leader of the Kauravas and a central antagonist to the Pāṇḍavas.

### Birth and Early Life

Duryodhana was born under ominous signs, which prompted well-wishers of the Kuru-Pāṇḍava race to advise Dhṛtarāṣṭra to abandon this "evil" boy. However, Dhṛtarāṣṭra's infatuation with his son led him to ignore these warnings, ultimately resulting in disastrous consequences for their lineage.

### Training and Education

Duryodhana underwent training under Kṛpācārya and Droṇācārya along with the Pāṇḍavas. The brilliance of the Pāṇḍavas roused envy and hatred in Duryodhana, leading him to devise numerous schemes to eliminate them, including attempts at poisoning and arson. However, these plans were thwarted by Vidura and other well-wishers.

### Military Training

Duryodhana received instructions in club fighting (gadā-yuddha) from Balarama. He was known for his prowess with the gadā (mace), rivaling Arjuna's archery skills, and was advised against his actions by Bhīṣma and Vidura.

### Political Struggles

Duryodhana became ruler of the earth but continued to plot against the Pāṇḍavas. He invited Yudhiṣṭhira for a game of dice orchestrated by Śakuni, resulting in the Pāṇḍavas' loss of their kingdom and exile. During this period, he also tried to humiliate Draupadī in the royal court.

### Military Alliances

Duryodhana formed strategic alliances with various rulers such as Bagadatta [4] and the Salvas during the Kurukṣetra War. He is described as an influential king with significant military prowess and was allied with Jarasandha's army against Mathura [2].

### Kaurava Leadership

As a leader, Duryodhana orchestrated numerous schemes to undermine the Pāṇḍavas' power and influence. He played a crucial role in arranging and leading battles at Kurukṣetra, where he fought alongside notable warriors like Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and Karṇa [2].

### Personal Traits

Duryodhana is characterized by his greed, jealousy, arrogance, and ignorance. His actions led to significant transgressions against dharma, making him an ātatāyin (enemy) in the Mahābhārata conflict.

### Final Battle and Death

During the Kurukṣetra War, Duryodhana fought valiantly but was ultimately defeated by Bhīma with a blow to his thighs. He lay injured on the battlefield along with eleven Akṣauhiṇis of Kaurava warriors.
