## Sampati: Overview

Sampati is a significant character in Hindu mythology, particularly noted for his role as a vulture king and his contributions to the quest for Sita in both the *Mahabharata* and the *Ramayana*. He is described with multifaceted roles ranging from an advisor to a warrior.

### Family Lineage

In the Puranas, Sampati is identified as the son of Aruṇa and Gṛdhri and the father of Vijaya and Prasaha [1]. According to another lineage in the *Ramayana*, he is one of the sons of Mali and Vasuda, and an elder brother to Jatayu. Both are descendants of Shyeni, who was one of Kashyapa's daughters [2].

### Role in the Ramayana

In the *Ramayana*, Sampati plays a crucial role as a vulture king who provides vital information about Sita’s location to Hanuman. He informs Hanuman that Sita is held captive in Lanka and advises the monkeys on how to proceed with their quest [3]. Throughout his appearances, he recounts past encounters with Jatayu and shares intelligence about Ravana's abode [4][5].

Sampati also acts as a spy gathering information on Lanka’s defenses and participates in battles against the titans. He is noted for battling Prajangha and aiding in the search for medicinal herbs [6][7]. Additionally, he assists in informing Rama about Sita's whereabouts in Lanka [8].

### Role in the Mahabharata

In the *Mahabharata*, Sampati is first introduced as Jatayu’s uterine brother. Later, he is identified as Jatayu’s elder brother and king of birds who assists Hanuman in finding Sita [9]. He also appears as a warrior leading a significant contingent of troops during Drona's Garuda formation [10].

### Summary

Sampati is portrayed as an essential figure across multiple texts, contributing significantly to the narrative through his strategic insights and combat skills. His roles span from providing critical information about Sita’s whereabouts to engaging in battles and advising warriors on their quests.
