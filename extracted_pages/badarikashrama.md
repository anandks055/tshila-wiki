## Badarikāśrama

### Overview
Badarikāśrama is a significant spiritual and historical site located in the Himalayas, near Rudra Himalaya, believed to be situated on Mount Kailāsa and considered identical to Mount Meru. It holds great importance as a revered location where various sages and deities engage in meditation and perform penance [1][2]. The Mahabharata (90. 27-34) refers to it, and the Padma Purana mentions Badarikatirtha [3].

### Spiritual Significance
Badarikāśrama is a site of historical and spiritual significance where twin-sages Nara-Nārāyaṇa performed rigorous penance [4][5]. Additionally, it was the residence of Gauḍapāda [6] and visited by sage Nārada in the first chapter of Nārāyaṇiya [7].

### Divine Activities
According to various Hindu texts, Badarikāśrama is associated with numerous divine activities. In the Shiva Purana, it was the location where Śaṅkhacūḍa received instructions and blessings from Brahmā for his marriage to Tulasī [8]. It also serves as a hermitage where Nara and Nārāyaṇa engage in penance and worship Śiva [9]. Furthermore, Vyāsa visited this site for penance and worship, and Subrahmaṇya went there to serve the lotus-like feet of his preceptor [10][11].

### Mythological Events
The Bhagavata Purana mentions Badarikāśrama as a significant hermitage where Nārada performs austerities alongside sages like Sanaka and Sanandana, and Uddhava goes to meditate upon Hari [12][13]. The site is also known for the residence of Lord Kṛṣṇa and where he instructed Uddhava to go and meditate while watching the river Alakanandā [14].

### Historical Accounts
In the Skanda Purana, Badarikāśrama is described as a sacred place visited by Gautama during his pilgrimage. It is also mentioned that Nara resides there and is visited by Narada during his meditations [15][16]. Soma performed penance at this site to attain lordship over planets, stars, and constellations, making it superior for performing holy baths during Kārttika compared to Avantikā [17].

### Literary References
The Mahabharata mentions Badarikatirtha, and various other texts such as the Padma Purana, Yoginitantra, and Bana’s Kadambari describe it as a significant spiritual site. According to these sources, a great pi (worship) is held here, but no worship is conducted for six months every year when it is covered with snow [18].

### Conclusion
Badarikāśrama remains an important site in Hindu mythology and spirituality, where sages engage in penance, deities perform significant activities, and various divine events take place.
