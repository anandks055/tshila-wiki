## Shalya: Overview and Roles in Hindu Literature

Shalya is a multifaceted figure in Hindu literature, appearing prominently as both a king and a warrior. He is the son of Ṛtāyana, the ruler of Madradeśa, and brother to Mādrī, who was one of Pāṇḍu's wives and mother to Nakula and Sahadeva [1][3]. Shalya had two sons: Rukmāṅgada and Rukmāratha. Despite his initial intentions to support the Pāṇḍavas in the Kurukṣetra war, he was eventually persuaded by Duryodhana to join the Kaurava side [1][3].

### Roles and Battles

Shalya played a significant role during the Kurukṣetra war. Initially, he served as Karṇa's charioteer but later took on the position of commander-in-chief of the Kaurava army after Karṇa’s death [1]. He was eventually killed in battle by Yudhiṣṭhira [1][2].

Shalya also had a prominent role in various battles and conflicts. For instance, he was involved in challenging Bhīṣma over the three princesses and participating in Draupadi's swayamvara [5]. Additionally, Shalya led massive armies to support both the Pāṇḍavas and later the Kauravas during critical phases of the war [5][8].

### Literary Contexts

In Hindu literature, Shalya is mentioned in various texts. According to the Harivamsha Purana, he was part of Jarasandha's alliance and served as an envoy for several missions [4]. In the Vishnu Purana, he is identified as a son of Viprachitti and Sinhi, noted for his attempt to prevent Kṛṣṇa from abducting Rukminī [7].

### Ayurvedic Significance

In Ayurvedic medicine, "Śalya" denotes major surgery, one of the eight branches of Ayurvedic practice. It also refers to foreign objects within the body that can cause harm and require surgical removal using specialized instruments [2][3].
