## Trijata: Demoness and Brāhmaṇa

### Demoness in the Ramayana

Trijata is primarily known as a demoness or rākṣasi who served under Ravana during his captivity of Sita. She was appointed by Ravana to lead other demonesses tasked with guarding Sita in Ashokavana (grove) in Lanka. When she observed her fellow demonesses harassing Sita, Trijata advised them to desist based on a prophetic dream she had, which foretold Rama's victory over Ravana in battle [1].

Trijata is noted for her wisdom and compassion towards Sita. In the Sundara-kanda of the Ramayana, she interprets dreams that predict Sita’s deliverance by Hanuman and comforts Sita with assurances of Rama’s eventual triumph [2]. She also shares a dream wherein she sees Rama rescuing Sita and defeating Ravana, causing her companions to fear for their fate [3].

In the Yuddha-kanda section of the Ramayana, Trijata informs Sita that Rama and Lakshmana have been killed by Indrajit but later comforts her, assuring that they are still alive based on troop behavior and the arrival of the Pushpaka chariot [4][5].

### Brāhmaṇa in the Epic Tradition

Additionally, there is another character named Trijata who appears as an old brāhmaṇa. This Trijata approached Sri Rama before his banishment to seek wealth and received it from him [6].

### References in Other Texts

In the Mahabharata, Trijata, a Rakshasa woman, is mentioned as comforting Sita with her dreams foretelling Rama's victory [7]. She is also honored by Rama himself after the battle [8].

The Concise Encyclopedia of Hinduism and various other sources provide consistent accounts of Trijata’s role in protecting and later comforting Sita while she was captive under Ravana. Her prophetic dreams played a crucial role in maintaining Sita's morale and assuring her of eventual deliverance.
