## Introduction

The Godāvari, also known as Gotamī nadi or Gautamī, is one of India's seven sacred rivers, highly venerated in Hindu rituals and worship. It originates from the Brahmagiri hill near Tryambakeśvar in Maharashtra and flows eastward to join the Bay of Bengal (ref 1). The river is considered extremely holy, especially during the Puṣkara-period, which occurs once every twelve years and lasts for twelve days (ref 1).

## Origins and Course

The Godāvari originates from the Brahmagiri hill near Tryambakeśvar in the Nasik district of Maharashtra. It flows eastward to join the Bay of Bengal near Rājamahendram or Rajamundry, where it is about 6.4 kilometers wide (ref 1). At this point, it divides into two branches: Gautamī Godāvari and Vasiṣṭha Godāvarī, with the latter further branching off into Vainateya Godāvarī (ref 1).

The total length of the river is approximately 1465 kilometers. It is joined by several important tributaries such as Purṇā, Phena-gaṅgā, Vardhā, Vainagaṅgā, Prāṇahitā, Indrāvatī, and Śabarī (ref 1). The river also serves as a boundary for various regions, including the Andhrapatha alongside the Krishna River and marks the southwestern boundary of the Kalingāraṇya forest (ref 3).

## Mythological Significance

According to Hindu mythology, the sage Gautama brought this river from the matted locks of Śiva near Brahmagiri. Śiva agreed to stay near his hermitage, which became the famous Tryambakeśvara (ref 1). The Rāmāyana describes that Śri Rāma lived in Pañcavati on the bank of Godāvari during his sojourn in the Daṇḍaka forest, and Sitā was abducted by Rāvaṇa from here (ref 1).

The river is also mentioned in several scriptures such as the Mahabharata, Brahmanda Purana, Matsya Purana, Padma Purana, Ramayana, and Suttanipata. It is noted for its spiritual significance and role in various mythological events (refs 6, 8).

## Pilgrimage Sites

Several holy places of pilgrimage are situated on the banks of Godāvari:
- **Nāsik**: A bath in the Rāmakuṇḍa on the left bank of Godāvarī is a must for pilgrims visiting Nāsik (ref 1).
- **Pañcavaṭī**
- **Govardhana**
- **Janasthāna**

Additionally, significant places along its course include Pañcavati and the hermitage of Śarabhañga. Bathing in Godāvarī during eclipses is considered a meritorious act according to Hindu traditions (ref 2).

## Cultural and Educational Importance

The river has cultural and educational importance, with several temples and sites dedicated to various deities along its banks. Notable among these are Rāma-bhadrācala in the Khammam district of Andhra Pradesh and the temple at Bhimedvara. The village of Kikaraparru is also situated near this river (ref 3).

## Modern Geographical Importance

In modern times, Godavari refers to a village in Gujarat with notable locations in Jamnagar and Surendranagar districts. These villages feature several temples such as Vakdeshwar Mahadev, Shiv Temple Jay Gangeshwar, Dhavdima, and Bharvad Temple (ref 9).

## Significance During Puṣkara-Period

A bath in the Godāvarī during the Puṣkara-period is considered extremely holy. This period occurs once every twelve years when the planet Bṛhaspati or Jupiter enters the zodiacal sign Simha or Leo. Śrāddhas and tarpaṇas performed during this time are believed to save ancestors from hell and even grant them liberation (ref 1).
