## Overview

The **Kauravas** are a prominent clan in Hindu mythology, particularly known as descendants of King Kuru and led by Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Duryodhana. They are often portrayed as the primary adversaries to their cousins, the Pandavas, in several religious texts such as the Mahabharata and the Skanda Purana [1][2].

## Historical Context

The Kauravas' narrative is deeply intertwined with that of the Pandavas, where they represent the opposing forces. In one instance, the Devas warn them about the potential dangers associated with obtaining sacred ash, highlighting their role in significant religious activities and decisions [1]. Additionally, the Kauravas are depicted as a rival clan to the Pandavas, serving as the opposition that the Pandavas must overcome [2].

## Military Role

During the epic battle of Kurukṣetra, the Kauravas are positioned as Duryodhana's army, directly opposing the forces led by the Pandavas [3]. This conflict underscores their role in one of the most significant battles in Hindu mythology.

## Religious Participation

Despite being adversaries to the Pandavas, the Kauravas also participate in shared religious activities. For instance, Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Duryodhana travel with the Pandavas to perform religious ceremonies at Hāṭakeśvara [4][5]. They are also involved in the marriage of Duryodhana and Bhānumatī, as well as the installation of Liṅgas alongside the Pandavas and Yadavas [6], demonstrating their active participation in communal religious rituals.
