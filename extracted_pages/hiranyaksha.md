## Hiranyaksha

Hiranyaksha is a significant figure in Hindu mythology, known primarily as an Asura who was killed by Lord Vishnu during his Varaha incarnation. He is described as the younger brother of Hiranyakashipu and one of Diti's sons, alongside other powerful Daityas.

### Birth and Lineage

Hiranyaksha was born from a curse placed on Jaya and Vijaya, two gatekeepers of Vaikuntha [2][3]. According to various sources, he is also the son of Visvamitra and Shalavati [1], though this contradicts other accounts that place him as one of Diti's sons with Hiraṇyakaśipu being his brother [4].

### Mythological Role

In several Puranas, Hiranyaksha is depicted as a powerful demon who submerges the Earth into the ocean (mahāsāgara) and abducts it to Patala or Nether regions [1][5]. This act prompts Lord Vishnu to incarnate as Varaha to rescue the Earth from the floodwaters [3][6].

### Defeat by Vishnu

Hiranyaksha was ultimately defeated by Vishnu in his boar (Varāha) form. The battle took place when Vishnu, in his Varaha incarnation, lifted the Earth out of the ocean and killed Hiranyaksha with his discus [7][8]. According to some accounts, he was also challenged and vanquished during battles against various avatars of Vishnu, including Narayana and Vasudeva (Krishna) [1][9].

### Family and Legacy

Hiranyaksha is noted for having six brave sons who fought valiantly in battles [2]. Additionally, he adopted Andhaka as his son despite Andhaka's disabilities [4], and fathered other Daitya kings such as Kukura, Śakuni, Bhūta Santāpana, Mahānāda, and Kālanābha, who were great followers of Shiva [8][9]. 

### Historical Context

Hiranyaksha's story is found in multiple texts including the Garuda Purana, Shiva Purana, Harivamsha Purana, Yoga Vasistha, Mahabharata, Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra, and Linga Purana. Each text provides various details about his life, lineage, and defeat, contributing to the rich tapestry of Hindu mythology.
