## Kaikeyi: A Queen and Mother in Ancient Indian Literature

Kaikeyi is a significant figure in ancient Indian literature, particularly known for her role as one of King Dasaratha's queens and the mother of Bharata. She appears in various texts such as the Puranas, Mahabharata, Ramayana, Bhagavata Purana, Skanda Purana, and Devi Bhagavata Purana.

### Personal Background
Kaikeyi is described as the youngest queen of Ayodhya and the wife of King Dasaratha [6]. She hails from the kingdom of Kekaya, where her father was named Aśvapati [4].

### Family Connections
She is the mother of Bharata. In the Bhagavata Purana, she is noted as a disowned mother by her son for failing to guide him towards salvation [1]. Additionally, in the Purana Index, she bears the surname of Bhadrā, who was one of Krishna's wives [1].

### Key Events and Actions
Kaikeyi plays a pivotal role in the narrative of Rama’s exile. According to various sources, including the Ramayana and Mahabharata, she is influenced by her servant Manthara to demand two boons from King Dasaratha: making Bharata the regent and sending Rama into exile for fourteen years [1][2][5].

In the Adhyatma Ramayana, Kaikeyi receives a portion of the sacred pudding alongside Kausalya and Sumitra [3]. Her actions are also significant in the Mahabharata, where she is portrayed as expressing disdain towards Draupadi and attempting to dissuade King Dasaratha from favoring her [1][2].

### Cultural Significance
Kaikeyi’s character is multifaceted. In some contexts, such as the Skanda Purana, she accompanies Rama on his pilgrimage to Dharmāraṇya [1]. However, she is primarily known for her manipulative actions that lead to tragic consequences in the Ramayana and Mahabharata narratives.

### Representation in Literature
Her character appears across multiple texts where she is depicted as a significant figure whose decisions have far-reaching effects on the characters around her. Her influence extends beyond the immediate narrative of Rama’s exile, impacting events described in various Puranas and epics [2][3].
