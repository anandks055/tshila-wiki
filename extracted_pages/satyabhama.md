## Satyabhama: A Significant Figure in Hindu Mythology

Satyabhāmā is a prominent figure in Hindu mythology, known as one of the eight queens (aṣṭa-mahiṣīs) of Kṛṣṇa. She was born as the daughter of Satrājit from the Yadu race and had previously lived as Guṇavatī, the devoted daughter of a pious brāhmaṇa named Devaśarman. Her purity, austerities, and devotion to Viṣṇu led to her rebirth as Satyabhāmā and marriage to Kṛṣṇa (SOURCE 2, SOURCE 3).

### Marriage to Kṛṣṇa

Satrājit had initially suspected Kṛṣṇa of murdering his brother but later sought amends by giving his daughter Satyabhāmā in marriage to Kṛṣṇa. This union was preceded by the theft of the Syamantaka jewel, which led to various complications and eventual reconciliation (SOURCE 1). Satyabhāmā was also one of the Saktis worshipped during the Gopala manifestation of Vishnu and is considered a significant figure in Hindu mythology (SOURCE 4).

### Accompanying Kṛṣṇa

Satyabhāmā accompanied Kṛṣṇa on several adventures, including his expedition to Naraka's city and his visit to Indra’s abode. She also played an active role in retrieving the Pārijāta tree from heaven (SOURCE 1). During this quest, she challenged Śacī’s claim over the Pārijāta tree, leading to conflicts between Kṛṣṇa and the gods (SOURCE 7).

### Domestic Roles

Satyabhāmā was known for her leadership among Krishna's wives and her contributions to family life. She performed various religious ceremonies such as observing the Punyaka vow and distributing gifts among Krishna’s wives (SOURCE 5). She is also noted for bearing seven sons and four daughters, including Bhānu and Bhaumarikā (SOURCE 1, SOURCE 8).

### Religious Contributions

Satyabhāmā was involved in numerous religious activities. For instance, she performed the Kalyāṇini vratam and participated in the retrieval of Vana's cows at Krishna’s request (SOURCE 5). She is also depicted as a devoted wife who sought to win her husband's heart through unwavering devotion and specific acts of kindness, as advised by Draupadi (SOURCE 6).

### Personal Characteristics

Satyabhāmā was known for her jealousy and resentment towards other wives, particularly Rukmiṇi, the chief queen of Kṛṣṇa. However, she is also recognized for her valour in defeating Kubera and her determination to teach a lesson to Indrāṇī (SOURCE 1).
