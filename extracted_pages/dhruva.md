## Dhruva

Dhruva, literally meaning "the steady one," is a prominent figure in Hindu mythology and scriptures. He was the elder son of King Uttānapāda from his first wife Sunīti [1][2]. Due to his father's favoritism towards his second wife Suruci, Dhruva faced neglect and humiliation [1].

After being snubbed by Suruci in front of his father, Dhruva sought solace from his mother. She advised him to perform severe penance to Lord Viṣṇu. During this endeavor, the sage Nārada met Dhruva and instructed him to meditate, which he did with great devotion [1]. As a result of his intense austerities, Dhruva was blessed by Lord Viṣṇu with a kingdom and a long life both in this world and after death. His position in the sky is now known as 'Dhruvanakṣatra,' or the Pole Star [1][2].

In addition to his spiritual journey, Dhruva is also renowned for his royal lineage and achievements. He was known for his devotion to Lord Viṣṇu and was rewarded with a stable position by Lord Brahmā after performing penance for three thousand divine years [4]. His ascetic practices were so severe that he earned a permanent place in the heavens [1][6].

Dhruva's legacy extends beyond his spiritual accomplishments. He is also described as a king who worshipped Śiva and was part of various royal lineages, including being one of Surabhi’s sons [4][5]. Furthermore, Dhruva appears in different contexts as one of the Vasus, with a son named Kāla, who influences the worlds [3][6][7].

In the Shiva Purana, Dhruva is identified as the Pole Star and serves as the prop for the circle of luminous bodies. He resides in the tail-region of the Śiśumāra celestial region after death [2][4]. His name symbolizes permanence, making him a symbol of eternal glory and spiritual achievement [1][8].
