## Mandodari

Mandodari is a prominent figure in Hindu mythology, primarily known as the chief queen of Ravana, the king of Lanka. She is renowned for her beauty and grace, often described as slender-waisted and adorned with pearls and gems [1][7]. Her beauty is so striking that even Ravana considers her his favorite among his wives [2].

In the *Rāmāyaṇa*, Mandodari is depicted as a wise and compassionate figure who attempts to pacify Ravana and offer him counsel. She advises her husband, often trying to temper his more aggressive inclinations [3][4]. After Ravana's defeat at the hands of Lord Rama, she mourns his loss deeply, expressing disbelief at his demise and attributing it to divine intervention [5][6].

Mandodari’s portrayal in other texts like the *Devi Bhagavata Purana* offers additional context. According to this text, Mandodarī is the wife of Rāvaṇa whose inadvertent consumption of Brāhmaṇa blood led to the birth of Sītā [1]. In another account, she is described as a princess from Simhala who initially rejects several marriage proposals due to her ascetic inclinations but later becomes disillusioned with worldly pleasures after marrying King Cārudeṣṇa and discovering his infidelity [3][4].

Mandodari embodies dignity and sagacity among the rākṣasas, recognizing the divine nature of Rama and Sita. Her character is emblematic of both beauty and wisdom, often lamenting her husband's fall while maintaining a profound sense of grace and compassion.
