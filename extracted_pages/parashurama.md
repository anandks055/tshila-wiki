## Overview

Paraśurāma, meaning 'Rama of the battle-axe,' is considered the sixth incarnation or avatāra of Viṣṇu among the ten Daśāvatāras.[1][2] He is often regarded as a partial manifestation of Viṣṇu due to his contemporary relationship with Rāma, who drew Paraśurāma's power into himself.[1][2]

## Lineage and Early Life

Paraśurāma was the son of sage Jamadagni and Reṇukā, daughter of King Prasenajit. A notable incident in his early life involved his father commanding him to cut off his mother’s head, which he did; however, by praying to his father, he managed to restore her to life.[1][2]

## Major Events

During Paraśurāma's absence, Kārtavīryārjuna, the king of Māhiṣmati, visited Jamadagni’s hermitage and entertained himself with the help of Kapilā, a divine wish-yielding cow. When King Kārtavīryārjuna attempted to take the cow for himself, Jamadagni refused, leading to his murder by the king.[1][2]

Upon learning about this tragedy upon his return, Paraśurāma performed severe penance to please Lord Śiva and received a divine paraśu or battle-axe from him. With this weapon, he not only avenged his father's death by killing Kārtavīryārjuna but also eliminated many tyrannical Kṣattriya kings who were oppressing their subjects.[1][2]

## Encounters and Battles

Paraśurāma was a great warrior and an expert in the use of various weapons. He taught archery to notable figures such as Droṇācārya and Karna.[1] He also fought with Bhīṣma but could not defeat him.[1][2]

In his encounter with Rāma, Paraśurāma lost all his power and retreated for tapas (meditation) to the Mahendra mountains.[1][2] Despite this setback, he is considered an immortal or cirajīvin and is believed to still be alive.[1][2]

## Spiritual Contributions

Paraśurāma is celebrated in various spiritual texts such as Anugitā and Brāhmaṇagitā for his contributions to the understanding of spiritual wisdom and the path to mokṣa (liberation).[3][4] He also played a role in significant events like the transformation of pools of blood into the Kurukṣetra lake.[5][6]

## Iconography

Paraśurāma is often depicted as a red-complexioned sage with white robes and jatāmukuṭa (crown of hair). In his right hand, he holds the paraśu (battle-axe), and in his left, the vaiṣṇavadhanus (bow of Viṣṇu) or tarjanī-mudrā (pose of warning or threat).[1]

## Legacy

Paraśurāma is known for various significant events such as challenging Rāma at Mithila, bringing the Vaitaranī river to the world, and surrendering his axe at Brahmakunda on the Brahmaputra.[7] He is also associated with ancient temples in locations like Nirmanda.[8]
