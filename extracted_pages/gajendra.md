## Gajendra: An Elephant King in Hindu Mythology

### Etymology and Definition
Gajendra, a term derived from Sanskrit meaning "lordly elephant," is often used to denote the chief or noblest of elephants. In various sources, it refers to an elephant king who has experienced human reincarnation due to a curse [1][2][4]. The title also signifies a great and respected leader among elephants [6][7].

### Mythological Background
Gajendra was originally named Indradyumna in his previous life as a human. He was a devoted follower of Lord Vishnu, known for his piety and adherence to Vedic practices [1][2]. Due to an inadvertent offense towards Sage Agastya, he was cursed to be reborn as an elephant [3].

### Story of Gajendramokṣa
In the divine narrative, Gajendra sought help from Lord Vishnu when trapped by a crocodile. This event is known as "Gajendramokṣa" and symbolizes devotion during distress (arthabhakti) [1][2]. According to the Śrīmadbhāgavata Kathāmrutasāra, Gajendra was caught in the jaws of a crocodile while bathing and invoked Lord Vishnu for rescue. The Lord appeared in an eight-armed form and liberated Gajendra from his captor [2].

### Symbolism
The story of Gajendramokṣa illustrates the struggle against material bondage and liberation through devotion to the divine. It is also depicted in various artworks, showing the elephant king alongside a crocodile and a Gandharva [1][5]. This event symbolizes the jiva’s (soul) journey from material entanglement towards spiritual freedom [2].

### Textual References
The narrative of Gajendra is prominently featured in texts such as the Vāmanapurāṇa and Śrīmadbhāgavata, where it serves as a parable of divine rescue and devotion [1][5]. The episode also finds mention in the Vācaspatyam, which elaborates on Gajendra’s previous life and his transformation into an elephant due to the curse by Sage Agastya [3].

### Cultural Significance
The story of Gajendramokṣa is widely celebrated in Hindu culture. It is believed that listening to this tale can purify one from sins and bring peace, especially for those plagued by nightmares [1][5]. The divine intervention through Vishnu’s form as Varadarāja underscores the power of devotion in overcoming adversities.

### Geographic Locations
In contemporary India, Gajendra also refers to small villages located in different regions. One such village is situated in Pindra Tehsil, Varanasi District, Uttar Pradesh, and another is found in Bellaguntha Tehsil, Ganjam District, Odisha [9]. Both locations are known for their temples and cultural significance.
