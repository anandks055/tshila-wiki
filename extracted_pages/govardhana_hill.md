## Govardhana Hill

Govardhana Hill holds a significant place in Hindu mythology and is revered for its dual role as both a symbolic representation and an actual pilgrimage site. In the iconography of Śrīkṛṣṇacandra, Govardhana Hill is depicted as being held by Lord Krishna, symbolizing his divine power and protection [1]. Additionally, it is a sacred mountain located in Vrindavan, where numerous pastimes of Lord Krishna are believed to have occurred. This makes Govardhana an important site for devotees undertaking pilgrimage.

An image of Gopāljī was installed on Govardhana Hill at the request of Shri Mahaprabhu, who wished to see Him [2]. The shrine containing this image is located within a cave near the village of Pēḍdāmādur. Another notable idol, known as Sakalnārāyaṇa, which represents Śrīkṛṣna, can also be found in this same location [1].
