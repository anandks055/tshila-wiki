## Kausalya

### Definitions and Descriptions

Kausalya is a multifaceted figure in ancient Indian literature and mythology. She is described as the wife of Dasharatha and mother of Rama, known for her deep happiness tied to Rama's achievements and profound sorrow during his exile [2][3][4]. Additionally, she is mentioned in the Mahabharata as the wife of Puru and the mother of Janamejaya, who performs three horse-sacrifices [1].

In some sources, Kausalya is referred to as a "siddha," indicating spiritual perfection or achievement [SOURCE 1]. She is also associated with the people of Kosala, where she is known as the mother of Rama and an epitome of maternal devotion and sorrow [SOURCE 4][SOURCE 5].

### Heritage and Lineage

Kausalya's lineage is richly detailed in various texts. According to the Vishnu Purana, Kausalya was another name for Hiraṇyanābha, a teacher who belonged to the line of disciples of Vyasa [SOURCE 6]. This association places her within an esteemed academic and spiritual tradition.

### Cultural Significance

In cultural contexts, the term "Kausalya" (ಕೌಸಲ್ಯ) in Kannada refers to skill or craftsmanship (ಕೌಶಲ), suggesting a broader meaning that extends beyond her personal identity [SOURCE 3].
