## Introduction
Lakshmana is a revered character in Hindu mythology, best known as the younger brother of Rama and a key figure in the epic *Rāmāyaṇa*. He is also mentioned in other texts such as the *Mahābhārata*, *Shiva Purana*, *Garuda Purana*, and various works by historical authors. Lakshmana is celebrated for his unwavering loyalty, bravery, and spiritual devotion.

## Lakshmana in Rāmāyaṇa
In the *Rāmāyaṇa*, Lakshmana is the son of King Dasharatha and Queen Sumitra [26]. He accompanies Rama into exile, demonstrating steadfast support and service throughout their journey. Trained alongside Rama by Sage Vishvamitra in both war and peace, Lakshmana plays crucial roles such as rescuing Rama and Sita from Indrajit’s Nāgapāśa and killing Indrajit with the Aindrāstra [12][15]. He is also known for his loyalty during the quest to rescue Sita and is married to Urmila, who leads an austere life during their exile [30].

Lakshmana receives spiritual teachings from Rama on knowledge, devotion, detachment, and methods of worship, aiming to overcome transmigration through Advaita Vedanta [5][29]. He is depicted alongside Rama in numerous religious sites and artworks and celebrated in texts such as the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*, *Rāmagitā*, and *Rāmarahasya Upaniṣad* [30].

## Additional Roles
Lakshmana is also known for significant events like cutting off Śurpaṇakhā’s nose and ears, leading to the naming of Nāsikā [6][7][8]. He accompanies Rama and Sita on various journeys, including visits to hermitages such as Valmiki, Bharadvaja, Savari, Gautama, and Sutikshna, as well as the confluence of Sarayu and Ganges rivers [3][9][10][11][12][14].

In spiritual contexts, Lakshmana is mentioned in the *Shiva Purana* as Rama’s loyal companion who accompanies him during his quest for Sita. He is also saved by Hanuman using the medicinal herb Sañjīvanī [3]. In the *Garuda Purana*, he is one of Vishnu's incarnations and a son of King Dasharatha, accompanying Rama into exile and fighting alongside him [2][3].

## Other References
Lakshmana appears in various roles across different texts. In the *Harivamsha Purana*, he is described as one of Sumitra’s sons alongside Satrughna and one of Krishna's wives with supernatural abilities [1][5]. Additionally, there are mentions of Lakshmanas who are poets, authors, and even characters in performances [4].

In the *Yoga Vasistha*, Lakshmana mirrors Rama’s detachment from worldly pleasures, following his habits and sharing his brother's renunciation. He receives spiritual guidance at Sage Vasishtha's hermitage and remains a steadfast companion to Rama throughout his journey [1][2].
