## Overview

Naimiṣāraṇya, a mythologically significant forest located on the right bank of the Gomatī nadi (also known as Gumt1), is situated about 40 kilometers from Sondilla railway station in Uttar Pradesh. This sacred site derives its name from a legend involving sages and Brahmā, where the rim (nemi) of a wheel dropped off there [1][2].

## Historical and Religious Significance

Naimiṣāraṇya is an important Hindu pilgrimage site and one of 51 Pithasthanas. It has been noted for its geographical importance in the Tāṇḍya-Mahābrāhmaṇa [3]. The forest is also recognized as a significant location where ancient Aryan sages performed writings and sacrifices [4][2].

The area was the abode of sixty thousand Rishis, and many Puranas were written here. It is situated on the left bank of the Gumt1 (Gomatī nadi) in the Sitapur district [3]. The Mahābhārata, Pancavimsa Brahmana, and Jaiminiya Brihmana all refer to this holy place as a site where various sages performed religious activities. According to the Padmapurana, Naimiṣāraṇya was also the location of a twelve-year sacrifice [4].

In the Garuda Purana, Naimisharanya is described as a sacred and holy forest known for its religious significance and spiritual activities. It is a place where sages and hermits perform penance [5]. Additionally, Sutji visited this forest to answer questions about Lord Vishnu’s incarnations from numerous sages engaged in penance [6].

The Bhagavata Purana mentions Naimiṣāraṇya as a significant location where sages travel from its banks to see Kṛṣṇa in Dvārakā and where Sūta narrates stories during prolonged sacrificial sessions [7][8]. The Skanda Purana highlights that it is a revered sacred land presided over by Viṣṇu, known for the penance performed by great sages. It is also listed among holy places conducive to the attainment of Siddhis and suitable for the Japa of Mantra [9][10].

In the Devi Bhagavata Purana, Naimisharanya is described as a sacred forest where sages and Ṛṣis gather to seek spiritual knowledge and liberation. The sages assembled here to hear the Purāṇas, and it was selected by Brahmā as a secure location during the Kali age [11][12].

## Modern Location

Naimisharanya is located in the tehsil of Mishrikh within the district of Sitapur in Uttar Pradesh. The postal code for this area is 261402, and it falls under the administrative divisions managed from Lucknow. Nearby transportation facilities include a railway station at Naimisharanya and Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport in Lucknow [13].
