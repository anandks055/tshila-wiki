## Varaha

### Definitions and Heritage

#### Etymology and Linguistic Roots
The term "Varaha" originates from Kannada (ಕನ್ನಡ), where it is spelled as ವರಹ. Phonetically, it is pronounced as "varaha." This word has multiple meanings that span both the natural world and historical contexts.

#### Zoological Interpretations
1. **Wild Boar**: In a zoological context, "Varaha" refers to the hog *Sus scrofa*, which is the wild ancestor of domestic pigs. These animals are characterized by their robust build and tusks, commonly found in various habitats across Eurasia. [1]
   
2. **Domestic Pig**: The term also denotes the domesticated version of this animal, commonly known as a pig. This usage reflects its widespread role in human agriculture and cuisine. [2]

#### Historical and Economic Contexts
3. **Monetary Coin**: Historically, "Varaha" was used to describe a gold coin that circulated during various periods with differing values and weights. These coins were significant in the economic transactions of their times. [3]
   
4. **Weight Unit**: Additionally, "Varaha" served as a unit of weight corresponding to the weight of one such gold coin. This usage highlights its importance not only as currency but also as a standard measure in trade and commerce. [4]
