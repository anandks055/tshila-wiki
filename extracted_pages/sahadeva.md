## Sahadeva: A Multifaceted Figure in Hindu Mythology and Literature

### Biography and Lineage
Sahadeva was the youngest of the five Pāṇḍavas and twin brother to Nakula. Born to Mādrī, one of King Pāṇḍu's wives, through the grace of the Aśvinīdevatās, Sahadeva is noted for his exceptional beauty and prowess in battle.[1][2] He was trained by Kuntī, the queen mother, and became proficient in scriptures and governance.[4]

### Military Campaigns
Before the Rājasūya sacrifice, Sahadeva led a campaign to conquer southern kings, collecting ransoms for the ceremony. He subdued rulers such as Vibhīṣaṇa and the Pandyas[3], and also conquered the king of Māhiṣmatī.[6] He is particularly noted for subduing Vinda and Anuvinda of Avantikā to make them pay contributions for the sacrifice.[2]

### Skills and Abilities
Sahadeva was highly skilled in taming horses and elephants, and he excelled at managing cows. His most remarkable ability was his trikālajñāna, or the power to know past, present, and future events.[5] During the Kurukṣetra battle, Sahadeva notably killed Śakuni.[9]

### Roles in Disguise
While living incognito at King Virāta's palace during the Pāṇḍavas' exile, he assumed different names: Tantripāla when overseeing the king’s dairy and Ariṣṭanemi in other contexts.[10][8]

### Agrapūjā
During the Rājasūya sacrifice, Sahadeva performed agrapūjā (worship of the greatest person present) to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, as advised by Bhīṣma.[4]

### Family and Lineage
Sahadeva was married to Draupadī, Vijayā (daughter of Śalya), and Bhānumati (daughter of King Bhānu).[5] He had two sons: Śrutasena from Draupadī and Suhotra from Vijayā.[5]

### Later Life
During the mahāprasthāna (the final journey towards heaven) after the Pāṇḍavas' reign, Sahadeva died prematurely due to his pride in intellectual powers. He passed away shortly after Draupadī.[8]

### Historical and Mythological Contexts
Sahadeva is also mentioned as a versatile plant with medicinal applications, used for treating various ailments according to the Sushruta Samhita.[1][2][3] Additionally, he appears in other texts such as the Garuda Purana where he is noted as the father of Krishashva and an ingredient in creating Tilaka marks.[1][2]

In the Harivamsha Purana, Sahadeva is identified as a Kashi king's son, continuing the lineage of kings. He also appears as one of Vasudeva’s wives and a participant in significant battles alongside the Yadavas against Asuras.[5][6][7]

### Cultural Significance
The Mahābhārata mentions several other Sahadevas: a great sage attached to Indra's court, a king in ancient India, and the son of Jarāsandha who briefly succeeded him after his death at Bhīma’s hands.[8] There is also a ratha temple named after him among the five rathas in Mahābalipuram.[5]
