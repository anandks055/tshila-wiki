## Introduction

Drona, also known as Droṇa, was a multifaceted figure in Hindu mythology, appearing as a teacher, warrior-preceptor, and advisor. His roles and attributes are described across various ancient texts including the Mahabharata, Shiva Purana, Garuda Purana, Harivamsha Purana, and historical sources.

## Roles and Attributes

### Teacher and Warrior-Preceptor
Drona served as a warrior-preceptor who trained both the Pāṇḍavas and Kauravas in the art of warfare. He imparted significant knowledge to Arjuna, including the powerful Brahmāstra [5][4]. As commander during the Kurukṣetra war, he orchestrated Abhimanyu's demise by devising the Chakravyuha formation, leading to his death [1][2]. Drona was later killed through deceit during this same conflict [5].

### Sacred Rituals
In the context of sacred rituals, Droṇa is associated with a specific shape of bricks used in the Agnicayana ritual, representing a trough [3]. Additionally, he distributed Buddha's relics after his death and is associated with the Drona-stūpa [1][2][3].

### Mythological Roles
In the Shiva Purana, Droṇa appears in various roles. He is described as a mountain from which Aṅgiras retrieves divine herbs to revive the gods during battles [1]. In another context, he is one of the flowers offered by Bhārgava in his worship of Śiva [2]. Furthermore, he is portrayed as a brahminical sage performing penance to obtain a son, Aśvatthāman, who is Śiva's incarnation [3].

### Historical Context
Historically, Drona was a Brahmin whose home was located in Vethadvīpa, near Allakappa [1]. He received the city of Ahicchatra from Arjuna along with Kampilya [2].

## Literary References

In the Mahabharata, Drona is noted for his rivalry with King Drupada and his mastery over the Brahma weapon. He was also a respected advisor to King Dhritarashtra and participated in significant events such as the Rajasuya sacrifice [1][7][8][9].

### Other Puranas
In the Garuda Purana, Drona is listed among the seven mountains and is described as the preceptor of the Kuru race and later as the commander of the Kuru forces during the Mahabharata war [1][2]. In the Harivamsha Purana, he is mentioned as a person residing on the mountain Raivata for many years [1].

## Conclusion
Drona's multifaceted nature encompasses roles ranging from warrior-preceptor to sage and advisor. His significance in Hindu mythology and ritual practices highlights his enduring importance across various texts.
