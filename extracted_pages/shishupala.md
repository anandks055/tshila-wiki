## Overview of Śiśupāla

Śiśupāla is a significant figure in Hindu mythology, known for his complex relationship with Lord Kṛṣṇa and his ultimate demise. He was born to Damaghoṣa and Śrutaśravas, inheriting traits from previous incarnations as Hiraṇyakaśipu and Rāvaṇa. His birth is marked by unusual physical features—he had four hands and three eyes—which were only resolved in Kṛṣṇa’s lap, foretelling his eventual death at the hands of Kṛṣṇa [1][2].

## Life and Character

Śiśupāla ascended to the throne of Cedi and was notorious for his tyrannical rule and womanizing behavior. His enmity towards Kṛṣṇa manifested in various battles, notably during the Rājasuya sacrifice where he openly vilified Kṛṣṇa [2][4]. Despite these abuses, Kṛṣṇa forgave him a hundred times before finally cutting off his head with the Sudarśana discus [1][3].

## Previous Incarnations

Śiśupāla was reborn as one of the manifestations of Jaya and Vijaya due to a curse. In previous lives, he had been Hiraṇyakaśipu and Rāvaṇa, who were both killed by avatars of Viṣṇu (Narasimha and Rāma) but did not attain salvation [1][3]. However, when Śiśupāla recognized Kṛṣṇa as an avatar of Viṣṇu, he welcomed death at his hands and attained mokṣa [1].

## Historical Context

In the context of major events in Hindu epics, Śiśupāla played crucial roles. He led the Chedi army against Krishna and was bound by nooses given by Hara through Rukmiṇī's son during battles. His actions and enmity towards Kṛṣṇa are depicted in various Puranas, such as the Garuda Purana and Harivamsha [4][5].

## Legacy

Śiśupāla’s legacy extends beyond his death, with his son Dhrishtaketu becoming a formidable warrior and ally of the Pandavas. Additionally, after Śiśupāla's death, Salva led an attack on Dvārakā [3][6].

## Summary

Śiśupāla was a complex figure marked by his enmity towards Kṛṣṇa and his ultimate recognition of Kṛṣṇa’s divinity. Despite his villainous portrayal in many texts, he is recognized for his significant role in the events leading to the Rājasuya sacrifice and his eventual liberation through devotion.
