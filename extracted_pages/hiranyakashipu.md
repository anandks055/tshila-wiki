## Overview of Hiranyakashipu

Hiranyakashipu, a powerful demon king and son of Kashyapa and Diti, is renowned in Hindu mythology for his intense hatred towards Lord Vishnu. Known by the epithet "one who has covered himself with gold" or “the greedy one,” he was born as an asura due to a curse from sages Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanatkumara, and Sanatsujata [1]. He performed severe austerities to please Brahma, gaining several boons that made him nearly invincible. Despite these powers, his tyrannical reign over the gods eventually led to his downfall at the hands of Vishnu in his Narasimha (man-lion) incarnation [2][3].

## Mythological Background and Birth

Hiranyakashipu was born as a consequence of a curse on Jaya and Vijaya, the gatekeepers of Vaikuntha. The sages Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanatkumara, and Sanatsujata cursed them to be reborn three times as rākṣasas (demons). Hiranyakashipu was born in one such incarnation alongside his twin brother Hiraṇyākṣa during the Kṛtayuga. They were later killed by Lord Vishnu in various avatars [1][4].

## Reign and Actions

Hiranyakashipu ruled with tyranny, conquering the gods and harassing everyone due to his anger over the killing of his brother Hiraṇyākṣa by Lord Vishnu [5]. He had four sons, including the famous devotee Prahlada. Despite his hatred towards Vishnu, Hiranyakashipu was unable to harm Prahlada due to Vishnu’s protection, leading him to attempt numerous ways to eliminate his son without success [1][2].

## Confrontation with Narasimha

Hiranyakashipu sought invincibility from Brahma through severe austerities and received several boons. However, these boons were not absolute, and he was ultimately killed by Vishnu in the form of Narasimha (man-lion), which signified the triumph of good over evil [2][3]. His death led to significant chaos among the Asuras due to his absence as a leader [6].

## Family and Lineage

Hiranyakashipu was one of Diti’s sons, and he had four sons: Anuhraadha, Hrada, Samhrada, and Prahlada. His sister Holika attempted to harm Prahlada but failed in her endeavors [1][5]. He is also noted as the great-grandfather of Bali, a strong hero who was later defeated by the gods [7].

## Cultural Significance

Hiranyakashipu’s story appears prominently in various texts such as the Vishnupurana and Bhāgavata [8], illustrating his role as an incorrigible tyrant whose defeat symbolized the triumph of divine will over demonic pride. He is also remembered for his confrontations with other deities like Shiva, who assumed the form of Sharabha to confront Narasimha after Hiranyakashipu’s death [1].

## Conclusion

Hiranyakashipu remains a significant figure in Hindu mythology, representing an embodiment of evil and tyranny. His story serves as a moral lesson on the ultimate triumph of good over evil through divine intervention.
